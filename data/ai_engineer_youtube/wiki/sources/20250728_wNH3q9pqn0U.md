## Summary

Stefania Druga demonstrates a realtime AI co-scientist that combines sensor readings, cameras, voice, text, experiment protocols, Gemini API calls, and open-source lab hardware so scientific agents can interpret empirical feedback during experiments rather than only producing offline plans.

## Notes

- The live demo connected a micro:bit/JackDac sensor board, heat pad, microscope, and camera to an AI assistant that could read temperature and other environmental conditions in realtime. (00:59-01:30)
- Experiment protocols provide task context: when the user defines experiment type, conditions, and constraints, image or sensor feedback can be interpreted in that experimental frame. (01:35-02:14)
- An object-tracking camera can run a model on-device and could be trained to track crystal growth or other target phenomena while experiment conditions such as temperature are adjusted. (02:21-03:13)
- The talk contrasts offline AI co-scientist planning over papers and data with realtime hypothesis formation from empirical lab data, sensors, images, audio, and observed robot failures. (04:49-08:17)
- The prototype uses a React app with JackDac/WebUSB sensors, webcams, text, voice, frontend hooks, context assembly, a backend, and Gemini API calls. (08:37-10:14)
- The crystal-growth experiment recorded microscope video and CSV sensor values, then plotted growth rates across samples and conditions; one observed lesson was that crystal formation happened in bursts after critical saturation, not as a smooth gradual process. (12:16-14:17)
- Open-source lab ecosystems such as Jubilee motion platform and open bioreactors are presented as a route from educational prototypes to practical lab automation for pipetting, droplet manipulation, and liquid handling. (16:09-17:07)
- Future co-scientist loops can use real camera, sensor, and voice traces to create simulations of processes such as crystal or bacterial colony growth, identify better conditions, and reproduce those conditions in the real lab. (17:16-17:59)
