## Summary

NLW reports early findings from a self-reported AI ROI survey and connects them to broader enterprise adoption patterns: production agent deployments are growing, but many organizations remain in pilot stages, and leaders appear to get more value when they treat AI adoption as a cross-organizational portfolio rather than isolated spot experiments. The useful engineering takeaway is that AI impact should be measured through multiple outcome categories, with explicit caveats for selection bias and with attention to where automation and agentic use cases outperform simple time-saving workflows.

## Notes

- The talk cites a KPMG quarterly pulse survey claiming full production agent deployment among surveyed billion-dollar-plus enterprises rose from 11% in Q1 to 42% in Q3, while still noting broader enterprise difficulty moving from pilots to scaled deployment. (02:47-04:30)
- NLW frames leading AI adopters as doing more than spot experiments: they pursue multiple use cases at once, think systematically about AI and agents, and include revenue growth, new capabilities, and new product lines alongside productivity. (05:27-06:07)
- The ROI survey grouped reported benefits into time savings, increased output, quality improvement, new capabilities, improved decision-making, cost savings, increased revenue, and risk reduction. (09:05-09:30)
- NLW explicitly caveats the survey as a self-selected, AI-interested audience before reporting that 44.3% saw modest ROI, 37.6% saw high ROI, and roughly 5% saw negative ROI. (09:34-10:11)
- Time savings was the most common category at about 35% of use cases, but the talk argues that five to ten saved hours per week can still mean seven to ten work weeks reclaimed per year. (10:39-11:36)
- Risk reduction was only 3.4% of primary ROI categories, but those use cases were described as the most likely to report transformational impact, with compliance and back-office volume cited as examples. (14:16-15:25)
- Use cases mentioning automation or agents were described as wildly outperforming the average in self-reported ROI, pointing toward a more advanced layer beyond first-tier productivity use cases. (15:56-16:40)
- The source argues that more submitted use cases correlated with better reported ROI, supporting a portfolio view of AI and agentic transformation across organizational disciplines. (16:40-17:21)
