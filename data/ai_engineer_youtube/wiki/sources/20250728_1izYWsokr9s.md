## Summary

Preeti Somal frames production agents as complex distributed systems whose core work is long-running workflow orchestration across LLMs, tools, chat history, users, parallel branches, and human approvals. The talk positions Temporal as a code-first durable workflow layer: developers keep business logic in ordinary SDK-backed workflows while Temporal handles retries, failure recovery, scale state, workflow history, and observability for agent execution.

## Notes

- Agents are described as complex distributed systems that must cope with unreliable LLMs, scale, durability, reliability, orchestration, state, human approvals, parallelism, tool calls, tracing, and visibility. (01:14-03:15)
- Temporal's role is framed as outsourcing reliability and scalability plumbing so developers focus on business logic through language-idiomatic SDKs. (03:17-04:19)
- In the agent architecture example, Temporal workflows orchestrate the user, system, LLMs, goals, tools, chat history database, interaction loops, signals, and queries. (06:41-10:20)
- The workflow abstraction removes bespoke plumbing such as hand-coded retry branches; Temporal handles those failure and retry pieces. (10:23-10:40)
- Temporal stores workflow history so teams can inspect how an agent navigated complex interactions and export that history for compliance or debugging in test/dev environments. (10:42-12:05)
- Tools can be wrapped as Temporal activities, LLM providers can remain interchangeable, interactions are managed with signals and queries, and failures are handled transparently by the workflow layer. (10:57-11:41)
