## Summary

Tom Shapland explains why voice agents interrupt users: most production stacks still use serial STT, VAD, LLM, and TTS pipelines where VAD is largely speech/not-speech plus a silence timeout. The talk argues for semantic, syntactic, prosodic, acoustic, and eventually full-duplex approaches to turn-taking, while noting that cascaded systems may remain important for production control.

## Notes

- Turn-taking is described as a hard, context-dependent control problem: humans vary by culture, individual, and moment, and interruptions in commercial voice-agent settings can cause immediate user abandonment. 00:41-02:24
- A common production pipeline is speech input, STT, VAD, LLM, streamed chat completion, TTS, and audio output; VAD often means detecting speech/not speech and applying a silence timeout such as roughly half a second. 02:26-04:08
- Human turn-taking relies on prediction: listeners infer the speaker's intended message, refine likely end-of-turn from syntax, and finalize with prosody or acoustic cues while already preparing a response. 04:20-07:55
- LiveKit's semantic end-of-utterance model uses recent conversation turns to predict whether the user's current turn is complete and can extend the VAD silence window when the semantics suggest the user is still mid-thought. 08:50-10:42
- Audio-plus-text completion models from vendors such as Daily, AssemblyAI, and Kyutai are presented as major improvements over traditional VAD, though STT-integrated completion models may only see the user side of the conversation. 12:59-14:39
- Speech-to-speech realtime APIs may still rely on VAD internally, and the talk says turn-taking remains unsolved across approaches. 14:41-16:00
- Full-duplex models such as Moshi and Sync LLM are framed as promising because they process input and generate output concurrently, but production systems may still prefer cascaded control for brand pronunciation, instruction following, and integrations. 16:06-19:02
- Backchannel detection remains difficult: LiveKit currently uses a simple duration threshold to decide whether user speech during agent speech is an actual interruption, while learned classification is identified as future work. 24:54-26:44
