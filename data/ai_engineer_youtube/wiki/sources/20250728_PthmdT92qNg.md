## Summary

Ben Stein argues that AI-agent product management has to shift from specifying exhaustive deterministic features toward defining agent affordances, discovering emergent behavior through prototypes and customers, and treating evals as the living specification for probabilistic product behavior.

## Notes

- A customer asked whether an AI teammate could be tagged in a Google Doc comment; the team had not designed that flow, but the agent could receive Google notification email and might still act successfully, exposing product behavior that was not captured in an upfront spec. (01:57-02:46)
- The talk identifies two sources of product uncertainty: teams do not fully know what foundation models can do, and free-form user surfaces invite customers to try unbounded workflows. (03:44-04:30)
- Instead of enumerating every Google Docs, Linear, Figma, or LinkedIn comment case, the product should define affordances such as communication, commenting, email, and collaboration, then rely on the agent workflow to compose them within guardrails. (04:36-06:42)
- Emergent behavior changes the product role from only writing requirements to discovering useful building blocks and how customers or agents compose them. (06:42-07:25)
- Evals become the product specification for probabilistic behavior: a subjective behavior such as being funny but not mean can be judged repeatedly, assigned a threshold, and used to decide whether a release is acceptable. (08:32-10:12, 15:12-15:48)
- Vibe-coded or runnable prototypes let product teams feel conversational agent behavior, such as whether clarifying questions are helpful or annoying, before they harden a spec. (10:46-12:57)
- Customer conversations should acknowledge that the vendor and customer are discovering future behavior together; expectations that require deterministic certainty may not fit early probabilistic agent products. (16:02-18:13)
