## Summary

Gergely Orosz frames AI adoption in software organizations as a sociotechnical change rather than a simple code-generation productivity story: token-count incentives can create Goodharted behavior, engineers need hands-on workflow practice rather than only theory, the software-engineer role is expanding toward product, testing, operations, and agent orchestration, and large companies are investing in internal agent infrastructure because their codebases and workflows need custom context, gateways, review systems, and service integration.

## Notes

- Token maxing arose where token output or AI spend was visible through leaderboards or lookup tools, and engineers worried those numbers could influence performance conversations. (01:17-02:32)
- The transcript gives failure examples where engineers asked agents to summarize documentation poorly or ran autonomous agents to produce low-value work just to raise token counts. (03:00-03:27)
- Orosz compares token-count measurement to earlier lines-of-code and pull-request-count metrics: once measured, engineers optimize the metric even when the behavior is not useful. (04:18-04:41)
- Individual AI use can be productive, but team productivity is less obvious because existing workflows are hard to retrofit around AI. (09:08-09:26)
- Effective use depends on repeated practice and workflow change; understanding model architecture can help generally, but it does not by itself teach engineers how to use AI tools well. (11:13-12:25)
- AI accelerates an existing trend where software engineers are expected to own testing, DevOps, product thinking, planning, and business understanding while teams become smaller. (12:48-14:33)
- Agent orchestration is closer to tech-lead work than people management: the operator coordinates work and feedback without people-management responsibilities, and agent feedback loops are much faster than human project cycles. (14:54-17:25)
- Large companies are rebuilding internal AI infrastructure around monorepo-aware background coding agents, MCP gateways, service discovery, on-call tooling, and risk-aware code review. (17:43-18:46)
- Internal AI platform work can be a lower-risk way to learn AI than shipping unwanted AI product features, and enterprise codebases can require custom context and integration that off-the-shelf vendors cannot provide. (18:56-19:25)
