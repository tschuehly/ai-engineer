## Summary

A behind-the-scenes promo from swix (AI Engineer co-founder) about the 2026 World's Fair, but it doubles as a field-structure snapshot: how the AI engineering conference's track lineup is curated and what that says about where the discipline is heading. The two durable signals are (1) the rise of organizational token economics ("token billionaires," the token-maxing vs token-reduction spectrum, AI factories) as a first-class leadership concern, and (2) a deliberate shift from horizontal tracks toward high-value AI verticals, with code-adjacent domains (finance, healthcare, deployed engineering, agentic commerce, GTM) treated as the next areas closest to takeoff. Most of the video is event logistics and is intentionally excluded.

## Notes

- ~50% of the conference topics are kept as "evergreen" and ~50% are replaced each year, which swix uses as a curation heuristic for what stays durable in AI engineering versus what is transient (02:30).
- New 2026 top tracks reflect field expansion beyond app building: auto research (new), the former GPU track split into inference / post-training / pre-training (data quality), plus memory and continual learning, and verticals (03:00).
- "Token billionaires": spending ~1B tokens/month is described as a low bar; some organizations spend ~10 trillion tokens/month (09:50-10:30).
- The token-spend spectrum is split roughly evenly between people trying to spend *more* in smart ways ("token maxing") and people trying to spend *less* ("reductions"); Alex Volkov (Weights & Biases) calls this the "ZL spectrum" after Mario Zechner (10:30-11:00).
- Leadership themes people ask for: real workflows from people not selling anything, token maxing/reduction extremes, and setting up "AI factories" for companies (11:00).
- 2026 bets on verticals over 2025's horizontals (design engineering, product management): the chosen verticals are deployed engineering (FDE), agentic commerce, healthcare, finance, and GTM (12:05-13:00).
- Finance takes precedence as the lead vertical; swix argues that after code, finance is "the most likely and closest for takeoff," followed by healthcare and law (12:40-13:20).
- Industry/research convergence: researchers move fluidly between research and engineering roles (citing Greg Brockman), and AIE positions itself as the industry counterpart to academic conferences (ICML, NeurIPS) with an ACM partnership on AI and Agentic Systems (06:00-07:00).
- The "war on slop" framing continues as a stated community value (17:00).
