## Summary

This Paper Club session treats DeepSeek R1/V3 as a practical example of reasoning-model post-training: more RL on the same base family increased benchmarked reasoning, longer generated reasoning traces, and JSON/function-calling behavior, while distilling those improved traces into Qwen 3 8B showed that small models can inherit meaningful reasoning behavior from a stronger teacher.

## Extracted Concepts

- [Scale Reasoning Models With RL and Verifiable Domains](../concepts/scale-reasoning-models-with-rl-and-verifiable-domains.md) - DeepSeek is presented as a shift from uniform next-token compute toward RL-trained test-time reasoning on checkable math and code tasks.
- [Distill Reasoning Traces Into Small Models](../concepts/distill-reasoning-traces-into-small-models.md) - the session highlights DeepSeek's follow-up distillation into Qwen 3 8B as evidence that reasoning traces can transfer capability to smaller models.

## Notes

- DeepSeek's May 28 update is described as a post-training improvement rather than a wholly new base model, with AIME 2024 rising from about 70% to 87.5% and average reasoning tokens increasing from about 12k to 25k. (09:50-12:36)
- The talk frames test-time scaling as dynamic compute: instead of spending the same compute on every generated token, a reasoning model learns to spend more compute on harder queries. (22:28-22:48)
- The speaker says DeepSeek trained reasoning with RL on verifiable code and math data, using checks such as compilation, factual correctness, and mathematical correctness. (22:48-23:07)
- The Qwen 3 8B distillation is described as using a stronger reasoning model's traces to improve an 8B model, with the speaker emphasizing that reasoning-model distillation can be a better small-model training recipe than training a small model directly. (12:39-15:05)
