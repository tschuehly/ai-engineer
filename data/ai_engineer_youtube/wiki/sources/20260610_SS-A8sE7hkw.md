## Summary

Two Google DeepMind speakers frame open-weight Gemma 4 as a sovereignty and ownership story rather than a pure capability story. Owning the weights lets a team run on its own hardware, customize, avoid loss-of-service risk, and keep proprietary data inside its infrastructure; the shift of Gemma's license from a custom license to Apache 2.0 removes the ~18-month procurement/legal barrier that previously blocked sovereign institutions (Ukraine, Bulgaria, Brazil cited). The talk reinforces Gemma 4's intelligence-per-size claim (31B dense ranks ~4th/7th among open models on LM Arena while running on a single GPU versus competitors needing ~200 GB / 4-5 GPUs) and the effective-parameter trick (E2B/E4B keep extra token-mapping parameters off the GPU). Ian Ballantyne adds an ownership decision framework: own/offload a workload when the model is capable, fits the hardware, meets the latency budget, and beats the cost — where on-device cost is energy rather than tokens, and rising agentic token generation is the pressure tipping the math toward ownership. Demos: agent-skill execution on a phone (Google AI gallery app), and a parallel multi-agent translation run on a 26B model in LM Studio on an M4 Mac.

## Notes

- Why own a model: run on your own hardware, customize, avoid loss of service, and send proprietary data that cannot leave your infrastructure; even the best proprietary model cannot help directly under those constraints. Gemini (hosted, API) and Gemma (open, owned) are positioned as complementary. (01:11-02:15, 02:55-03:24)
- Four Gemma 4 sizes: E2B and E4B (mobile/edge/IoT), where "E" = effective — an E2B uses about as much memory as a 2B model (~2B on the GPU) but has ~5B parameters; the extra parameters are token-mapping rather than transformer parameters, so they can live in other memory and the GPU footprint stays 2B/4B. Both take text/vision/audio input, output text only, and do thinking, coding, and function calling on a phone. (02:24-03:35)
- Larger sizes: a 26B mixture-of-experts (~4B-class experts; 26B total parameters but ~4B working space) for accessibility, and a 31B dense as the strongest model — the speaker's daily driver for coding, agentic, and multilingual work. (03:35-05:00)
- Intelligence per size: on LM Arena ELO the two models rank ~4th and 7th among open models; the top 20-30 open models are at least 2-3x and in some cases 20x larger. The 31B runs on one GPU while comparable-quality competitors need ~200 GB of memory (≈4-5 GPUs), a large price difference. You don't need the most intelligent model to summarize mail, do minimal tasks, code, or run search/doc agentic tasks. (04:18-06:00)
- AI.dev playground lets you try Gemini, VO, and the 26B/31B Gemma free, including vision + thinking + code execution at once. (06:00-06:30)
- License: a custom Gemma license forced sovereign institutions through ~18 months of procurement/legal review (which rarely works); Gemma 4 moved to Apache 2.0 going forward, making legal sign-off tractable. Sovereign deployments: Ukraine (parts of services), Bulgaria (national LLM on Gemma 2 → Gemma 4), Brazil (Portuguese on Gemma 3). (07:07-09:00)
- Language fine-tuning caveat: the base is already strong (top 2-3 in many languages even at 31B), so fine-tuning for one specific language can yield as little as ~1% — verify before investing. (09:00-09:35)
- Ownership economics (Ian): agentic work raises token generation (OpenRouter "State of AI" report puts programming among the highest combined input+output token tasks), so owning models controls cost or amortizes sunken hardware. Decide via thresholds: capable + fits hardware + meets latency + acceptable cost. Latency: user-facing (~seconds) vs batch. Cost: owned sunken infra vs leasing GPU time. (09:41-12:37)
- On-device, you pay in energy (GPU/NPU utilization) not tokens, so scheduling matters — realtime response vs an offline background job run when the phone is plugged in at night. (14:00-14:50)
- Phone demo: agent skills running on the phone (Google AI gallery app, iOS/Android, ~2B/4B model) can take device actions (calendar, maps) or run user-defined skills; Gemma 4 reasons about which action to take and reliably makes the function call, an improvement over the previous generation. (12:55-13:55)
- Enterprise scaling: drop from a 300B-class multi-GPU model to a single H100/A100 or even an L4, lowering cost; MedGemma (medical variant) is deployable to 1-2 GPUs to serve a whole hospital on private, controllable data. (14:53-16:05)
- LM Studio demo: the 26B model (4B activated parameters, ~26 GB RAM including context) runs on an M4 Mac (unified memory ~48 GB); an orchestrator farms a translation out to sub-agent windows, then compiles results back and generates a web page — a parallel multi-agent workload on a laptop. (16:05-18:25)
- Getting started: take any OpenAI-compatible interface, point it at Ollama or LM Studio, and pick the Gemma model — that's the only code change to try it. Then drop it into existing workflows, bolster your eval suites (benchmarks show general capability, but what matters is your task), and weigh serving tradeoffs (own GPU = uptime control plus maintenance/ongoing/capex cost; mobile = accelerator/RAM limits but offline + on-device private data; enterprise = infrastructure plus ongoing cost). (18:24-20:10)
