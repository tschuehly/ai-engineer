## Summary

Barr Yaron presents early results from Amplify's 2025 State of AI Engineering survey, framing AI engineering as a broad, still-forming practice where teams use LLMs across many internal and external use cases, update models and prompts frequently, rely heavily on RAG, vector databases, human review, and offline evals, and still see a production gap for agents and multimodal systems.

## Extracted Concepts

- [AI Engineering Practice Is Heterogeneous and Fast Moving](../concepts/ai-engineering-practice-is-heterogeneous-and-fast-moving.md) - survey respondents use LLMs across multiple use cases while updating models and prompts frequently.
- [Prompt Management Lags Prompt Iteration](../concepts/prompt-management-lags-prompt-iteration.md) - frequent prompt changes coexist with a large unmanaged prompt population.
- [Multimodal Models Have a Production Adoption Gap](../concepts/multimodal-models-have-a-production-adoption-gap.md) - image, video, and audio production usage trails text, with audio showing the strongest stated adoption intent.
- [Agent Adoption Trails LLM Adoption in the Workplace](../concepts/agent-adoption-trails-llm-adoption-in-the-workplace.md) - respondents report ordinary LLMs working well much more often than agents, even though most plan to use agents.
- [AI System Evaluation Still Depends on Human Review](../concepts/ai-system-evaluation-still-depends-on-human-review.md) - production monitoring combines observability, offline evals, user data, benchmarks, and human review, with evaluation named the top pain point.
- [Dedicated Vector Databases Remain Common RAG Infrastructure](../concepts/dedicated-vector-databases-remain-common-rag-infrastructure.md) - most respondents store context in dedicated vector databases, split between self-hosted and third-party options.

## Notes

- The survey had 500 respondents, with the largest group identifying as engineers; the talk emphasizes that many people doing AI engineering do not yet have "AI engineer" as their title. (01:30-02:36)
- Many experienced software engineers are still AI newcomers: among software engineers with 10+ years of software experience, nearly half had worked with AI for three years or less, and one in ten started in the previous year. (02:52-03:14)
- More than half of respondents use LLMs for both internal and external use cases, and 94% of LLM users use them for at least two use cases while 82% use them for at least three. (03:21-04:05)
- Besides few-shot learning, RAG was the most popular customization technique, with 70% of respondents using it; fine-tuning was more common than the speaker expected, especially among researchers and research engineers. (04:08-04:44)
- Among fine-tuners, 40% mentioned LoRA or QLoRA; other methods included DPO, reinforcement fine-tuning, supervised fine-tuning, and hybrid approaches. (04:46-05:14)
- More than 50% of respondents update models at least monthly, 17% update weekly, 70% update prompts at least monthly, and one in ten updates prompts daily. (05:20-05:49)
- Despite frequent prompt changes, 31% of respondents reported having no prompt-management process. (06:07-06:20)
- Image, video, and audio usage lag text usage by significant margins; among non-users, 37% of those not using audio planned to adopt audio. (06:25-07:46)
- For survey clarity, an AI agent was defined as a system where an LLM controls core decision-making or workflow; 80% of respondents said LLMs were working well at work, but fewer than 20% said the same about agents. (07:48-08:16)
- The majority of production agents reported in the survey had write access, typically with a human in the loop, and some could act independently. (08:34-08:52)
- Monitoring and evaluation were multi-method: 60% used standard observability, over 50% used offline evals, and quality evaluation combined user data, benchmarks, and human review. (08:52-09:33)
- 65% of respondents used a dedicated vector database for context storage; among those, 35% primarily self-hosted and 30% primarily used a third-party provider. (09:35-10:02)
- Evaluation was named the number one most painful thing about AI engineering today. (11:16-11:31)
