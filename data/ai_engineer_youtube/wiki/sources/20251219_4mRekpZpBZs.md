## Summary

Arman Hezarkhani argues that AI changes engineering incentives because hourly, project, salary, and equity models often do not directly reward faster high-quality output. Tenex's operating model splits client work into roadmapping and execution, converts architecture design documents into story-pointed tickets, pays engineers a base plus quarterly story-point compensation after accepted delivery, and uses strategist/client approval plus QA as counterweights against inflated scope, quality drops, and adversarial behavior.

## Notes

- Tenex charges clients for story points delivered and pays engineers based on story points they complete, which the talk positions as a way to align customer, company, and engineer incentives around output rather than time. (01:12-01:39)
- The speaker frames uneven AI use among engineers as an incentive issue: founders seek every efficiency gain, while salaried engineers with annual bonuses and equity may not have a direct reason to adopt unfamiliar agent workflows. (03:19-04:42)
- The talk criticizes hourly pay for rewarding time spent and project pricing for encouraging padded estimates because engineers are poor at predicting duration and want downside protection. (05:08-06:07)
- Tenex separates roadmapping from execution: strategists distill client product requirements, engineers produce architecture design documents, tickets receive story-point estimates, and engineers are paid per story point after ticket acceptance. (07:36-09:22)
- In one example, a billboard client had slow manual creative moderation; Tenex scoped and built an AI moderation system in two weeks and reported 96% accuracy compared with human moderators. (10:06-10:48)
- In a retail edge-AI example, Tenex expanded a low-power camera heat-mapping deployment by proposing and building five parallel quantized models for heat mapping, queue detection, theft detection, and related signals. (11:02-11:57)
- The talk calls out risks in story-point compensation: inflated estimates, rushed low-quality work, and sharp-elbowed behavior. Tenex counters with separate strategist scoping, internal QA, client approval, and hiring standards. (12:00-14:08)
