## Summary

Philipp Krenn explains retrieval from search-engine fundamentals through semantic and hybrid retrieval: vector search is one retrieval feature, not the whole retrieval layer; inverted indexes shift tokenization and positional work to ingestion; BM25 balances term frequency, rarity, and field length; and production RAG often needs lexical, sparse, dense, filters, RRF, and reranking selected by query shape.

## Notes

- Vector search is framed as one feature of retrieval alongside keyword and hybrid search, with Lucene-style search fundamentals applying broadly beyond Elasticsearch. 02:15-03:05
- Search engines differ from ordinary databases by doing much of the analysis at ingestion: tokenization, offsets, and positions are stored so highlighting and phrase matching do not require reanalyzing long text at query time. 07:17-08:41
- An inverted index stores analyzed tokens, document pointers, occurrence counts, and positions; that makes later retrieval fast because the query can jump from token to matching documents. 22:14-23:24
- BM25 extends TF-IDF-style scoring by considering term frequency saturation, inverse document frequency, and field-length normalization. 39:05-40:49
- Dense vector retrieval represents documents or chunks in learned vector space, but the dimensions are opaque and quality depends on whether the embedding model captures the corpus meaning. 48:09-50:47
- Hybrid search can combine sparse, dense, keyword, or multiple dense searches; RRF blends results by rank position instead of relying on raw scores from different retrieval mechanisms. 01:21:30-01:23:07
- For many production use cases, especially exact brands or short keyword queries, vector-only retrieval can miss terms users know exist, so hybrid search or query-shape routing is often safer. 01:27:11-01:29:00
- Rerankers can rescore candidates from lexical or hybrid retrieval, not only vector results; the transcript reports customer eval gains from reranking existing candidate sets. 01:37:30-01:38:55
