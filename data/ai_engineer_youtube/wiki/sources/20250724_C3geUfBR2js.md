## Summary

Clay Bavor frames customer-facing agents as the next branded digital surface after websites and mobile apps, requiring an "AI architect" role that blends technical capability sense, brand and experience taste, and business-outcome ownership. The source emphasizes starting with narrow valuable customer journeys, recognizing the hidden production burden below model/framework choices, testing nondeterministic agents with simulated users before launch, and closing the loop after launch through conversation review and coaching.

## Notes

- Sierra presents customer-facing agents as branded company surfaces that can handle chat or phone actions for hundreds of millions of consumers, including examples such as SiriusXM's Harmony agent sending a satellite signal to restore radio service. (01:17-02:09)
- The AI architect is compared to the early web master: a role responsible not only for technology, but for how the company's agent looks, feels, sounds, behaves, and serves business outcomes. (02:38-05:03)
- The role can emerge from CX, support, care, retail, engineering, or other teams, but Bavor is especially interested in people close to customers who become hands-on enough with the technology to shape agent behavior. (05:12-06:10)
- Successful agent programs tolerate some probabilistic risk, start from concrete customer or business problems, and may begin as narrowly as processing one return before expanding. (06:34-08:05)
- Mature customers reorganize CX and support around improving the agent: some reviewers inspect hundreds of conversations per day to coach better wording, decisions, empathy, and judgment. (08:09-08:56)
- Bavor warns that build-versus-buy decisions often underestimate the "agent iceberg": regression tests, model migration, voice speaker separation, interruptions, lifecycle tooling, and nontechnical editing/coaching surfaces sit below model and framework choices. (09:16-11:35)
- The agent development lifecycle needs user simulation because a single input/output test is insufficient for nondeterministic software; simulated personas, accounts, and devices can generate large pre-launch test conversations. (11:55-13:40)
- Product teams should track the direction of model capability, not only the current state, by periodically retrying hard tasks and reasoning about cost, latency, and capability trajectories. (14:24-16:49)
