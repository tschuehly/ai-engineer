## Summary

Annika Brundyn and Aastha Jhunjhunwala introduce NVIDIA GR00T N1 as an open, customizable vision-language-action foundation model for humanoid robots. The talk explains why physical AI needs data, training, and edge deployment as one lifecycle; why robotics lacks internet-scale action data; how GR00T N1 combines a slow System 2 planner with a fast System 1 control policy; and why embodiment-specific action decoders let a shared foundation model adapt to different robot bodies and downstream tasks.

## Notes

- The talk frames physical AI as necessary for industries where work requires instruments and devices in the physical world, not chat alone. (01:24-01:57)
- Humanoid form is presented as an engineering shortcut for generalist robots because much of the human-built world is already shaped around human bodies. (01:57-02:35)
- The physical AI lifecycle has three stages: generate or multiply data, consume data during model training, and deploy the trained model on the robot or edge device. (02:37-03:12)
- NVIDIA's "three computer problem" maps those stages to different compute: simulation on OVX/Omniverse-style machines, training on DGX-style systems, and deployment on edge devices such as AGX. (03:15-03:53)
- GR00T N1 is described as an open, customizable, cross-embodiment robotics foundation model with a two-billion-parameter base model that can be modified for specific embodiments and use cases. (03:53-04:45)
- Robotics lacks internet-scale action datasets; real robot trajectories are usually collected through human teleoperation and are scarce, expensive, and bounded by human and robot fatigue. (04:47-05:58)
- The data strategy combines scarce real robot trajectories, abundant but indirect internet videos of humans doing tasks, synthetic simulation data, and world-model-based trajectory multiplication such as DreamGen. (05:58-07:35)
- GR00T N1 consumes image observation, robot state, and language prompt, then outputs robot action trajectories represented as vectors controlling joints and continuous motion. (07:54-09:12)
- The architecture separates a slow System 2 planner that decomposes complex tasks from a fast System 1 executor that runs around 120 Hz. (09:18-10:13)
- The action decoder is embodiment-specific: it translates output tokens into action vectors for a humanoid hand, industrial arm, or other robot body. (11:47-12:53)
- Imitation learning is bottlenecked by expensive expert data, while reinforcement learning avoids that bottleneck but faces sim-to-real transfer gaps. (13:06-14:50)
