## Summary

Jellyfish's pull-request and tool-usage analysis frames AI coding transformation as an outcome-measurement problem: interactive AI coding tools correlate with higher PR throughput and lower cycle time, while autonomous agents are still a small share of merged PR activity. The talk also identifies distributed repository architecture as a likely context bottleneck, because current tools often work one repository at a time and struggle when cross-repo relationships live only in senior engineers' heads.

## Notes

- Jellyfish combines AI coding-tool interactions, autonomous-agent interactions, PR review bots, GitHub/source-control data, and task-system data from roughly 20 million PRs, 200,000 developers, and 1,000 companies, with results spanning June 2024 to the talk's present. (01:03-02:14)
- The talk prefers developer adoption rate over generated lines of code: adoption is measured as the fraction of coding time where a developer uses AI tools, then averaged across the company. (02:23-03:37)
- In the tracked cohort, median company adoption rose from roughly 22% in summer 2024 to near 90%, while only about 44% of companies had any autonomous-agent activity in the prior three months and that activity represented less than 2% of merged PRs. (03:40-05:34)
- Higher AI adoption correlated with about 2x PR throughput and about 24% lower PR cycle time from first commit to merge, but PR throughput should be read as a change metric because absolute PR counts vary with scoping and architecture. (05:44-08:30)
- Higher AI adoption also correlated with PRs about 18% larger in net lines added, with similar file counts; Jellyfish did not find a statistically significant relationship with bug-ticket creation or PR revert rates, and saw higher bug-resolution rates because teams used AI on well-scoped bug backlog work. (08:46-10:54)
- When segmented by active repos per engineer, centralized and balanced architectures showed roughly 4x PR-throughput trends, distributed architectures resembled the global 2x trend, and highly distributed architectures showed essentially no positive correlation between AI adoption and throughput. (12:12-14:44)
- The proposed explanation is context: current coding tools are usually best at one repository at a time, while cross-repo relationships and product/system context are often undocumented or inaccessible to tools and agents. (14:44-15:26)
