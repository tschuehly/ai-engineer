## Summary

Jyh Jing Hwang presents Waymo's EMMA research as an exploration of Gemini-style multimodal foundation models for autonomous driving: route text plus 360-degree camera video can be trained self-supervised to predict future waypoints, enriched with reasoning channels for critical-object and meta-decision explanations, and evaluated with generated sensor simulations that vary weather and time of day.

## Notes

- Long-tail driving scenarios, such as a traffic controller overriding a red light or a scooter rider slipping on wet pavement, become frequent enough at fleet scale that autonomous-driving systems must handle them deliberately. (03:39-06:20)
- EMMA uses route text and eight surrounding cameras as input to a Gemini-based model that predicts future vehicle waypoints. (06:30-07:53)
- Driving logs make the waypoint task self-supervised because the future vehicle locations are already recorded. (07:58-08:19)
- The prototype tests camera-only, high-definition-map-free planning, which is useful for generalization research but exposes weather and nighttime sensitivity in generated-sensor evaluations. (08:19-08:45, 15:45-16:42)
- The talk treats evaluation as part of the solution: open-loop replay is weaker than simulation, and generated video can create controllable sensor conditions for planner testing. (14:05-16:42)
