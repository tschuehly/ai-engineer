## Summary

Jasper Zhang argues that AI compute shortages should be addressed partly by better allocation of existing GPU supply, not only by building more data centers. The talk frames a GPU marketplace as an aggregation and orchestration layer over fragmented providers, using Kubernetes-style agents, flexible spot/on-demand/reserved access, provider benchmarking, and resale of idle capacity to reduce cost, improve utilization, and support training plus online and offline inference workloads.

## Notes

- The talk's thesis is qualified: building data centers still matters, but building data centers alone does not solve AI compute supply, cost, and sustainability constraints. 00:31-00:41
- Zhang cites fast-growing data-center demand alongside construction, grid-connection, energy, emissions, and supply-deficit constraints, then argues that idle GPU capacity makes allocation a parallel infrastructure problem. 01:36-04:07
- A marketplace or aggregation layer can connect fragmented GPU clouds and data centers to users who need GPUs, reducing the need to negotiate with many suppliers directly. 04:11-04:49
- Hyperbolic's example architecture uses a Kubernetes-like agent, HyperDOS, so participating clusters can join a global orchestration layer; users can rent spot, on-demand, reserved, or hosted-model capacity. 05:03-05:40, 13:11-14:11
- The talk claims marketplace aggregation can reduce compute cost through queueing-style pooling and a uniform distribution channel, with the source's example showing large H100 price differences across providers. 06:13-07:13
- Flexible GPU access is framed as matching workload phases: reserve a baseline, burst during training experiments, then release or resell idle capacity after training shifts into hosting. 08:29-10:25
- Zhang says customers want to run AI workloads, not merely own GPUs, so marketplaces can evolve toward all-in-one platforms for training, online inference, and offline inference. 11:21-11:43
