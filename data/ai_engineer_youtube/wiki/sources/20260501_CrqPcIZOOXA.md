## Summary

Mayank Pant frames AI pricing as a fast-moving product and infrastructure problem: AI companies face unpredictable compute costs, power-user margin risk, technical charge metrics that customers may not understand, and feature value that changes quickly. The talk recommends defining customer-perceived value, choosing charge metrics that map to that value, using hybrid pricing to combine predictable revenue with usage-based margin protection, adding billing guardrails, and keeping pricing infrastructure flexible enough to iterate.

## Notes

- AI pricing differs from traditional SaaS because margins are lower and usage-sensitive; 5-10% of users can consume 80% of compute, and external infrastructure costs can move unpredictably. (01:44-02:31)
- Technical pricing in tokens or API calls may fit infrastructure sellers but can confuse application customers who think in outputs such as decks, slides, images, documents, solved tickets, hires, or qualified leads. (02:32-03:03, 09:01-10:08)
- The talk presents a five-step pricing workflow: define value, define the charge metric, pick the pricing model, add guardrails, and iterate. (06:11-06:33, 09:01-15:15)
- Hybrid pricing combines a base fee with a scaling or usage fee so customers can start with predictable commitment while heavier use pays for the value consumed. (11:36-13:03)
- Guardrails include usage caps, threshold notifications, manual or automatic top-ups, pausing until the next period, and rate limiting to prevent bad code from burning through usage. (13:10-15:06)
- Customer-facing plans or credit balances can remain stable while the internal mapping of credits to features changes as capabilities become standard or new features are introduced. (16:13-16:43, 19:17-23:00)
