## Summary

Simon Podhajsky argues that read-only personal AI systems are not incomplete agents but a separate product category: observers that synthesize patterns across email, journals, tasks, CRM, browser history, and notes without modifying those source systems. The talk shows how cross-source "cognitive exhaust" can reveal intention-action gaps, attention drift, and relationship opportunities, while also warning that aggregated personal context creates mosaic-effect privacy risk and does not eliminate exfiltration risk when shell or network access remains.

## Notes

- The system starts with six read-only sources and intentionally gives the AI no permission to write back into the original systems (00:30-00:39).
- Useful outputs come from cross-source signals: intention-action gaps, attention drift, and relationship decay are not visible from a single tool such as email, tasks, or browsing history alone (01:03-01:26).
- The architecture separates source systems, analysis workspace, and reviewable output; outputs can land in a separate Obsidian vault, Notion workspace, text files, or another review destination (01:29-01:51).
- Weekly reflection runs as a Claude command or skill that launches a Python script, gathers read-only source data, calls the Anthropic API for structured outputs, and creates a Markdown document for human review (02:11-02:46).
- A second workflow maps recent reading to relevant people in a personal network by combining browser history, curated databases, Vivaldi SQLite data, and CRM or friend-relationship data, but still only suggests who the user might contact (03:41-04:08, 04:14-07:04).
- The read-only constraint is justified by risk asymmetry: a bad read-only suggestion can be ignored, while a bad write action can affect relationships, career, or reputation (07:28-07:57).
- The talk argues that write-back can contaminate the observational data: once the AI writes into the sources, the system observes a human-AI hybrid rather than the user's own patterns (07:57-08:29).
- Read-only does not make the system secure. Aggregating many small personal signals creates mosaic-effect privacy risk, and shell access or open network access can still provide external communication paths for exfiltration (09:50-11:14).
