## Summary

Denys Linkov argues that modern AI teams should be shaped around the company's actual bottleneck rather than a reflexive push to hire top AI researchers. The talk frames AI delivery as a cross-functional system spanning use-case definition, product integration, ROI, data, workflow testing, interfaces, customer empathy, model training, model serving, domain expertise, and ongoing learning.

## Notes

- Company type changes the missing capability: technology companies may need domain/data expertise, tech-enabled companies may need technology capability, and verticalized companies can succeed or fail depending on how well the two sides combine, 01:13-02:40.
- Linkov challenges reflexively hiring AI researchers: unless the company is a model provider or has scale/specialty needs, model pretraining or advanced fine-tuning may not be the first value bottleneck, 03:53-04:33.
- The AI team workload includes defining use cases, integrating into existing products, measuring ROI, finding data, testing workflows, building interfaces, selling, and making customers care, 05:13-05:52.
- A team should identify whether its bottleneck is feature shipping, acquisition, retention, monetization, scalability, reliability, or observability before hiring, 06:10-06:42.
- In one team-building example, the target skill mix was model training, model serving, and business acumen; the required bar was enough model architecture, encoder fine-tuning, data engineering, platform abstraction use, and customer conversation skill, not frontier-model research depth, 07:21-09:16.
- As open-source and commercial AI tooling improved, the same organization could rely more on existing serving platforms and commercial APIs, while domain knowledge for medical-record processing became a higher bar, 09:18-10:30.
- Inner loops are daily activities needed for team success, while outer loops are differentiating capabilities; weak technical loops hurt execution and weak domain loops hurt product-market fit, 10:43-11:55.
- Upskilling should teach people to build, become domain experts, and be human-facing; domain experts should define use cases and write evaluations, while engineers should join customer calls, 12:46-13:43.
- Hiring should be for holding context and acting on context; agents may help, but expertise is needed to verify context and execution, and machines cannot be held accountable, 14:07-14:47.
- Hiring trends should be checked against first principles and interviews should ask questions relevant to the job instead of unrelated coding puzzles that LLMs can now solve, 15:07-16:11.
