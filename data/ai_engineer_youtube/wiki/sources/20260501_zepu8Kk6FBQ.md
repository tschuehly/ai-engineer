## Summary

Swyx describes how AI Engineer uses agents such as Devin and Town Assistant outside narrow coding tasks: converting Figma and design references into site changes, managing conference schedule data as code, researching vendors, coordinating speaker changes, and preparing curated research documents. The durable lesson is that agents can increase small-team throughput when they remove dependency-chain chores, make non-technical collaborators comfortable with natural artifacts, and shift products toward agent-facing APIs, CLIs, and MCP surfaces rather than human-only dashboards.

## Notes

- AI Engineer operated as a nine-person tiny team running a more-than-$9M business, and the talk positions agents as operational leverage for tiny teams rather than only coding acceleration. (02:23-02:43)
- Swyx describes connecting Devin to Slack and Figma, then using it to turn a Figma design into the live ai.engineer website, reducing a workstream that might have taken weeks. (03:21-05:12)
- A key claimed benefit is that agents absorb dependency-chain chores such as setup, dependency installation, and prerequisite integration tasks, while also enabling parallel work rather than only autonomous work. (04:16-04:47)
- Non-technical collaborators adopted agent workflows by annotating designs and prompting as they would communicate with another human, which made agent use accessible beyond engineers. (05:33-06:03)
- The team used agents for conference data management, including speaker and schedule changes, after moving content from a CMS into code as the source of truth. (07:46-09:20)
- Agents were also used for ETL from external vendor systems, vendor research and buying, and speaker research compiled from rough Apple Notes into a formatted Notion document. (09:21-11:43)
- The talk warns that teams should not dismiss concerns from less agent-enthusiastic employees, because those employees often absorb the cleanup when agent-led changes go wrong. (11:49-12:35)
- Swyx connects this operational pattern to agent experience: if bots are becoming primary users, APIs, CLIs, and MCP surfaces matter more than dashboards alone. (12:48-13:38)
