## Summary

Raiza Martin frames AI product work as a product-judgment problem rather than a raw model-capability problem: teams should rebuild interfaces around clear user outcomes, earn trust by delivering the first promised job reliably, expose model limits instead of hiding them, and use restraint so AI features create agency and delight rather than a kitchen sink of demos.

## Notes

- AI has blurred product, engineering, and UX roles because access to expertise is easier, making personal clarity about one's value and product point of view more important. 00:36-02:04
- Current AI interfaces are in a clunky transition period because many products were designed before AI-shaped interactions were possible; this creates a rebuilding opportunity. 03:00-05:08
- Product work starts with finding an opportunity in the chaos, then forcing a product into existence with personal clarity about vision, purpose, and taste. 05:58-08:55
- NotebookLM/Tailwind was hard to explain internally and externally, but the clear use case was putting many documents in one tool and interacting with them rather than bolting AI onto an existing surface. 09:03-10:29
- Taste is framed as outcome clarity, not aesthetics: the core question is the single outcome the product must deliver flawlessly for every user. 10:35-11:14
- "AI demo disease" describes a product that demos well but is grounded in hype rather than a durable user outcome. 11:20-11:44
- Users care less that something is AI than whether the product delivers their intent in a way that feels inevitable. 11:44-12:05
- NotebookLM's auto-summary helped users understand the unfamiliar behavior of uploading many files and interacting with them; it acted as both a tutorial and a useful at-a-glance feature. 12:10-13:15
- Trust is built by exposing model edges and designing failure states humanely, rather than instrumenting only the best case. 13:21-14:55
- A good AI app still needs deterministic product basics before delightful probabilistic behavior; a failed first summarization can cause users to leave permanently. 14:55-16:52
- Delight should come through agency rather than trickery: NotebookLM audio generation felt magical because users selected the source material and felt like equal participants with the machine. 17:14-19:02
- Teams should distinguish between shipping model capabilities and shipping new outcomes; product builders find the right boundary by living with the product as users do. 19:05-20:13
- Huxe initially had chat, podcasts, news, images, and video, but daily use and user trials showed people gravitated to one thing, exposing lack of focus and "demo disease." 23:10-24:29
