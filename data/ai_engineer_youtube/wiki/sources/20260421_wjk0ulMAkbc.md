## Summary

Tuomas Artman argues that AI makes implementation speed abundant, so durable product advantage shifts toward saying no, preserving taste, exposing engineers to customer context, and maintaining quality routines that keep bugs and small regressions from accumulating.

## Extracted Concepts

- [AI-amplified shipping speed needs stronger product taste](../concepts/ai-amplified-shipping-speed-needs-stronger-product-taste.md) - this source frames fast AI-assisted implementation as a feature-creep risk unless teams preserve judgment about what not to ship.
- [Quality Wednesdays train engineers to notice small regressions](../concepts/quality-wednesdays-train-engineers-to-notice-small-regressions.md) - this source describes Linear's recurring practice of finding and presenting small quality fixes.
- [Zero-bug policies turn bug inflow into immediate work](../concepts/zero-bug-policies-turn-bug-inflow-into-immediate-work.md) - this source explains treating new bugs as top-priority work instead of letting a backlog form.
- [Product engineers need direct customer context](../concepts/product-engineers-need-direct-customer-context.md) - this source connects product engineering to direct access to customer Slack channels, recorded calls, and tagged product feedback.
- [AI agents still need human taste for interaction quality](../concepts/ai-agents-still-need-human-taste-for-interaction-quality.md) - this source distinguishes agent-generated UI mechanics from human judgment about timing, animation, and feel.

## Notes

- AI reduces the old friction that forced teams to think before building, making it easier to ship every request and produce convoluted software that confuses users. (00:46-02:15)
- Linear does not ship customer requests directly; the team collects feedback, finds the root problem behind request clusters, and designs a solution for that underlying need. (04:25-05:38)
- Artman says about 10% of Linear bugs were already fixed by a single-shot AI instance, while higher-level design and functionality choices still require human thinking. (05:46-06:32)
- Quality is hard to measure with a single short-term metric; poor quality may not affect revenue until customers gradually choose a better-feeling competitor. (08:15-11:56)
- Quality Wednesdays ask each engineer to find and show one quality fix, which trains the team to notice small interaction issues during unrelated feature work. (12:00-16:04)
- Linear's zero-bug policy assigns reported bugs immediately, makes them the highest priority, and distinguishes bug fixes from separate quality improvements. (16:39-19:42)
- Current agents can inspect screenshots or DOM state but do not yet experience time, frustration, or interaction feel, so generated animations may be mechanically correct but unnatural until a human tunes them. (20:08-22:18)
- Linear hires for product engineering through a paid one-week work trial and exposes engineers to customer Slack channels, recorded calls, and tagged feedback so product judgment is not isolated in PM roles. (22:41-26:23)
