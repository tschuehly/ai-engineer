## Summary

Yegor Denisov-Blanch presents an enterprise AI software-engineering ROI framework based on field data, arguing that AI impact depends less on raw token spend than on codebase hygiene, usage quality, and balanced outcome metrics that catch quality and rework regressions.

## Notes

- The talk says its research combines time-series git history with cross-company data and an ML model trained to replicate panels of human expert code evaluations. (00:21-01:38)
- A matched cohort of AI-using and non-AI teams showed a median productivity gain around 10%, but the speaker emphasizes a widening gap between top and bottom performers. (02:03-03:12)
- Raw AI usage, measured as token spend per engineer per month, had a loose correlation with productivity lift, suggesting usage quality matters more than usage volume. (03:14-04:02)
- An experimental environment cleanliness index based on tests, types, documentation, modularity, and code quality correlated more strongly with AI productivity gains. (04:02-04:49)
- The proposed ROI framework measures AI usage and engineering outcomes, then pairs a primary engineering-output metric with guardrails for rework, quality, risk, people, and DevOps. (10:28-12:51)
- A case study found that PR count rose after AI adoption, while code quality decreased, rework increased, and effective output did not meaningfully improve. (12:57-15:28)
