## Summary

Cassidy Hardin explains Gemma 4 as a four-model open-weight family: smaller effective 2B and 4B models optimized for on-device multimodal input, plus larger 26B MoE and 31B dense models aimed at reasoning, coding, function calling, and agentic workflows. The useful engineering details are the architecture tradeoffs behind that split: interleaved local/global attention, grouped query attention, sparse MoE activation, per-layer embeddings stored outside VRAM, and configurable multimodal token budgets for vision and audio inputs.

## Notes

- Gemma 4 is presented as four model categories: effective 2B and 4B models for on-device applications, a 26B mixture-of-experts model with roughly 3.8-3.9B active parameters per forward pass, and a 31B dense model for advanced reasoning. (00:51-03:28)
- The Apache 2.0 license is framed as a developer-accessibility choice intended to support the development lifecycle from testing to deployment. (01:54-02:15)
- The 31B model is described as having a 256K context length and native support for thinking, function calling, and structured JSON outputs for autonomous workflows. (02:25-02:56)
- Gemma 4 dense models interleave local and global layers, using local sliding windows of 512 tokens in smaller models and 1,024 tokens in larger models while keeping global layers able to attend across preceding tokens. (04:24-05:48)
- Grouped query attention reduces key/value head cost, with stronger grouping in global layers and an 8-query-to-1-key/value ratio described as an efficiency improvement across models. (05:51-06:43)
- Gemma 4 MoE uses one shared expert, 128 total experts, and eight activated experts per forward pass, replacing dense feedforward blocks with sparse expert selection. (06:47-07:38)
- Effective 2B and 4B models are defined by operating parameters rather than total representational parameters; the E2B is described as operating at 2.3B parameters with 5.1B representational depth. (07:42-08:17)
- Per-layer embeddings add layer-specific token embeddings while storing the PLE table in flash memory instead of VRAM, reducing pressure on scarce phone and laptop memory. (08:25-10:56)
- Gemma 4 vision support lets developers choose image resolution and soft-token budget; OCR and object recognition should use higher image budgets, while text-only uses can allocate less. (11:36-16:06)
- Audio support in E2B and E4B combines an audio tokenizer, mel spectrogram processing, convolutional downsampling, and a 305M-parameter conformer for translation and speech recognition inputs. (16:31-17:33)
- Developers can self-host via Hugging Face, Kaggle, and Ollama, while larger 31B and 26B models are also exposed through AI Studio and Vertex for hosted prototyping. (18:06-18:34)
