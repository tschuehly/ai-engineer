## Summary

Christopher Lovejoy argues that vertical LLM applications win by building systems that turn domain-expert review into measurable improvements, not by relying only on stronger models. Anterior's workflow combines customer-specific context, domain-expert labeling, failure-mode ontologies, production-derived eval sets, and domain-expert PM prioritization so clinicians and engineers can close last-mile accuracy gaps.

## Notes

- Vertical AI's limitation is framed as whether the system understands the specific industry, customer, and workflow context, not whether the base model can generally reason, 01:19-01:55.
- The clinical example shows hidden ambiguity in apparently simple requirements: what counts as conservative therapy, unsuccessful treatment, and six weeks of documentation depends on domain interpretation, 02:25-05:02.
- Anterior reports moving from a strong model/pipeline baseline around 95% to around 99% by adding an adaptive domain-intelligence loop that converts domain insights into pipeline context, 05:29-06:33.
- The measurement loop asks domain experts and customers to identify the few metrics that matter most, such as false approvals in medical-necessity review, 07:10-08:21.
- Failure-mode ontologies should be led by domain experts; Anterior grouped medical-necessity failures into medical-record extraction, clinical reasoning, and rules interpretation categories before drilling into subtypes, 08:22-09:15.
- Expert review tooling lets clinicians inspect source records, guidelines, AI outputs, reasoning, correctness, and failure-mode labels in one surface, 09:15-10:35.
- Labeled production failures become ready-made data sets for engineers to improve one failure mode while watching for regressions across pipeline versions, 10:48-12:02.
- Domain experts can also propose domain knowledge additions from the same review surface; evals or human gates decide whether those suggestions should reach production, 12:04-14:23.
- The full loop routes production outputs through expert review, performance insights, domain-expert PM prioritization, engineering experiments, eval thresholds, and release decisions, 16:44-18:08.
