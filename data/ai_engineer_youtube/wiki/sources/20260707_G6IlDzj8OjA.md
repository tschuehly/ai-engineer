## Summary

Victoria Melnikova (runs new business at the developer-tools consultancy Evil Martians; hosts the Dev Propulsion Labs podcast) argues that in 2026 the scarce skill for developer-tool and AI startups is distribution, not building: "building software has never been easier, which means the bottleneck has moved" to distribution, made harder because AI flooded inboxes and feeds with slop so it is "really hard to tell the real deal" and cut through the noise. She frames go-to-market diagnosis with Evil Martians' PMF Compass (two axes — product signal vs. revenue, derived from 37 successful devtools): if signal > revenue, work on distribution; if revenue > signal, work on product; if equal, go full speed — and observes that 9 out of 10 early-stage devtools have signal stronger than revenue "because technical founders really hate marketing." She lays out six GTM-hygiene foundations (crystallize a validated value proposition with paying customers → understand your "shelf space" in a developer's life → become the authority on the problem → map the ecosystem of friends and foes → distribute early, even before the product is ready), then a set of SF-centric tactics drawn from interviews with devtool founders (Sam Lambert/PlanetScale, David Cramer/Sentry, Paul Copplestone/Supabase, Zeno Rocha, Jason Bosco/Typesense): come to SF (a "network game"; staying after YC ≈ raise twice as much twice as fast), get loud with banners (SF billboards do two jobs — signaling that you exist/invented a category, and earning subconscious mind share; a credibility proxy for non-VC-backed companies), do events (meetups, launch parties, user conferences like Supabase Select), do fun/"crazy" marketing, and lean into fail stories ("perfect is boring; people love a good fail story, especially with an elegant recovery"). The through-line is that the durable moat is the founder's personal brand — "it's not your model, it's not your team, it's not your funding, it's you" — because founder-led sales builds trust ("people want to see a real human behind the product"), the market pendulum is swinging back to craft/artisanal work, and AI "cannot really replace you, but it can amplify your signal." Advice: be unapologetically yourself and turn your unique quirks into a "genius zone."
