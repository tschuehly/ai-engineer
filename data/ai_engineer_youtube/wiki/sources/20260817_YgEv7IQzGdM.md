## Summary

Ankit Jain (co-founder, Aviator, building an AI code verification platform) returns to a five-layer trust model he published on Latent Space a few months earlier and corrects it: the model treated review as a correctness problem, and review was never only about correctness. His starting claim is that the debate about when teams stop reading code line by line is already settled because they stopped — over 30% of changes merge with no review, review wait time is 4x what it was, and the review that does happen is an AI writing code, an AI reviewing it, the two arguing in a web UI, and a human skimming the thread before merging. "When AI reviews and nobody reads, we have configured the wrong thing." He splits review into two halves: *semantic accuracy* (bugs, conventions, security), where better tooling can substitute, and *alignment* (knowledge sharing, mentorship, architectural feedback, onboarding), which "must survive." Spec-driven development does not rescue alignment, because a spec written up front with no feedback loop is the 1970 waterfall model, and the decisions that actually matter get made in the coding session and thrown away when the pull request opens. His proposal keeps them: capture the session, convert those decisions into acceptance criteria with an LLM, pair them with invariants mined from your own recurring review comments (the "AI slop registry"), generate a test plan, and have a verification system run it against a live preview — filling forms in a browser, capturing screenshots and database snapshots as evidence. The review surface becomes intent plus evidence instead of the diff.

## Cross-Source Discrepancy

- Jain's opening statistics almost certainly come from the same Faros AI 2026 survey the wiki already cites through Alex Volkov's ThursdAI talk (861%, incidents per PR, merges without review all appear in both), but the merge-without-review figure is restated differently and the two readings are not equivalent. Volkov's rendering is "a 31% increase in PRs merged with no review at all"; Jain's is "over 30% of changes are actually getting merged without a review at all" — a rate, not a delta. Neither speaker shows the underlying table here, so the wiki records both phrasings and treats the direction (merges without review are rising sharply) as the supported claim rather than the absolute share.
