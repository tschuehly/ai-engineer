## Summary

Nathan Wan frames revenue cycle management as an underexplored healthcare AI domain where manual, rules-driven, inconsistent administrative work directly affects hospital margins. The reusable AI engineering lesson is to connect end-to-end operational data to measurable outcomes, prevent late denials upstream where possible, and keep clinical experts in the loop when GenAI drafts appeal artifacts that depend on patient records, payer policies, and medical necessity arguments.

## Notes

- RCM covers the financial process of the patient's healthcare journey and includes eligibility checks, registration, documentation, coding, and denial management, 01:26-01:39, 05:05-05:21.
- The talk reports that 40% of hospitals operate at a negative margin and links the margin pressure to delays, denials, rework, and lost revenue around manual revenue-cycle processes, 00:23-00:52.
- Denials are a core friction point because providers spend time and money managing appeals while payment can be delayed for months; the example claim was denied and appealed four times and paid roughly 200 days after the procedure, 07:18-08:40.
- Payers also use AI to increase adjudication and denial volume, which can increase provider backlog unless provider-side systems prevent errors earlier, 08:48-09:17.
- Many denials are preventable technical or documentation errors rather than medical disagreements, so longitudinal RCM data can connect late denials back to earlier missing data, registration errors, or prior authorization problems, 09:19-11:35.
- Clinical denials require appeal packets assembled from patient records, clinical guidelines, payer policies, and coverage rules; GenAI can assist, but clinical experts should approve final letters before submission, 12:20-14:29.
- Ensemble reports a 40% time reduction for the clinical appeal process and evaluates quality through denial overturn rate and directly measured ROI, 14:52-15:29.
