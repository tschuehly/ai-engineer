## Summary

Eliza Cabrera and Jeremy Silva argue that AI product work should stop treating AI as a separate strategy sidecar or visible feature label and instead integrate AI capability, reliability risk, evaluation, and bottom-up discovery into the core product roadmap. Their crawl-walk-run framing starts with embedded backend acceleration, moves through contextual personalized copilots, and reaches cross-product autonomous or proactive experiences only after teams have accumulated enough product, platform, risk, and UX maturity.

## Notes

- Treating AI as the headline of product, go-to-market, or strategy helped communicate technological leadership early, but by 2025 similar AI positioning no longer creates differentiation because many teams are doing the same thing. 01:07-02:03
- Chat UIs, content UIs, multimodal demos, RAG, larger context windows, copilots, and agents are useful ways to learn the technology, but they do not by themselves produce a differentiated strategy. 02:08-03:50
- A common enterprise failure pattern is a centralized AI strategy running as a sidecar to core product strategy, which produces separate initiatives, separate teams, and bolt-on non-integrated features. 03:50-04:28
- The talk identifies three causes of AI sideshows: quarantining AI risk into corners of the product, prioritizing visible technology demonstrations over customer needs, and pushing solutions top-down instead of pairing top-level strategy with bottom-up product discovery. 04:28-05:41
- The proposed remedy is to integrate AI risk into product planning, build evaluation and testing systems, start from the customer problem, and give product teams room to experiment, prototype, and fail fast under a clear strategy. 05:41-07:09
- The crawl phase embeds AI into existing workflows without much new product surface, such as semantic search over previous support questions or generated knowledge-base content and translations. 07:40-08:50, 11:00-13:31
- The walk phase adds contextual and personalized AI surface area without fully rethinking the app architecture, such as a Workday assistant that understands page context and sensitive data boundaries. 08:50-09:20, 13:31-14:59
- The run phase requires rethinking UI, UX, and app architecture for interoperable cross-product AI, such as autonomous support agents or Workday assistant capabilities that listen to policy changes, notify users, and act across the suite. 09:20-10:16, 14:59-15:52
- Dynamic products may move beyond yesterday's roadmap by using newly digitized data, multimodal inputs, interoperability, and environmental responsiveness to extend the product problem space. 16:09-17:42
