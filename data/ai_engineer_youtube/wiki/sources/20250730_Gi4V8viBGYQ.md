## Summary

David Mytton frames AI bot defense as layered request policy: site owners need to distinguish beneficial search crawlers, training crawlers, user-triggered AI fetches, and browser-like operator agents, then combine voluntary crawler policy, user-agent verification, IP reputation, proof-of-work friction, signatures, fingerprints, and rate limits to control automated traffic without breaking legitimate access.

## Notes

- Automated clients are already a large share of web traffic, and AI crawlers can turn dynamic pages, database-backed content, and large assets into direct infrastructure and bandwidth costs. (00:39-03:29)
- The talk distinguishes OpenAI SearchBot-like index crawlers, ChatGPT User real-time fetches, GPTBot training crawlers, and operator-style browser agents that may act on behalf of a person. (05:02-07:28)
- `robots.txt` is useful for cooperative crawlers and for clarifying site policy, but it is voluntary and can expose disallowed paths to hostile scrapers. (07:42-08:37)
- User-agent strings are useful but spoofable; claimed crawler identities should be verified with source IP and reverse DNS where providers support it. (08:40-10:23)
- IP reputation can include data-center, ASN/operator, VPN/proxy, residential/mobile, and geography signals, but geolocation and residential-proxy routing make it unreliable as a sole source of truth. (10:27-12:56)
- CAPTCHA can be solved cheaply by AI or through audio transcription, while proof-of-work raises cost for bulk crawling but may not beat high-profit abuse. (13:01-14:42)
- HTTP message signatures, private access tokens, TLS/HTTP fingerprints such as JA4, and fingerprint-keyed rate limits are stronger tools for identifying or throttling clients across rotating IP addresses. (15:34-18:41)
