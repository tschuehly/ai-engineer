## Summary

Alvaro Morales argues that AI pricing needs active instrumentation and experimentation because model costs, capabilities, adoption patterns, and customer ROI expectations move too quickly for static SaaS pricing. The talk adds a direct-versus-indirect monetization decision, a value-metric spectrum from resource units to outcomes, and a simulation workflow that tests alternate pricing structures against real usage data before launch.

## Notes

- AI pricing is harder than traditional software pricing because model and inference costs, product capabilities, and customer ROI expectations change quickly, while provider bills can create immediate margin pressure. (01:28-03:08)
- Teams should decide whether an AI feature should be directly monetized as a standalone product or add-on, indirectly monetized by driving upsell, bundled to incentivize adoption, or offered free because it increases downstream behavior such as bookings. (03:27-05:53)
- Agent pricing can sit on a spectrum from resource units such as tokens, to workflow steps and whole workflows, to labor-replacement units, to true outcome-based pricing. (05:54-07:05)
- Outcome-based pricing requires customer and vendor alignment on the outcome definition plus objective measurement; customer support is easier because resolution and escalation can be checked, while creative or less directly financial outcomes are harder. (07:45-09:35)
- Orb customers used "shell" pricing structures during closed beta to test AI agent monetization without billing customers, motivating simulations over product-usage data. (11:19-13:23)
- Pricing simulations can compare add-on fees, token charges, tiered included usage, top-line revenue impact, average customer change, revenue mix, and per-customer impact before a pricing launch. (13:29-16:43)
