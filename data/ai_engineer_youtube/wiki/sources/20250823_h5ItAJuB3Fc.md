## Summary

Diego Rodriguez argues that generative-media evaluation must move beyond easily measured object, color, and embedding metrics toward perceptual and preference-aware evaluation. The source connects codec-style perceptual compression, internet media artifacts, FID sensitivity, and subjective aesthetic judgment into a practical warning: media evals need to model what humans notice, what artifacts the data already contains, and whose taste or creative intent the product is optimizing for.

## Notes

- Aesthetic evaluation is framed as evaluation under human perception, opinion, and taste rather than only objective task completion (00:36-00:48).
- The talk uses an obviously unnatural AI-generated hand as a failure example: humans react immediately, while a model-driven analysis can over-process and still soften the defect (00:50-01:25).
- JPEG-style compression deletes information according to human perceptual limits; the speaker generalizes the same pattern to audio and video codecs (04:02-05:55).
- Because internet training data is often compressed, media models and evals may inherit codec artifacts and human perceptual blind spots rather than neutral visual truth (06:33-07:00).
- FID/Clean-FID-style scores can change sharply under JPEG artifacts even when images are perceptually similar, which makes them risky as sole model-quality metrics (07:00-07:40).
- The speaker recommends metrics that account for human perception, data artifacts, and individual opinion, including classifiers trained from examples of images a user considers good (12:30-16:21).
