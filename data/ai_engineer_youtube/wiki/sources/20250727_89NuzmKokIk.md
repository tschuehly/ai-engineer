## Summary

Taylor Jordan Smith frames production LLM evaluation as a layered engineering practice: start with system-performance benchmarks, add factual and formatting checks, then layer in safety, bias, and application-specific custom evals. The workshop demonstrates GuideLLM for inference latency and throughput, lm-eval-harness with MMLU-Pro for factual benchmark work, and promptfoo for customizable safety and bias checks, with CI/CD-style automation as the intended production direction.

## Notes

- Production GenAI systems need evals because reliability, scalability, safety, policy restrictions, legal exposure, bias, cost, performance, and knowledge cutoffs all affect whether a model can be used with customers. (01:45-05:01)
- Benchmarking is presented as a subcategory of evaluation: benchmarks use controlled data sets and tasks to compare models or hardware, while evaluation is the broader end-to-end assessment process. (09:26-10:24)
- A pragmatic eval rollout can start with component-level tests such as RAG chunk retrieval or latency/throughput, then expand to integration, UI, and end-to-end tests as priorities and resources allow. (12:12-13:26)
- The model-evaluation pyramid starts at system performance, then adds formatting, factual accuracy, safety, bias, and application-specific custom evaluations. (13:30-14:52)
- GuideLLM is demonstrated for latency, throughput, inter-token latency, time-to-first-token, request-rate sweeps, P99, and JSON outputs, with input/output token profiles shaped by chatbot or RAG use cases. (14:53-23:08)
- The workshop then moves to lm-eval-harness with MMLU-Pro for factual benchmark work and promptfoo for customizable safety and bias tests. (25:33-30:42)
- For proprietary data, the speaker suggests adapting open-source benchmark formats, such as MMLU-style multiple-choice evals, and changing data sources or forking the harness as needed. (26:07-26:59)
- In response to a production-eval question, the speaker describes the desired shape as CI/CD automation for eval frameworks, analogous to unit testing but tied to running customer use cases. (31:03-31:58)
