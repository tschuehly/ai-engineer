## Summary

BlackRock describes a modular framework for scaling internal knowledge apps in investment operations: domain experts use a sandbox to define extraction templates, validations, field dependencies, model strategies, and review runs, then an app factory turns those artifacts into deployed document-processing applications with platform, access, cost, and human-review controls.

## Notes

- BlackRock classifies internal AI applications into document extraction, complex workflow automation, Q&A/chat interfaces, and agentic systems, all aimed at augmenting or accelerating existing investment-operations systems. (01:32-02:19)
- The new-issue operations example ingests a prospectus or term sheet, extracts structured values with domain-expert input, applies transformation logic, and integrates results into downstream security setup systems. (02:23-03:34)
- The scaling bottlenecks include domain-expert prompt engineering, LLM strategy selection, context-window limits for long documents, model/vendor testing, deployment, access control, cluster choice, and cost controls. (03:38-06:55)
- The architecture separates a sandbox for prompt, extraction-template, model-strategy, run, transformer, and executor iteration from an app factory that takes definitions and spins out user-facing apps. (07:01-08:29)
- Extraction templates include field names, data types, extracted-versus-derived source, required status, field dependencies, validations, and QC checks such as callable-bond fields requiring call date and call price. (08:47-11:04)
- Operators review extraction outputs, compare runs, and can build low-code/no-code transformation and execution workflows instead of manually downloading CSV/JSON outputs and pushing downstream transformations. (11:46-12:54)
- The presenters caution that production knowledge apps need prompt-engineering skills for domain experts, firm education on LLM strategies, ROI checks against off-the-shelf products, and human-in-the-loop design first in regulated environments. (12:57-14:05)
