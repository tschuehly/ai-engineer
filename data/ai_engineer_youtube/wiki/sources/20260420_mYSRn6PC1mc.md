## Summary

This workshop frames deep research as an end-to-end AI engineering system that needs different autonomy levels for different work phases: exploratory research benefits from an agent that plans, searches, inspects, pivots, cites sources, and iterates, while technical writing benefits from a more constrained workflow with explicit guidelines, few-shot examples, reviewer loops, trace observability, and calibrated judge evaluation.

## Notes

- AI engineering system choices are constrained by cost per task, latency, quality, and data privacy, and the workshop describes an "autonomy slider" from prompting to workflows to agentic systems where more autonomy also means less control and higher cost. 06:22-07:44
- Many client requests that sound like "build an agent" are better modeled as simple workflows with data, tools, memory, prompt chains, routers, and strict conditions rather than open-ended agents. 07:49-09:26
- A deep research system is presented as a reasoning system that plans what to research, has autonomy over the research path, uses web or API tools, cites sources, uses feedback loops, and pivots before synthesizing. 20:28-21:27
- For article production, the speakers split research and writing because research needs flexibility while writing needs constraints; the research agent produces a `research.md` artifact that the writing workflow consumes sequentially without heavy orchestration. 25:56-28:10
- The writing workflow loads guideline Markdown, research Markdown, profile/static files, and few-shot examples into a system prompt, generates a draft, then runs evaluator-optimizer loops with separate writer and reviewer contexts to reduce self-approval bias. 01:11:21-01:22:47
- Observability is needed because terminal logs are hard to debug for workflows and agents; Opik is used to capture traces, LLM calls, tool calls, inputs, outputs, metadata, latency, cost, threads, and trace details. 01:33:29-01:35:58
- The judge is treated as a binary classifier that outputs pass/fail labels, is aligned with domain-expert labels, and is tuned on a dev split before final validation on a test split using F1 score. 01:43:43-01:45:38
