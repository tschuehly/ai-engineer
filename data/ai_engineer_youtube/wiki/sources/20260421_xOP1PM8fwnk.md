## Summary

Sander Dieleman explains the engineering shape of large-scale image and video diffusion systems: curated data can matter more than optimizer tweaks, learned latent autoencoders make media tensors tractable while preserving spatial structure, diffusion sampling builds samples through iterative denoising, guidance trades diversity for conditional quality, distillation reduces the number of sampling steps, and production media models need explicit control signals such as camera motion, masks, and depth.

## Notes

- Data curation is described as essential to high-quality large-scale models, and the speaker says time spent improving data can be a better investment than tuning the model or optimizer (02:55-03:39).
- Pixel-space diffusion becomes impractical as resolution and video duration grow; learned autoencoder latents reduce tensor size while keeping spatial topology useful for neural architectures (04:02-08:16).
- Diffusion models iteratively denoise rather than jumping directly to one clean image; a one-step prediction averages many possible clean images and becomes blurry, so sampling takes many smaller steps (12:24-15:18).
- The source presents diffusion as coarse-to-fine generation: low-frequency/global structure appears before fine detail, which fits image generation better than strict autoregressive ordering (19:12-19:59).
- Diffusion transformers differ from LLM transformers: media denoisers can use bidirectional attention and do not need causal masking (20:42-21:02).
- Guidance changes the sampling procedure to trade diversity for sample quality and conditional adherence; very high guidance can overconstrain the model and create artifacts (24:10-27:41, 32:50-33:25).
- Diffusion distillation targets latency by reducing the number of sampling steps rather than primarily shrinking the model (28:04-29:50).
- The source distinguishes text conditioning from richer controls such as camera motion, masks, and depth maps for image and video generation (30:04-31:08, 37:33-38:06).
