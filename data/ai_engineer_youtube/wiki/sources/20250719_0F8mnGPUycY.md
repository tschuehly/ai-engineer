## Summary

Sharif Shameem frames AI engineering as exploratory capability discovery: because even model builders do not fully know what frontier models can do, small shareable demos are a practical way to probe latent capabilities, reveal new product surfaces, and inspire others to continue the search.

## Notes

- Demos are presented as the best way to interact with and understand models whose capabilities cannot be fully predicted from specs or static analysis. (02:09-02:26)
- Early GPT-3 app-generation demos worked around a roughly 2,000-token context window by splitting output into multiple parallel prompts and joining the components later. (03:56-04:14)
- A browser-agent experiment failed when a raw shopping page was too large, so the demo used a custom HTML parser to reduce the page to its core essence before passing it to GPT-3. (04:54-05:18)
- The talk treats failed or partial demos as useful evidence: the shopping agent got distracted by terms of service, but the attempt still revealed surprising agency-like behavior in a base model. (05:15-05:29)
- A Gemini 2.5 Pro basketball-shot tracker is used as an example of a demo that makes video-first model experiences easier to imagine. (05:35-06:00)
- The speaker argues that even if model weights were frozen, many useful products remain undiscovered because current models already contain underexplored capabilities. (06:14-06:30)
- AI engineering is described as closer to searching for hidden capabilities than executing a fully known plan; demos are the tool for that search. (06:33-07:11)
- The Q&A reiterates that the motivation comes from frustration that powerful models exist but few people know what they can do, and from revisiting older human-computer interaction ideas with today's tools. (15:44-17:05)
