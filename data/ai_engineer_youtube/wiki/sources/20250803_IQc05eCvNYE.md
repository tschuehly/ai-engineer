## Summary

Peter Robicheaux argues that practical computer vision still has a larger capability gap than speech or language for real-world systems. The talk highlights saturated pattern-matching benchmarks, weak visual fidelity in VLMs, the limits of caption-contrastive pretraining, and Roboflow's RF-DETR/RF100-VL direction for evaluating domain-adaptable object detection.

## Notes

- Real-world systems that perceive motion usually need multiple frames per second and often need edge execution because centralized computation can add too much latency for decisions. (01:17-01:36)
- Robicheaux argues that ImageNet and COCO are mostly pattern-matching evals and do not require much visual intelligence, which weakens incentives to build smarter visual features. (01:50-02:13)
- VLM failures on clock-reading and bus-direction examples show models with abstract object concepts but poor precise visual feature perception. (03:24-04:44)
- MMVP pairs images that are close in CLIP space but far in DINOv2 space to expose the limits of caption-contrastive features for fine visual distinctions. (04:05-05:21)
- DINOv2's self-supervised features can discover object masks, segments, and analogous part structure, raising the question of how to align such visual fidelity with language-usable VLM features. (05:58-06:49)
- RF-DETR swaps an LW-DETR backbone for a DINOv2-pretrained backbone and reports stronger results, especially on RF100-VL domain-adaptability tests. (08:39-09:41)
- RF100-VL collects 100 curated object-detection datasets from Roboflow Universe and includes difficult domains such as aerial views, microscopes, and X-rays. (10:17-11:17)
- The few-shot RF100-VL track provides class names, annotator instructions, and 10 visual examples per class; the talk frames current VLM inability to combine all three as a major shortcoming. (15:33-17:10)
