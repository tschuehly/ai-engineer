## Summary

Benjamin Cowen, a forward-deployed ML engineer at Modal (a general-purpose serverless compute platform offering serverless functions and hardened code-execution sandboxes), argues that as AI products mature and specialize, fine-tuning becomes the destination rather than the exception — because the frontier labs want their models to win at everything while you want yours to win at your specific business logic. He frames a model spectrum from the frontier API (fast to build on, but no customization past prompt engineering, and "caveman mode" token-cutting won't scale at 100×/1000×) to full in-house training (total algorithm control but full responsibility for a cluster, infra engineers, and the whole stack), with a serverless middle ground that keeps algorithm control and fast iteration without managing the cluster. He offers three concrete signals it is time to fine-tune (API cost exceeding what customers pay you; eval plateau; latency/throughput needs a shared endpoint can't meet), a garbage-in-garbage-out prerequisite (collect data and mature evals first), and the readiness claim that if you've built an agent harness and are collecting eval data you already have what you need to start RL. The practical enablers: supervised fine-tuning (and RL) fit in ~300 lines of Python via open-source libraries, and serverless compute is useful for training, not just inference — fan-out hyperparameter search where "every minute on your cluster isn't sacred," and RL rollouts that are "massively embarrassingly parallel," with Modal customers scaling to 50,000–100,000 sandboxes just for RL rollout.
