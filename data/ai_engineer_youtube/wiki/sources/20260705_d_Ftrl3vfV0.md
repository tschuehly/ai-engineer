# Your AI Product Will Fail Unless You Can Explain It

## Summary

Veronica Hylak (Hey AI; AI product leader, ~10 years in tech, six in AI starting with autonomous military ships, and a YouTuber whose AI explainers have reached 8M+ views working with YC startups, AI tools, and safety orgs in SF) gives a six-minute communication playbook for technical founders: since founders now ship faster than ever but "you only have one shot to make your mark" that "lasts about the time it takes to ride an elevator," the fatal gap is being unable to answer "why does this matter to an average user?" The typical AI pitch ("we're building an agentic AI orchestration platform for enterprise knowledge retrieval… it's agents talking to other agents inside a multi-agent workflow") makes buyers glaze over. Her three-part fix turns complex AI products into stories people "instantly understand, remember, and want to buy": (1) **the wound** — open on the user's biggest pain point, not what you built; immerse them in their day-to-day and what they're tired of doing, then in ~20 seconds identify the wound, say "we fix that," and show how; (2) **make it click** — pass the 17-year-old test, tie the product to a viral story people already understand (the McDonald's AI drive-thru putting bacon on ice cream), ban words no one can picture, and give a mental image instead ("Devin, the AI software engineer"; "a smoke alarm for AI behavior") as a front door, saving the precise technical definition to close the sale; (3) **show the transformation** — stop describing and start proving value with an explicit before/after ("30 minutes digging through docs → one question, answer in 10 seconds with sources attached"). The close: 15 years ago the market might have found you; now "the products with the clearest stories are the ones that get funded, bought, and talked about," and "great tech that nobody understands dies quietly."

## Notes

- Framing cold open: "This is your buyer. And this is the typical AI pitch" — "we're building an agentic AI orchestration platform for enterprise knowledge retrieval… it's agents talking to other agents inside a multi-agent workflow… autonomous intelligence for your workflows." "This is why so many AI products are in trouble" (00:00-00:32).
- The stakes: founders ship faster than ever and "the tech is pretty epic, but you only have one shot to make your mark, and in this market that shot lasts about the time it takes to ride an elevator." The talk promises a "three-part fix for turning complex AI products into stories people instantly understand, remember, and want to buy" (00:32-00:53).
- Credibility: "made AI explainers that have hit 8 million views, and helped YC startups, safety orgs, and AI teams do exactly what I'm about to show you" (00:53-01:04).
- **Step 1 — the wound** = "the biggest pain point. What is hurting the customer? What is the exact moment your user wants to throw their laptop out a window?" One rule: "Do not start with what you built. The very first slide of your pitch should immerse people in their day-to-day… and show them what they are already tired of doing" (01:04-01:33).
- Bad vs good wound: "we built an agentic orchestration SecOps platform for enterprises" (a real Series B pitch) — "nobody feels anything when you say that." Good: "Security teams are exhausted managing dozens of disconnected tools. Alerts live in one system, tickets in another, vulnerabilities in another, and the real investigation is buried in Slack threads and random screenshots. We fix that by putting it all into one place." That "touches a wound because I understand the emotion now" (01:33-02:19).
- Wound format = ~20 seconds, three things: "Identify the wound, say we fix that, then show how." The product "starts claiming space in their mind because now they can see exactly how it impacts their day-to-day" (02:19-02:37).
- **Step 2 — make it click.** The test: "Could a 17-year-old understand what you do? If not, you're probably going to lose the room." One of the fastest fixes: "tie your product to a viral story people already understand" — the McDonald's AI drive-thru clips (bacon on ice cream): "you instantly get the problem, AI doing something stupid in public." So don't open with "we are an agent observability platform"; say "If McDonald's had used us, that drive-thru never would have made it to TikTok. We catch when AI agents go off script and give teams a chance to correct them before it becomes a PR nightmare" (02:37-03:28).
- Ban abstract words: "ban these words that no one can picture. Give me a mental image instead." Examples: "Devon [Devin], the AI software engineer" and "We're a smoke alarm for AI behavior" — "not perfect technical definitions, and that is fine… They are the front doors into the rest of the conversation. You can give the technical version later to close the sale. Just don't make it the first thing people have to understand" (03:28-04:05).
- **Step 3 — show the transformation.** "This is where you stop describing the product and start proving its value." Weak claims — "we improve code quality with AI" / "we increase productivity" — sound nice "but I still do not know what actually changes for me once your product enters my world. Show me what life looks like before and show me the after." Concrete: "Before, your support team spends 30 minutes digging through docs and tickets; with us, they ask one question and get the answer in 10 seconds with the sources attached." "If I cannot see what changed, I do not feel the story" (04:05-04:51).
- Method applied to the elevator: "Your team is wasting hours digging through Slack, email, and random Excel spreadsheets just to find one piece of information that you need. We fix that. We connect all that fragmented knowledge in one place so you can search it and get the answer you need in seconds. Same product, different story" (04:52-05:20).
- Close: "15 years ago, if you built something great, the market might have found you. That's not true anymore. The products with the clearest stories are the ones that get funded, bought, and talked about. Great tech that nobody understands dies quietly." Recap: "Identify the wound, make your product click, show the transformation" (05:20-05:52).
