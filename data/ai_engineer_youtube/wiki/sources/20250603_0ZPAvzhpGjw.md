## Summary

Rick Blalock frames an agent-native company as one where agents are embedded into product, operations, hiring, and culture rather than used as occasional assistants. The talk emphasizes daily review and dispatch of agent work, flatter human-plus-agent organizations, AI fluency as a hiring requirement, and onboarding that includes setting up the agent systems a new hire needs to do their job.

## Notes

- Agent-native companies put agents at the core of work rather than treating AI as a bolt-on feature or occasional document/chat aid; the test is whether removing agents materially breaks the company's throughput and product model (03:01-05:41).
- The described operating model shifts humans toward higher-level navigation while routine work and micro-decisions are offloaded to agents (05:41-06:04).
- Department-level agents should participate in product, support, and operations, with interfaces and handoffs across departments rather than isolated single-team experiments (06:29-07:04).
- The org shape becomes flatter and more network-like when coordination and execution can be handled by agent systems, but that assumes humans can orchestrate those systems effectively (07:59-09:04).
- A practical day starts by checking what agents completed, what needs approval, and what new work should be kicked off, then reviewing PRs, collateral, emails, or other artifacts later in the day (10:51-12:42).
- Hiring and onboarding change: AI fluency becomes a must-have, interviews should probe whether candidates can guide agents, and new hires may spend early onboarding time configuring the agents needed for their role (14:03-17:38).
