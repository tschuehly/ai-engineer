# Treat slop as a quality failure, not an AI provenance label

Summary: Slop is better treated as low-quality, inauthentic, or inaccurate output than as a synonym for AI-generated output. The useful engineering question is whether the work meets the quality bar, not whether AI participated.

Use when:
- Reviewing AI-generated artifacts without assuming provenance alone determines quality.
- Defining quality gates for code, content, releases, or agent outputs.

Details:
- swyx accepts the low-quality, inauthentic, or inaccurate part of the slop definition but rejects the idea that slop must be generated by AI. (02:15-02:31)
- The source argues that humans and AI can both be agents of slop, so teams need quality and taste checks that catch bad work regardless of origin. (02:31-03:43)
- The same tool, model, studio, trend, or startup idea can produce high-quality or sloppy output depending on execution, curation, and context. (02:40-04:24)
- For AI engineering, this means "AI-assisted" should not be used as either an automatic dismissal or an automatic excuse; the output still needs direct evaluation against accuracy, authenticity, security, and product quality.
