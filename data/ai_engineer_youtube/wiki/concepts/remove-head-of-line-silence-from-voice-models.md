# Remove Head-Of-Line Silence From Voice Models

Summary: Leading silence generated by a voice model is still latency, even if the audio is filtered before playback. Realtime voice systems should look for model- or data-induced head-of-line silence and remove it at generation time when it consumes the conversational budget.

Use when:
- Debugging voice-agent responses that feel slow despite acceptable network and model throughput metrics.
- Evaluating TTS fine-tunes, voice clones, or training data preparation for realtime use.

Details:
- Gabber labels Orpheus's generated leading silence as "head of line silence" and attributes it to fine-tuning data where voice-actor recordings included silence at the beginning. (07:43-08:24)
- A default Orpheus voice had about 600 ms of leading silence; filtering the silent audio after generation did not recover the compute time because the model still had to generate those tokens. (08:30-09:20)
- Gabber found they could fine-tune the silence away in a LoRA clone, reducing leading latency to roughly 100 ms P50 and effectively gaining about half a second back for the rest of the voice-agent pipeline. (09:26-10:52)
