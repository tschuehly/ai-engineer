# AI Coding Adoption Needs Hands-On Production Practice

Summary: AI coding adoption is a practice problem, not just a license rollout. Leaders, senior engineers, and skeptical practitioners build real fluency when they must ship bounded production work with the tools and reflect on the resulting capability gap.

Use when:
- Planning AI coding enablement for experienced engineers or leaders.
- Designing adoption programs that avoid performative usage metrics.
- Explaining why senior engineers may resist agent tools even when productivity gaps appear.

Details:
- Yegge says senior and staff engineers are often the group refusing coding-agent adoption, even as some organizations observe large productivity gaps between agent users and non-users. (03:03-03:40)
- Kim cites the goal of elevating others after an "aha moment," which turns the adoption problem into shared practice rather than tool availability. (17:06-17:22)
- Cisco Security's described program required 100 top leaders to vibe-code one feature into production in a quarter, then survey completion status and aha moments. (20:19-20:45)
- This differs from token-volume or seat-license adoption metrics: the useful signal is whether practitioners can deliver a real, reviewable production artifact and explain what changed in their workflow.
