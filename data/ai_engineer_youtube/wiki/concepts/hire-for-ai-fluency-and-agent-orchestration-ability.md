# Hire for AI fluency and agent orchestration ability

Summary: Agent-native organizations should evaluate whether candidates can guide, supervise, and improve agents in their domain. AI fluency becomes a role requirement when each employee is expected to manage agent counterparts for part of their work.

Use when:
- Updating hiring criteria for teams that expect employees to work through agents.
- Designing onboarding for roles that need role-specific agents, workflows, or agent-maintained context.

Details:
- The talk argues that agent-native companies need people who can directly guide agents to the task at hand and apply their domain expertise through those agents.
- New roles and titles may combine domain expertise with AI know-how, such as agent manager or AI customer lead.
- Interview processes should probe whether candidates use AI, can learn agent-guidance practices, and can bring in or develop teams that use agents effectively.
- Onboarding may need to include installing or configuring the agentic tools and systems that make a new hire productive, including time spent setting up agents for the role.

- **A concrete loop for the probe this page recommends, plus an axis it omits.** Debois treats the new titles as advertising rather than evidence — "AI product engineer, forward deployed engineer, agentic engineer, AI engineer. It doesn't mean anything… it's not a validation of the skills as such" — and describes a three-stage substitute: an exercise where the candidate is told to "go nuts on the AI," then a walkthrough asking "please explain me what happened. Why is this a good idea?" to test taste and engineering, then a read on whether the person shares their work. The ordering does the work, since the artifact is produced with maximum assistance and then defended, which is the situation the job actually puts people in. The third axis is the one this page does not have, and it follows from the shared-fix multiplier: an improvement that stays on one person's machine is not leverage. He also insists the three not be averaged into a seniority band, because the resulting development plans differ. The whole sequence is reported second-hand as "what I hear from most companies," with no outcomes.
