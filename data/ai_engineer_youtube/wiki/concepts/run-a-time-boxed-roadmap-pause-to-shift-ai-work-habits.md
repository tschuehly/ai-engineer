# Run a Time-Boxed Roadmap Pause to Shift AI Work Habits

Summary: To change AI-era work habits at scale in an established company, pause the roadmap for a fixed window and give small autonomous pairs the agency to ship something real; the constraint-plus-autonomy is what breaks entrenched process, but it only works on top of enablement scaffolding that already exists.

Use when:
- Planning an organization-wide AI adoption push in a large, established company rather than a 0→1 startup.
- Diagnosing why AI enablement produced course completions and license usage but little durable change in how people actually work.

Details:
- Automattic's "Radical Speed Month": pause roadmap work for 30 days, pair with a partner, and ship something real; AI was encouraged but not required. The first wave was about a third of the ~1,400-person company (≈500 people) starting ≈794 projects, run in staged waves. (00:15-00:35, 03:56-04:48)
- The intervention only worked on top of scaffolding that predated it: access to tools and courses, then a role-specific two-week immersive AI enablement course for every employee, encouragement to contribute in code, a systems/operations team providing security, processes, and documentation good enough that a non-engineer can spin up a working dev environment, and an internal knowledge MCP server ("Context") exposing decades of documented company knowledge to AI tools. (03:20-04:40)
- The reusable payoff is the agency, not the AI: the sprint let people "own the entire process," experiment, and find a personal workflow "without a lot of time in negotiations and handover," which "is very hard to do" in large organizations with 5–20-year-old software and thousands of people, versus small 0→1 teams where velocity looks entirely different. (12:44-13:20, 02:35-03:05)
- To implement even at team level: changing tools or process in a large org "requires shifting the human behavior behind them," so provide access to the new tools, find your enablers and champions, create space for experimentation, and give people agency to break out of their habits. (17:15-17:42)
