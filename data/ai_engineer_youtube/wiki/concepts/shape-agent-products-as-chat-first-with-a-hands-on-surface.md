# Shape Agent Products as Chat-First With a Hands-On Surface

Summary: For a product where the model is often smarter than the user on the average task, the empowering shape is two modalities: a single conversational entity you can ask for help with anything, anywhere, plus a collaborative hands-on UI you drop into when you want to inspect, steer, or shape the work yourself. Chat is the default ("talk and let them cook"); the hands-on surface preserves the human's feeling of mastery.

Use when:
- Choosing the primary interaction surface for a coding or general agent product.
- Deciding between a CLI, an IDE-first layout, and a chat-first app for agent collaboration.
- Designing how a user moves between "just delegate" and "get into the weeds."

Details:
- Motivation: models are good enough that "if you pick a medium length computer task and give me and the model the same amount of time… the model will do a better job than me for the average task," so the design question is what the product should *feel* like — and the goal is "squarely not to automate engineers" but to "maximally empower" them. (05:34-06:34)
- Two modalities, from sci-fi/superhero intuition that the simple ideas are "approximately right": chat ("some people think chat is dead. I think chat is underrated") and "some kind of hands-on experience." You want "a single entity that you can ask for help with anything, anywhere," and "a powerful collaborative UI that you can use when you want to inspect, steer, or shape things yourself." (06:36-07:11)
- Team analogy: most of the time "you're just talking about stuff, and your team is just doing stuff" — you don't want to watch over the shoulder for every unit of work; you "let them cook," then "every now and then… dig in all the way to the weeds." The product should let you "retain this feeling of mastery of the work," not make it hard to get to the details. (07:23-07:57)
- Surface critique — why chat-first, not CLI or IDE: "you can't really build that collaborative interface for any kind of work in a CLI. It's mostly chat." And "in an IDE, the order is wrong… you're starting with the code, but now it's time to transition to working with teammates where you chat first and you dig in when you need it." (08:59-09:11)
- Adoption evidence: people who said "I will never leave my terminal / Vim / Emacs" and internal skeptics ("people love the CLI, they love the IDE") ended up using the chat-first Codex app. (12:37-... paraphrased from 08:16-09:11 / earlier "fun story")
- The hands-on surface is not just code: a plain chat interface handles coding "and for anything else" — you can point at a specific thing and say "make this change," or make the change yourself. (07:59-08:30)
- Complements [Build Product Primitives Before Feature Surfaces](build-product-primitives-before-feature-surfaces.md): both reject the simple-vs-capable tradeoff — a streamlined default that can still open up to deep, direct control.
