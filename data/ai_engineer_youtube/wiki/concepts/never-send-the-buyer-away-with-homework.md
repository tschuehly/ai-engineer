# Never Send the Buyer Away With Homework

Summary: Any task you hand to a buyer leaves your control and returns on their schedule, if at all. Do the integration, the setup, and the internal legwork yourself — in their office if necessary — because the cost of a handoff is not the buyer's effort, it is that the cycle's clock stops and you cannot see it.

Use when:
- An evaluation requires the customer to configure something, gather data, or get an internal answer.
- A deal has gone quiet after a step that ended with "let us know when you've done X."
- Designing the onboarding or integration path for a product sold into an organization.
- Choosing between a self-service path that the buyer completes alone and a guided one you run.

Details:
- **The rule.** "Make it easy on your buyers. Don't give them homework. Don't make them go and fill out a form or, if you can avoid it, integrate something without you hand-holding them through that. Never say, 'Hey, go do these five things and then come back to me.' Or 'Go ask this person about this and come back to me.' You do as much of the work for them. You hand-hold them through that." ([Rosenthal](../sources/20260826_wdTRsfw0KG0.md), 05:52-06:15)
- **The stated cost is control and elapsed time, not friction.** "Whatever you do, don't send your buyer away to do something and wait for them to come back because you lose total control of the sales cycle and it makes everything take longer." A handed-off task is unobservable: you cannot tell a buyer who is blocked from one who has deprioritized you, so the only signal is silence. (06:15-06:31)
- **The remedies are deliberately expensive and synchronous.** "Maybe that means you have to set up hackathons and come in their office and do it with them in person. Maybe that means you have to set up some sort of Zoom session and do it live with them." The point of the live session is that it converts an open-ended task into a bounded appointment on a calendar — the work happens at a time you can both see. (06:15-06:31)
- **The general form.** "Remove as much friction as you can in the buying process. Fewer options is better on pricing. Make the paperwork easy and fast. Make the approval flow as manageable as possible." Note that "fewer options is better on pricing" is a friction claim about the *decision*, not the price level — a separate lever from the one in [Price Under the Department Threshold So Adoption Can Spread](price-under-the-department-threshold-so-adoption-can-spread.md). (06:31-06:48)
- **This is the human-buyer statement of the agent-buyer rule.** [Self-Serve Onboarding Is a Precondition for Agent Recommendation](self-serve-onboarding-is-a-precondition-for-agent-recommendation.md) argues that an agent will not recommend a tool whose adoption requires demos and sales emails, because the recommendation would come with an errand attached. Both pages say the same thing about handed-off work, and they resolve in opposite directions: for the agent you remove the human from the path, and for the enterprise buyer you insert yourself into it. The shared invariant is that *the work does not go to the counterparty* — see also [The Install Handoff Is Now a Prompt](the-install-handoff-is-now-a-prompt.md), where the same principle produces a pasted prompt instead of a laptop handoff.
- **The tension with pilot avoidance, and how it resolves.** The same talk says to avoid handing over product access ([Treat a Pilot as a Second Sales Process You Run for Free](treat-a-pilot-as-a-second-sales-process-you-run-for-free.md)) and to do the buyer's work for them. These are consistent: both keep the activity on your side of the table. The pilot rule says do not hand over the *product*; this rule says do not hand over the *task*. The substitute both point to is the same — you run it, over Zoom, against their data.
- **The limit worth naming.** Doing the buyer's work does not scale, which is why this rule belongs with a self-serve motion rather than in place of one: the automated path absorbs everyone who does not need hand-holding, and hand-holding is spent where the machine stopped. It also has a legitimate ceiling — some homework is the buyer's own governance (security review, procurement, legal), and the answer there is to automate your side of it rather than to do theirs. See [Automate the Security Review Path Because Deals Stall There](automate-the-security-review-path-because-deals-stall-there.md).
