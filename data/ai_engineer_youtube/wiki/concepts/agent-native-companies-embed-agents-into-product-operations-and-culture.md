# Agent-native companies embed agents into product, operations, and culture

Summary: An agent-native company treats agents as core operating primitives, not as occasional productivity tools. A useful test is whether removing agents would materially degrade throughput, product capability, and day-to-day operating rhythm.

Use when:
- Distinguishing AI-enhanced organizations from agent-native operating models.
- Designing company workflows where agents participate across product, operations, support, and culture.

Details:
- Agent-native means building the company around agents at the foundation of product, operations, and culture rather than adding AI as a side feature.
- AI-enhanced work can still function when AI is removed; agent-native work loses a core execution layer when agents disappear.
- In the described model, routine tasks and micro-decisions move to agents while humans focus on higher-level navigation and company or product success.
- Department-level agents should have interfaces, handoffs, and cross-department integrations instead of staying confined to one team or feature.
- **The test applied to a revenue org rather than to engineering.** At Exa the group that would fail hardest without agents is go-to-market, not engineering: a dozen shared Slack agents over internal data, account research done by asking agents, demos built with them, and a forward-deployed engineering group whose job includes "maintenance and feature building of these AI systems" alongside running deals. The reported outcome is qualitative — "our go-to-market team is very lean, but very productive" — so this is evidence of an operating model, not of its returns. ([Wang](../sources/20260826_6pbQgnJ9Voc.md), 07:34-08:20, 10:08-10:12, 15:28-15:56)
