# Reject Skeuomorphic Dev Tooling and Legacy Workflow Constraints

Summary: Many developer tools and conventions (terminal-centric workflows, Git defaults, editor religion, language identity, sunk-cost attachment to code) are skeuomorphic holdovers kept for familiarity rather than utility; in the AI era, question each one by asking whether you do it because it's right or just because it's always been done that way.

Use when:
- Deciding whether a team workflow, tool, or convention still earns its place as agents change how software is built.
- Noticing resistance to a more useful AI-native interface because it feels unfamiliar.
- Removing friction (deleting code, resetting an approach) that human pride or sunk cost is protecting.

Details:
- Skeuomorphism analogy: iOS 6→7 dropped skin-of-a-real-object textures once Apple no longer had to *convince* users a phone could replace a physical compass or book; the flatter iOS 7 compass is objectively more useful (clear target vs current heading, big numeric readout) even though users initially disliked it. "We're currently in our skeuomorphic phase as software developers." 04:09-06:41
- The terminal case: "We're pretending our terminals are the ultimate interface when they're not even good interfaces... Natural language has no place in a terminal, but we pretend it does because the terminal's familiar." Said as someone who "loves their terminal deeply" — the point is familiarity, not utility. 06:23-06:41
- Arbitrary conventions to interrogate: "why can't we commit our environment files?" — a Git default built for one specific thing that "took over our brains"; qualifying yourself by which languages you know (a "real developer" gatekeeping habit that "just doesn't matter that much anymore"). 07:00-08:22
- Sunk-cost and pride: fear of deleting code, and "guilt merging" a PR that isn't the right solution because a teammate spent a week on it. Agents lower this cost — "you don't have to feel bad when you shut down their work." 08:33-09:18
- The reusable test: "Is this how we do things cuz it's right, or is this how we do things cuz it's just how we've always done it?" Applies to tools, systems, frameworks, and languages we treat as identity. 07:33-08:22
- This mindset is the enabler for rescoping ambition and thinking wider: legacy constraints are what keep builders from attempting the newly-viable larger projects.
