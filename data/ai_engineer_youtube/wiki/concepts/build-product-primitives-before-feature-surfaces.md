# Build Product Primitives Before Feature Surfaces

Summary: Durable retention comes from building capable underlying primitives, then assembling simple default experiences from them. This lets beginners start cleanly while power users, teams, and enterprises can access deeper capability without outgrowing the product.

Use when:
- Designing AI tools or developer products that must be simple for first use but deep enough for expert workflows.
- Deciding whether to add an isolated feature or invest in lower-level product primitives.

Details:
- Retention is framed around power users, teams, and enterprises that eventually need configurability, advanced features, self-hosting, or data-control options while the first experience must remain streamlined. (10:45-12:38)
- Raad rejects the simple-versus-capable tradeoff as a misconception; products can be both if teams do more upfront design work. (12:41-13:07)
- Instead of building one feature surface directly, teams should build broad primitive layers, then assemble the common simple experience from those primitives. (13:09-13:35)
- As users mature, the product can expose more direct access to the underlying primitives so they can flex the product instead of leaving for a more capable alternative. (13:37-14:10)
- AI does not remove the need to understand the problem space deeply enough to know which primitives should exist. (14:12-15:07)
