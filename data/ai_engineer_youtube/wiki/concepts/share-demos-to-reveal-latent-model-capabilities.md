# Share Demos To Reveal Latent Model Capabilities

Summary: AI demos are capability probes, not only marketing artifacts. A small runnable demo can expose a model behavior that documentation, benchmarks, or the model lab's own expectations did not make obvious.

Use when:
- Exploring what a new model family can do before committing to a product surface.
- Trying to communicate a future-facing AI product idea without waiting for a complete production system.

Details:
- Shameem argues that models cannot be fully understood without interacting with them, and that interesting demos are a practical way to explore what is possible. (02:09-02:26)
- Early GPT-3 code and browser demos showed that language models could behave as software-building and web-browsing reasoning engines, even when the interface was rudimentary and the model was only a base completion model. (03:16-05:29, 12:48-14:01)
- A Gemini 2.5 Pro basketball-shot tracker made video-first model experiences concrete by using uploaded gameplay video to provide coaching-style feedback, expanding the perceived design space beyond code or screen-watching helpers. (05:35-06:00)
- The talk warns that obvious model labels, such as "coding model" or "reasoning model," can understate latent capabilities; good demos reveal possibilities through exploration and play. (09:02-09:30)
- Sharing demos moves the field forward because it lets other builders see a capability and branch from it, even if the original demo is incomplete or fragile. (10:57-11:14)
