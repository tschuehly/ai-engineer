# Co-Discover Emergent Agent Behavior With Customers

Summary: Early AI-agent products often cannot promise deterministic knowledge of every possible behavior, so customer trust comes from honest co-discovery, fast feedback, and shared evaluation of what the agent actually does.

Use when:
- Customers ask whether an agent can handle an undocumented workflow.
- A team is selling or deploying an AI-native product whose behavior depends on model capability, open-ended user inputs, and tool composition.

Details:
- The Google Doc comment example shows product discovery happening after a customer asks about an undocumented behavior, not only during internal roadmap planning. (01:57-02:46)
- The talk argues that product work becomes discovering functionality: teams build composable blocks, let customers or agents try them, and observe emergent behavior that could not be fully specified from a blank PRD. (06:42-07:34)
- Runnable prototypes are a product-management tool because teams can feel whether conversational behavior is useful or irritating, such as when too many clarifying questions make an agent annoying despite sounding reasonable in the spec. (10:46-12:57)
- Customer trust should be framed around inventing the workflow together when the product is probabilistic; customers who need deterministic certainty may be a poor fit for early agent behavior. (16:02-18:13)
