# Co-Design In Person Because Remote Channels Filter the Requirements

Summary: Zoom calls and support tickets only carry requirements someone already knew how to state as a request. The work that would tell you what to build — the twenty-year-old workaround, the papercut nobody logs — surfaces when you are physically present, which is why deployment into a non-technical organization is a co-design problem rather than a support problem.

Use when:
- A product is technically correct in a customer's environment and still unused, and the ticket queue is empty.
- Deciding whether "we do discovery calls" is sufficient requirements-gathering for a vertical AI deployment.
- Choosing between building a new surface and embedding into the tool the user already has open.
- Budgeting deployment effort for AI into an industry whose staff are not software people.

Details:
- The analogy and the transfer: Jensen Huang "dominated the market in his words with extreme hardware software co-design. Designing the chips and the software together as one system. We look at this through the lens of extreme software service co-design. How do you co-design our products with the people and the processes at our businesses? And I'd argue this is only possible from being within under the same roof." ([Shenoy](../sources/20260828_B0fjR3yaZFU.md), 15:32-16:08)
- **The channel claim, stated as an impossibility rather than a preference**: "You cannot co-design software with the services business over Zoom or over a support ticket. You have to be there. You have to be in person." The mechanism is a filter — a ticket is written by someone who already framed their problem as a feature request, so the channel selects for articulated needs and discards the rest. (17:02-17:14)
- Two ways to meet people, and they are different moves. **Metaphorically**: "bringing products to their systems so that the energy required for enablement is kept low" — "a product that's natively embedded into Excel or into their ERP system, maybe their 3D design software, or maybe even their Microsoft products like Outlook, Gmail." **Physically**: "Get on a plane, show up, say hi, learn what people actually do." (16:08-16:38)
- The physical list is specific enough to be a method rather than a sentiment: a lunch and learn at one of the companies; going to their industry conference and running a cotton-candy stand; going mountain biking and asking about "all the difficulties that they have with their actual day-to-day jobs"; showing up one-on-one, "or sometimes even two-on-one," to show people how to use the tools and learn from the feedback. The common property is that none of these is a meeting the customer prepared for. (16:38-17:02)
- The "energy required for enablement" framing is the reusable design rule underneath the surface choice: every new window, login, or context switch is activation energy charged to a user who has not yet been convinced. Embedding into the ERP or Excel is not a distribution tactic, it is a reduction in the price of the first use.
- The audience is named as the point: "this is what the rest of the world really looks like. It's not like the folks in this room or in San Francisco." The talk is addressed to an AI-engineering conference and the argument is that its intuitions about how software reaches users do not hold for a 200-person property management firm. Closing line: "In order to get AI diffusion to work, you have to touch some grass." (17:02-17:22)
- What this argues against. It is an explicit rejection of the model in which a capable enough product diffuses on its own — the same rejection made by the electricity analogy that opens the talk, where the technology existed in the 1880s and Ford's electrified assembly line arrives in 1924 because "you have to rip out the existing motors and equipment… You have to go and train everybody." (01:36-02:28)
- Limits. This is one operating model argued from the inside, with no comparison against a remote-first or vendor-side deployment, and the conclusion that co-design is "only possible from being within under the same roof" is also the justification for the speaker's company owning the businesses it deploys into. Nothing here is measured. ([Provenance and Limits](../sources/20260828_B0fjR3yaZFU.md))
