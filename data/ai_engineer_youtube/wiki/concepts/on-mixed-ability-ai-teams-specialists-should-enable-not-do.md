# On Mixed-Ability AI Teams, Specialists Should Enable Not Do

Summary: When AI lets everyone do a little work outside their domain, the highest-leverage move for a strong specialist on a mixed-ability team is to enable and teach teammates rather than to do more of their own specialty; engineers in particular shift from builders to enablers as more non-engineering roles start contributing in code.

Use when:
- Staffing a mixed team (designers, engineers, PMs, operators) where all members are expected to contribute in code with AI.
- Deciding where a strong specialist should spend time on an AI-assisted team, or diagnosing why individual output is high but the team is not leveling up.

Details:
- On a four-person, two-hour build (two designers weak in code, one engineer, one product lead), the engineer's biggest contribution was setting the others up for success — creating the GitHub project, teaching basic Git commands, and explaining versioning — so everyone could commit code; the impact of enabling others exceeded the impact of doing more engineering herself. (06:20-09:10)
- The learning gap is real even for experienced people: the designer-speaker "thought she understood Git but didn't" and needed someone to show the right way to set up a project; a low-risk throwaway project (unrelated to real products, "no risks involved") kept the focus on learning. (07:30-08:30)
- The durable rule: as more non-engineering roles start working in code, "engineers will need to become enablers and teachers," and the same holds for any strong specialty on a mixed team — help others level up, "because now with AI, we can all do a little bit of the work that's outside of our domain." (09:10-09:40, 16:08-16:24)
