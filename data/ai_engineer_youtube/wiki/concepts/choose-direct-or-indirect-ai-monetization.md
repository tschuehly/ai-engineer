# Choose Direct or Indirect AI Monetization

Summary: An AI feature does not always need a separate price. Teams should choose whether to sell it directly, package it as an add-on, bundle it into tiers, or use it to drive downstream revenue based on who receives value and what behavior the feature should encourage.

Use when:
- Deciding whether a new AI capability should be a standalone product, paid add-on, bundled tier feature, or free adoption driver.
- Evaluating whether direct charging would reduce the customer behavior the AI feature is meant to create.

Details:
- Direct monetization fits when the AI capability is separable and valuable to a subset of the audience, such as a paid add-on above a base product seat.
- Indirect monetization fits when the AI feature is meant to increase adoption, upsell into higher tiers, or drive a downstream transaction rather than create a separate billable unit.
- Bundling can be a deliberate monetization choice: a product can include AI in business or enterprise tiers while raising those tiers to reflect the added value.
- Free AI features can still be economically rational when they absorb inference cost but increase higher-value downstream behavior, such as bookings or purchases. (05:12-05:53)
- **The choice narrows when the agent is the product rather than a feature of it.** This page's options assume a human audience whose behaviour you are shaping. Garvin's first role removes that latitude: "if the agent can be the product and run up a token bill it's important for you to be able to meter on that." An agent that runs unattended can consume without bound, so indirect monetization stops being a pricing preference and becomes an uncapped liability. Bundling and adoption-driver framings still apply above the metered floor — but the floor has to exist. ([Garvin](../sources/20260828_mJqwmmOx4WA.md), 09:53-10:07)
