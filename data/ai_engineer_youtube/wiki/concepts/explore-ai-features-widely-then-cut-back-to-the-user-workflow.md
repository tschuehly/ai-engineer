# Explore AI Features Widely Then Cut Back to the User Workflow

Summary: Cheap AI-assisted implementation can justify broad feature exploration, but only if the team is willing to remove what user testing does not validate. The durable product should collapse back to the simplest workflow for the selected user group.

Use when:
- AI coding tools make it cheap to prototype many product variants.
- A product surface has accumulated multiple AI buttons, templates, chats, or hidden automations before one user workflow is clear.

Details:
- In the Minute transcription product, the team had many plausible AI features and used AI coding assistants to try variants quickly with different groups of government users. 08:55-09:24
- The wide exploration only worked because the team then stripped back to what worked; Lowe notes that AI-generated features can carry less sentimental attachment, making deletion easier. 09:27-09:42
- The exploratory UI included multiple templates, optional agenda input, AI edit, AI chat, speaker-name prediction, and transcript citations, but testing showed users found the surface overwhelming and often did not use the features. 09:50-10:43
- Focusing on probation-service users let the team remove template picking, agenda input, and confusing overlaps between AI edit and AI chat, producing a simpler Justice Transcribe workflow. 10:47-11:47
- The lesson is not "ship every AI feature"; it is to experiment hard with real users, then cut back and streamline around demonstrated workflow value. 11:47-12:04
