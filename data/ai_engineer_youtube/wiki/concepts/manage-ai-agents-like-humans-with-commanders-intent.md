# Manage AI Agents Like Humans With Commander's Intent

Summary: The most practical way to get trustworthy work from AI agents is to reuse the patterns you already use to manage people — onboard them on how your business works, get a second opinion before believing anything, and prompt with commander's intent (tell the agent *why*, not just *how*). Beware that agents trained on human material tend to micromanage themselves, so pull them back to the intent.

Use when:
- Deciding how to structure prompting, review, and onboarding for an agent workflow and looking for a durable default.
- An agent follows literal instructions but produces low-quality or off-target work.
- You catch an agent over-specifying or "improving itself" into micromanagement.

Details:
- Thesis: establishing trust with AI "actually isn't new… we already know a lot about how to do this for people," so "when in doubt, manage your agents like other humans." If you'd help a team of humans do something a certain way, do that with your agents too. (08:30-08:48)
- Commander's intent (the one practical tip): a doctrine from the armed forces — "tell your agents *why* you want them to do something, and they will do it a lot better." It works on humans too, because "people don't generally like being micromanaged." (08:48-09:12)
- Self-micromanagement caveat: agents are trained on human material and "like to micromanage themselves, and that's not usually what you want." "Don't tell Claude to improve itself, you'll get micromanagement" — "pull it back and say, remember, we're talking about the why." (09:12-09:28)
- The framing generalizes the talk's other patterns as human-management moves: a librarian is onboarding a new hire on the business; a jury-and-judge is asking for a second opinion before believing a subjective call; agent tiers is not staffing important work with someone too junior for it.
- Corollary — scaffold before YOLO: don't expect an agent to one-shot a complex deliverable; give it maintained context first (product-capability references, personas, positioning) the way you'd brief a person, then turn it loose. (09:28-11:30)
