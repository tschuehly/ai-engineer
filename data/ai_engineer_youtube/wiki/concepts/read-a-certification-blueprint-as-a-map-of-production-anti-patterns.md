# Read a Certification Blueprint as a Map of Production Anti-Patterns

Summary: A model vendor's certification blueprint is a support-ticket distribution in disguise. Its weighted domains and its production scenarios say where the vendor watches customers fail, so the blueprint is worth reading as an engineering field guide whether or not you ever sit the exam — and the fastest way to read it is to ask what each scenario's anti-pattern is, since the wrong answer is what the scenario exists to catch.

Use when:
- Deciding what to teach a team adopting an agent platform, and wanting a priority order someone else already paid to discover.
- Auditing your own agent architecture against the failure modes a vendor considers common enough to test.
- Evaluating whether a certification is worth the time, separately from whether its curriculum is.

Details:
- The premise for reading it at all: "I think if you're interested in a career in agentic AI, then certainly take a look at least what the exam is about, because I feel that Anthropic knows how people are using their system and what the issues are going to be." The blueprint is treated as vendor telemetry made public. ([Coyle](../sources/20260808_Z-c11pV_uvU.md), 01:04-01:19)
- **The reading method is anti-pattern first.** Coyle walks each scenario backwards, leading with what not to do, on the analogy of the early-1990s design patterns movement, which arrived with a catalog of the moves that quietly ruin you: "we have patterns for agents, but there's also anti-patterns. And I think anti-patterns are a key to understanding what you should not do because understanding what you should not do is the key to leading you to what you should do." The exam-taking version is the same: "one of the big things is what not to do and that often can be the key to getting these questions right." ([Coyle](../sources/20260808_Z-c11pV_uvU.md), 02:18-02:44, 04:29-04:47)
- **The weighted domains, as reported.** Agentic architecture 27%; configuring the Claude Code system and workflow 20%; prompt engineering and structuring output, "using JSON all over the place"; tool design and Model Context Protocol integration; and context management and reliability. Two percentages are given in the captions and the remaining three are not, so the weighting is partial. Read as a priority list, the top two say that most of what goes wrong is architecture and harness configuration rather than prompting. ([Coyle](../sources/20260808_Z-c11pV_uvU.md), 03:24-04:13)
- **The scenario list is the more useful artifact.** Six production scenarios exist and the exam "will randomly choose four and all the questions will be centered around the four." Named in the talk: a customer support resolution agent (agentic loops, control, and [stop reasons](read-the-stop-reason-before-you-read-the-answer.md)); code generation with Claude; a multi-agent research system ("How do you distribute your agents? Hub and spoke. Who's the orchestrator? How much information should they know?"); developer productivity with code (subtask isolation — "keep your tasks in their little universes"); Claude Code for continuous integration; and structured data extraction. ([Coyle](../sources/20260808_Z-c11pV_uvU.md), 04:13-06:04)
- The anti-patterns the talk extracts from those scenarios are each recorded separately in this wiki, and they are ordinary engineering mistakes rather than model-specific trivia: using the response without reading the stop reason ([Read the Stop Reason Before You Read the Answer](read-the-stop-reason-before-you-read-the-answer.md)); loading one agent with every tool instead of specializing ([Task-Tuned Tool Sets Beat Generic Integration Surfaces for Core Coding Loops](task-tuned-tool-sets-beat-generic-integration-surfaces-for-core-coding-loops.md)); letting subtask output and reasoning spill into the main thread ([Bound Context Twice](bound-context-twice-fork-the-subtask-then-compact-on-a-token-threshold.md)); showing a critic the reasoning that produced the claim ([Withhold the Producer's Reasoning From the Critic](withhold-the-producers-reasoning-from-the-critic.md)); and leaving interactive permission prompts in a pipeline ([Permission-Stable Command Wrappers Reduce Approval Friction](permission-stable-command-wrappers-reduce-approval-friction.md)).
- Exam mechanics, for the separate decision of whether to sit it: released in March, scenario-based, timed, and proctored; available to companies in the Anthropic ecosystem, with individuals paying $99 and eligible once every six months; multiple choice in form, but "based on realistic constraints and realistic scenarios" rather than recall. ([Coyle](../sources/20260808_Z-c11pV_uvU.md), 02:46-03:24)
- **What this page is worth and what it is not.** The evidence is one instructor's reading of a published blueprint, not a vendor statement about how the domains were weighted or a study relating them to real incident data; the inference that the weights encode support volume is plausible and unverified. Two of the six scenarios are named but never opened — Claude Code for CI gets a single anti-pattern and structured data extraction was promised as a section and cut when the talk ran out of time. A vendor blueprint also has a second job that pulls against this reading: it certifies competence in *that vendor's* product surface, so its silences are as much about product scope as about what does not break. ([Coyle](../sources/20260808_Z-c11pV_uvU.md), 05:58-06:04, 19:15-19:22)
