# Turn Unfiled Conversation Into Concrete Prototypes

Summary: Connecting an agent to your email, meeting notes, and issue tracker solves reading, not selection — a human still has to notice something worth building and tell the agent which item it is. The move that removes that carrier is to let the agent watch the raw conversation stream, decide for itself what is new work, and produce a concrete prototype nobody asked for, on the understanding that the output is a thing to react to rather than a change to ship.

Use when:
- An agent already has MCP access to Slack, Notion, email, or a bug tracker and still only works on what a person hands it.
- Deciding whether customer calls, onboarding sessions, and team meetings should produce notes or should produce code.
- Judging what the acceptable output quality is for work that no one requested.

Details:
- **The gap this closes, named precisely.** The signals already exist and are already reachable: "all that stuff already exists. It's in all those different systems. People hook them together with MCPs. So now your coding agent can check the email or check Notion or whatever it might be. But… how does it know what to work on? It's still kind of stuck everywhere. And so some humans are involved in taking stuff from one place and telling it solve email number 48 or ticket number 6,000. But that's still a lot of coordination." Retrieval was the solved half; triage was the human residue. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 06:06-06:33)
- **The signal set is deliberately broad and mostly unstructured.** "A Slack conversation… a meeting you have with a customer, an onboarding call, a sales call… an internal team meeting… something from Sentry or bug tracker, a bug report from a customer, an email, feature request." The interesting members are the ones with no ticket behind them: a spoken remark in a meeting has no identifier for a human to hand over. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 05:51-06:06)
- **The mechanism, and the demonstration.** A meeting bot is invited "to Meet or Zoom or Teams," listens, and creates work; the demo instance sat in a Google Meet at their expo booth for four hours. A passer-by said they wanted coding agents to have "clear criteria to evaluate whether it did a good job on the work before it tells me that it's done." Nobody filed anything: "they had that idea, the bot just picked up on it. None of us did anything manually. It created this ticket and started working on it." Asked afterwards to "take a screenshot of what you did," it returned an image of two new acceptance-criteria fields added to the product's own ticket form. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 06:41-08:03)
- **The deduplication rule is what makes it survivable.** "If it finds existing work, it'll link to it. So it's not going to just create new work if it's something already working on." Without this, a system that opens a ticket per idea heard in conversation produces the failure another team reported directly — agents "not wired correctly" filing hundreds of issues in a couple of weeks ([wire issue-filing authority before giving agents a tracker](wire-issue-filing-authority-before-giving-agents-a-tracker.md)). Linking to prior work is the cheapest form of the signal-grouping step that machine-signal pipelines build explicitly. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 07:06-07:15)
- **The output contract is "concrete thing to react to," not "shippable change."** "Am I going to ship this one exactly how it is? Like, no, probably not. But it's a new idea, it's concrete, I can play with it, I can go and actually like use the live preview and like see if this improves performance." That bound is what makes an unrequested prototype cheap to reject; a system judged on merge rate would have to be much more conservative about what it acts on. It also inherits [prototype gravity](unimplemented-plans-signal-a-working-decision-layer.md) as its main risk — a running prototype exerts pull toward shipping simply by existing, and this system manufactures them in bulk. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 08:03-08:14)
- **The reported yield, with its missing denominator.** "Every time we have an onboarding or customer call or team meeting, we almost always have dozens of new ideas that are prototyped, but more importantly, at least a few shippable PRs with a very minimal intervention." No definition of shippable, no merge count, no measure of how much of the batch was noise, and no accounting of the review load that dozens of unrequested prototypes per meeting create. Read against [AI output speed can overwhelm review capacity](ai-output-speed-can-overwhelm-review-capacity.md), the unpriced term is obvious. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 08:24-08:42)
- **How this differs from the observability-to-PR pattern.** Pipelines that convert metrics, errors, session replays, and Slack messages into pull requests ([observability-to-PR agents](observability-to-pr-agents-turn-incidents-into-reviewable-fixes.md)) start from machine-emitted signals that already have structure, a timestamp, and an implicit definition of "something is wrong." Here the input is human speech about what might be good, with no wrongness to detect and no schema to normalize into — so the hard step moves from grouping and weighting to deciding that an offhand sentence was a product idea at all. The two are complementary inputs to the same "signal becomes a reviewable diff" shape.
- **The same "nobody filed it" gap exists in operations, and there it has a different shape.** Resolve AI describes work that *is* named and understood — watching a deploy land healthy, a morning system digest, "hey that P99 drift kind of came back. Is somebody looking at that or not?", a quarterly capacity report — and still has no trigger, because "on-call you've got a page that goes off… Incidents you create a bridge… but there's just a long tail of other things that we are accountable for" with no ceremony attached. This page's problem is that nobody *noticed* the work; that one's is that everybody knows about the work and nothing fires for it. The remedies differ accordingly: inference from unstructured speech here, a schedule or event subscription there. See [Give Unowned Operational Work a Trigger](give-unowned-operational-work-a-trigger.md). ([Justin Smith](../sources/20260809_vSx5IULvBns.md), 09:04-10:16)
- **Who it changes the job for.** The same effect the wiki records from support teams — proximity to fresh customer context being the scarce input, not engineering capacity ([support-led coding agents exploit fresh customer context](support-led-coding-agents-exploit-fresh-customer-context.md)) — arrives here without anyone changing role, because the conversation itself is the trigger. The precondition is the same one everything else in this source rests on: [environment isolation](environment-isolation-is-what-lets-non-engineers-trigger-real-work.md), so that work triggered from a meeting has somewhere to run.
