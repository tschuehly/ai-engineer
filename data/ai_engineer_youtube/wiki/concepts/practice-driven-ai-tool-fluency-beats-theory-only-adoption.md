# Practice-Driven AI Tool Fluency Beats Theory-Only Adoption

Summary: Engineers get better at AI tools through repeated hands-on workflow experiments and willingness to revise habits; model theory alone does not teach when and how to use agents productively.

Use when:
- Planning AI enablement for engineering teams.
- Diagnosing why technically strong engineers underuse or misuse coding agents.

Details:
- The transcript says experienced engineers initially held back when AI tools were only mildly useful on existing codebases, especially when they did not help with real refactors, bugs, or local workflows. (05:57-06:49)
- Orosz cites Simon Willison's observation that even after years of use there was no stable manual; practitioners kept changing workflows while discovering what worked. (11:13-11:27)
- Two adoption lessons are separated: getting good takes time and repeated use, and understanding model internals or attention mechanisms does not automatically create practical tool fluency. (11:27-12:09)
- Teams getting more value are described as low-ego, open to learning, and willing to leave priors behind without abandoning engineering experience. (12:23-12:41)
