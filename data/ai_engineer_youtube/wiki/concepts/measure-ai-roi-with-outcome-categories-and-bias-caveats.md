# Measure AI ROI with outcome categories and bias caveats

Summary: AI ROI measurement should separate kinds of impact instead of collapsing everything into generic productivity. Self-reported AI adoption data can be useful early signal, but it must carry selection-bias caveats and should not be treated as a controlled field study.

Use when:
- Designing an enterprise AI impact survey or dashboard.
- Interpreting self-reported AI ROI from users, teams, or leadership cohorts.

Details:
- A practical ROI taxonomy can include time savings, increased output, quality improvement, new capabilities, improved decision-making, cost savings, increased revenue, and risk reduction. (09:05-09:30)
- The survey was explicitly caveated as coming from a self-selected, AI-interested audience that voluntarily reported use cases, so its ROI rates should be treated as directional evidence rather than representative population measurement. (09:34-09:48)
- Reported ROI should distinguish current impact from expectation: the source reports modest and high current ROI, but also notes high optimism that ROI will grow over the next year. (09:34-10:36)
- Negative ROI in this framing can mean costs have exceeded gains so far, not necessarily that the program is judged a failure. (10:01-10:11)
