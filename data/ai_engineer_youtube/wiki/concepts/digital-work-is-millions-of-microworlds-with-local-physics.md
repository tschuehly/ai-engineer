# Digital Work Is Millions of Microworlds With Local Physics

Summary: Enterprise and personal digital work is not one world but millions of small ones — every domain, profession, and company different, and the same software configured differently in each — each with its own structures, constraints, affordances, and dynamics. That heterogeneity is the argument that no single static model can cover the space, and that competence has to be acquired per deployment rather than shipped in the weights.

Use when:
- Someone proposes that the next model release will make a per-customer integration layer unnecessary.
- Explaining why an agent that works in your environment fails in a customer's without any change in task difficulty.
- Deciding whether to invest in a per-deployment learning mechanism versus waiting for a better base model.

Details:
- The claim: "modern society is really not just one unified world. It's millions of these micro worlds. Like every domain, every profession is different, every company is different. Even if you're using the same software, every company configure it differently. So it's extremely idiosyncratic, especially in the digital world." ([NeoCognition](../sources/20260812_I6aiEf3aEFQ.md), 05:25-05:56)
- The phrase that makes it operational: each microworld "has its unique local physics, like different structures, constraints, affordances, and dynamics that you have to learn." Those four terms are a usable inventory of what an agent has to acquire before it can act well in a new environment — not facts about the domain, but the rules of that particular instance of it. ([NeoCognition](../sources/20260812_I6aiEf3aEFQ.md), 05:52-06:02)
- The compression argument against a single model: "it's just like too heterogeneous and dynamic for any monolithic model to try to compress it into one static representation." Note that the objection is *dynamic* as well as heterogeneous — even a model large enough to hold every configuration would hold a stale one. ([NeoCognition](../sources/20260812_I6aiEf3aEFQ.md), 06:02-06:12)
- The conclusion drawn: "agents must continually learn on the job to acquire what I call specialized expertise for each specific microworld." ([NeoCognition](../sources/20260812_I6aiEf3aEFQ.md), 06:12-06:24)
- This supplies the general account behind a pattern the wiki has already seen measured in one vertical: Anterior reports model and pipeline work reaching roughly 95% while a customer- and workflow-specific context loop pushed toward 99% ([Last-Mile Domain Context Beats Model Chasing](last-mile-domain-context-beats-model-chasing.md)). The microworlds framing says that gap is structural rather than a property of clinical review.
- The mechanism is left open here. The wiki holds several instantiations — skills packaging procedural expertise, per-site skills so an agent does not rediscover a website, expert taxonomies encoded as graphs, running profiles for a person — and this concept is the reason each of them exists, not a preference among them.
- Provenance and limits: asserted, not measured. There is no count behind "millions," no study of configuration variance, and no experiment separating heterogeneity from difficulty. The speaker is COO of a continual-learning company, and this argument is that company's premise.
