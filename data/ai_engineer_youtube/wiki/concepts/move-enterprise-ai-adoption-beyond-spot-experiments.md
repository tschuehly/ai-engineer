# Move enterprise AI adoption beyond spot experiments

Summary: Enterprise AI adoption becomes more credible when it moves from isolated pilots into a systematic portfolio of use cases across teams. The source links higher reported ROI to organizations that submit and pursue more use cases, while also noting that many enterprises remain stuck between pilots and scaled deployment.

Use when:
- Planning an enterprise AI transformation program.
- Diagnosing why isolated agent or AI pilots are not changing organizational outcomes.

Details:
- The talk contrasts growing production agent deployment with the reality that many enterprises are still in pilot or experimental phases and struggle to scale beyond the first exciting experiments. (02:47-04:30)
- Leading adopters are described as doing multiple things at once, thinking about AI strategy comprehensively, and looking beyond first-tier time savings toward revenue growth, new capabilities, and new product lines. (05:27-06:07)
- The source reports that more submitted use cases correlated with better self-reported ROI, which supports treating AI adoption as a cross-organizational portfolio rather than a single isolated tool rollout. (16:40-17:21)
- This source is useful but not definitive evidence: the survey is self-reported and from an AI-engaged audience, so portfolio breadth should be treated as a plausible adoption signal, not proof of causality. (09:34-09:48)
- **Why the spot experiment does not graduate on its own, stated as an identity.** Shenoy's account of the failure mode needs no metrics: "a lot of people will use Claude Code or give it to their whole enterprise, expect folks to just start using it. Everyone assumes the usage just shows up. But as we all know, that's simply not the case. It never does. Getting a 100-year-old firm to change its processes is hard. You could have the best AI coworker on Earth. And if the person who's closed the books for the last 20 years continues to do things the same way, nothing changes. Nothing happens." Provisioning a tool across an enterprise looks like the deployment step and is not; it is the step before the deployment step. ([Shenoy](../sources/20260828_B0fjR3yaZFU.md), 14:49-15:32)
