# Reply to Every Inbound and Over-Capture at Signup

Summary: Two cheap decisions made before you can act on demand determine whether you can act on it later: capture more contact fields at signup than your current process uses, and send everyone an automated acknowledgment even when you have nothing to sell them. Both fix problems that only become visible months later, when the automation finally exists and the leads have already gone elsewhere.

Use when:
- Inbound volume exceeds what the team can answer and the plan is to answer the best ones.
- Designing a signup or waitlist form for a product whose sales motion does not exist yet.
- A capability is nine months out and the demand for it is arriving now.
- A backlog of unanswered leads is about to be worked through by newly built automation.

Details:
- **The volume, and the staffing behind it.** "In the beginning, we were just completely overwhelmed with inbound, which I know sounds like such a champagne problem, but it was me and four sales reps, and we were getting, you know, 10,000 inbound a day." ([Rosenthal](../sources/20260826_wdTRsfw0KG0.md), 04:14-04:31)
- **The capture regret is specifically about future automation.** "I would have, first of all, added way more fields to our sign-up form, like phone number, because eventually when we did catch up and we built all the automation and we figured out how to handle these leads, I didn't have a lot of information to go follow up with people. So, add a very in-depth sales form. It can be optional, but if people are willing to give you their phone number, you should get it, cuz at some point you might want to call them or have AI call them." The argument is not that more fields help today — they cost conversion today. It is that the field you did not collect is unrecoverable, and the machine you build later can only act on what the form asked for. (04:31-05:04)
- **Optional is the mechanism that makes over-capture affordable.** The advice is an in-depth form with optional fields, not a longer required one. The people willing to give a phone number self-select into a segment your later automation can reach by a channel it otherwise could not.
- **The silence cost, stated as an outcome rather than a risk.** "Try to respond to everyone… I wish I would have at least just sent some email out saying, 'Hey, we hear you. We don't have our enterprise product ready yet. You're on our waitlist. Tell us XYZ about what you're looking for.' Just some sort of automated email campaign to make them feel like we cared. Because when we finally did launch our enterprise product 9 months later, almost every company said, 'Well, you never got back to me. And I went out and I bought Microsoft Copilot.'" (05:04-05:36)
- **The acknowledgment does double duty as a capture instrument.** "Tell us XYZ about what you're looking for" turns a holding reply into a requirements survey across the whole waiting population — which is exactly the correction to the sampling problem in [Ship Self-Serve Before the Enterprise Tier](ship-self-serve-before-the-enterprise-tier.md), where the enterprise product was specified from whoever was loud enough to reach one person. An automated reply to everyone samples the quiet majority too.
- **Why "answer the qualified ones" is the wrong triage under this failure mode.** Triage assumes the unanswered leads are still available when you return. In an emerging category with funded substitutes, they are not — the named substitute here was purchased and deployed during the wait. The scarce resource is not attention, it is the option to sell to a company that has not yet bought something else.
- **The related instrumentation move on the same form.** [Attribute LLM-Sourced Inbound With a How-Did-You-Hear Field](attribute-llm-sourced-inbound-with-a-how-did-you-hear-field.md) makes the same argument for a different field: a free-text "how did you hear about us?" was the only instrument that could see assistant-driven inbound, and it worked because it was already on the form when the channel appeared. Both pages describe fields whose value is realized only after the thing they measure exists.
- **The countervailing consideration the talk does not raise.** Longer signup forms reduce completion, and an auto-reply to a segment you cannot serve for nine months sets an expectation you then have to meet. Neither cost is quantified here; both were evidently judged smaller than the observed loss. Also note that "10,000 inbound a day" and "almost every company" are recollections, given with no volumes, window, or measured churn to the named competitor.
