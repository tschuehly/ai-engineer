# Explain an AI Product as a Wound → Click → Transformation Story

Summary: Complex AI products get understood, remembered, and bought when they are pitched as a three-beat story — open on the user's pain ("the wound"), make the product instantly click, then prove the before/after transformation — instead of leading with the category label ("we built an agentic orchestration platform").

Use when:
- Writing a landing page, launch post, demo intro, deck's first slide, or elevator pitch for an AI product that people don't instantly "get."
- A pitch keeps starting with "we're building an agentic X for enterprise Y" and losing the room.
- You can ship fast but still can't answer "why does this matter to an average user?" in one breath.

Details:
- The premise: shipping is easy now, but you get "about the time it takes to ride an elevator" to make your mark, and "great tech that nobody understands dies quietly." The clearest stories are the ones that get funded, bought, and talked about (00:34-00:52, 05:20-05:35).
- **Beat 1 — the wound.** Start with the biggest pain point, not what you built: "do not start with what you built." The first slide should immerse people in their day-to-day and show what they are already tired of doing. Bad: "we built an agentic orchestration SecOps platform for enterprises" — "nobody feels anything when you say that." Good: "security teams are exhausted managing dozens of disconnected tools — alerts in one system, tickets in another, the real investigation buried in Slack threads and screenshots. We fix that by putting it all in one place." That touches a wound because the listener feels the emotion (00:53-02:09).
- The wound has a tight format: in ~20 seconds do three things — **identify the wound, say "we fix that," then show how** — so the product "starts claiming space in their mind" because they can see how it hits their day-to-day (02:19-02:37).
- **Beat 2 — make it click** (see [Give a Concrete Mental Image Instead of a Category Label](give-a-concrete-mental-image-instead-of-a-category-label.md)): pass the 17-year-old test, tie to a story people already understand, and give a picturable image rather than a category (02:37-04:05).
- **Beat 3 — the transformation.** Stop describing the product and start proving its value with an explicit before/after. Vague value claims ("we improve code quality with AI," "we increase productivity") fail because "I still do not know what actually changes for me." Concrete: "before, your support team spends 30 minutes digging through docs and tickets; with us they ask one question and get the answer in 10 seconds with the sources attached." "If I cannot see what changed, I do not feel the story" (04:05-04:51).
- The full method applied to the elevator: "Your team is wasting hours digging through Slack, email, and random Excel spreadsheets to find one piece of information. We fix that. We connect all that fragmented knowledge in one place so you can search it and get the answer in seconds." Same product, different story (04:52-05:20).
- This is a communication/positioning discipline, not a marketing-virality tactic — it complements [AI Does Not Replace Shareable Product Marketing](ai-does-not-replace-shareable-product-marketing.md) (earning attention) and [Fundraising Pitches Should Explain Why You and Why Now](fundraising-pitches-should-explain-why-you-and-why-now.md) (the investor narrative), but its unit is the user-facing explanation of what the product does and why it matters.
