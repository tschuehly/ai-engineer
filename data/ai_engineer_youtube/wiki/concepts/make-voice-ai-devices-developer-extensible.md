# Make Voice AI Devices Developer-Extensible

Summary: Voice AI hardware can be treated as a developer platform rather than a fixed command appliance. Open ecosystems, app editors, dev kits, and custom form factors let voice interfaces spread into speakers, toys, robots, appliances, and home automation.

Use when:
- Designing AI voice products that need community extensions or hardware customization.
- Comparing fixed assistant appliances with open voice-agent platforms.

Details:
- OpenHome criticizes existing smart speakers as hard to talk to, non-customizable, and lacking community, then positions its AI-driven speaker around natural conversation instead of command language. (03:20-03:58)
- The platform pitch emphasizes developer ecosystems: more than 10,000 developers building custom smart speakers, voice AI applications, games, personalities, and home automation tools. (04:07-05:40)
- The hardware strategy is intentionally broad: developers can put voice AI on smart speakers, talking toys, AI robots, and AI appliances. (05:10-05:25)
- Embodied / ambient endpoint (ElevenLabs / statue app): the motivation for hardware form factors is to *remove the screen* so the technology doesn't feel "tacked on" — the team is commissioning a physical statue with a microphone, speaker, and phone inside so you talk directly to it, and a K6 red phone booth where you pick up the handset and talk to an agent ("Sir Michael Caine"), instead of routing the interaction through a phone screen. 14:18-14:50
- Voice-from-provenance design: an inanimate object's voice should be derived from its history (a rock quarried in China, carved in Vietnam, 200 years among British-museum tourists → a blended accent), and as people start saying "I want to go to this floor please" to elevators, "what should a lift sound like" becomes a real product question for embedded voice agents. 07:55-09:45
