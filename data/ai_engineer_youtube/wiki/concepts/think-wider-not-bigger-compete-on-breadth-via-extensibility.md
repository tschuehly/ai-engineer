# Think Wider, Not Bigger: Compete on Breadth via User Extensibility

Summary: AI collapses the cost of adding product breadth (range of areas covered), so a small team can now span the surface area of an incumbent like AWS or Salesforce if it architects the product for user-driven extensibility — cover enough of the spectrum to onboard most users, then let users build the vertical features you don't support.

Use when:
- Deciding whether to compete on depth in a narrow space or breadth across many areas.
- Designing a product platform that should let users fill gaps you can't staff.
- Sizing an AI startup's ambition against a large incumbent.

Details:
- Breadth vs depth: every product has a range of areas it covers (breadth) and a number of features per area (depth). The old rule was that a small team couldn't out-*breadth* a giant, so it competed on *depth* in a narrow space — e.g., Vercel offers far fewer services than AWS but deeper front-end-leaning full-stack features, "so much so that even the agents prefer it." 13:20-14:16
- What changed: breadth is now viable. "I'm not saying you can build something as reliable as RDS. I'm saying that you can build a database platform into your product in a day or two of work with enough prompting and enough effort." 14:18-14:56
- The unlock is extensibility, not exhaustiveness: build enough across the spectrum to let most users *start*, and when a user needs a vertical feature you don't support, "it's not your problem... they can build the features that are missing themselves" — if you architect the product so users can do things you never anticipated. 14:56-15:10
- Accidental example: Slack "is now the platform people run their agents in half the time... it's not a good product, but it's the right shape for people to build the features they want into it through the somewhat functional Slack bot APIs." Right shape beats feature completeness. 15:08-15:23
- Framing to calibrate ambition: "It's time to compete with Slack... build your own AWS... challenge Salesforce directly... If your idea doesn't feel stupid, it's cuz your idea's not big enough." 15:23-15:42
- Complements the tier-collapse concept: rescoping ambition down a tier is *why* breadth is now affordable; this concept is *how* to spend that affordance (wide + extensible rather than deep + closed).
