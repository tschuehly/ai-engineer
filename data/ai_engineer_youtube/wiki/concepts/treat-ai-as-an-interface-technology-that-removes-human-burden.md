# Treat AI as an Interface Technology That Removes Human Burden

Summary: Reframe AI as an *interface* technology, not only an intelligence technology — a system that can finally remove interface constraints rather than a smarter engine hiding behind a prompt. The design question becomes "what burden are we still putting on humans only because the machine used to be too limited to carry it?" Move timing, modality, context-gathering, ambiguity-noticing, and repair off the human and let the AI *participate* mid-thought using the affordances humans already use with each other.

Use when:
- Designing the interaction surface for a capable model and deciding how much work the human should still do.
- Choosing a modality: resisting the reflex that every AI product must be chat, must be voice, or must be a wall of markdown.
- Building multi-party or ambient AI (meetings, assistants that listen) where knowing *who* is speaking and *when* to speak matters as much as what to say.

Details:
- Mindset shift: "AI is not just an intelligence technology; it's increasingly becoming an interface technology," so "book-smart models alone are not enough." Stop picturing AI as "a smarter machine hiding behind prompts, agents, loops, and all the old paradigms"; see intelligence itself as a thing that can "remove interface constraints and amplify human potential." For 75 years humans adapted to the machine's syntax, forms, timing, and batch; a system that can reason, listen, infer, and adapt "should be able to meet us partway, if not all the way." 16:31-18:11
- Operating design question: "what burden are we still putting on humans only because the machine used to be too limited to carry that burden itself?" Ask it and "the whole interface space opens up." The answer "isn't always chat, it isn't always voice, and not a wall of markdown" — it is the affordances humans already use with each other: "a question, a pause, a sketch, a checklist, a quiet aside, or saying nothing at all," in an interface "where timing and modality aren't the human's job anymore" and "choosing the right channel at the right moment is done by the AI." 18:11-19:07
- Participation vs backchanneling: the field is converging on a participating interface (OpenAI GPT real-time 2 backchannels "mhm"/"right"; NVIDIA's research model yields on interruption and resumes the thread — "real turn taking, listening and speaking at once in real time"), but "making listening noises is not really the same as knowing who's in the room." Models trained only for audio flow "are not trained to tell that 'Hey Ted' wasn't meant for it." 12:29-14:10
- A working participation protocol needs multi-party awareness: a utility-driven model that creates goals, labels each participant statement as a question/proposal/answer, tracks who a speaker refers to (the "critical" part), and "only takes a turn when no one else is speaking or holding the floor" — so it can resolve an objective with "no one wrote the prompt, no one patched the turn and hit submit; the system was in the conversation, following it, understanding, and choosing its moment." 14:35-16:31
- Payoff and caveat: taking the interface burden off people is where adoption follows ("when you take that burden off people, the [friction] disappears and adoption follows"); the goal is "not by making everything magical, not by making everything voice, not by replacing human judgment, but by making computers for once more fluent with us." Cites Sherry Turkle (MIT): "conversation is the most human and humanizing thing we do." 19:03-20:09
