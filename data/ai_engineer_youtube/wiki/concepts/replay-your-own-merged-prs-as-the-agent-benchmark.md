# Replay Your Own Merged PRs as the Coding-Agent Benchmark

Summary: The cheapest coding-agent eval a team already owns is its own merge history. Pick pull requests that represent work you were happy with, replay each one across the harnesses you are considering, and read off quality against cost and quality against time on your codebase. This answers a question no public leaderboard can, because public coding benchmarks are built on a specific stack and yours probably is not it.

Use when:
- Choosing a default coding agent, harness, or model tier for a team and wanting evidence stronger than a vibe check.
- A public benchmark ranks a model highly on a language, framework, or task shape that does not match your repository.
- Deciding whether to spend time evaluating each newly-hyped model, or whether a third-party task router can be trusted to pick for you.

Details:
- **The construction, which is deliberately provenance-blind.** "We select pull requests that represent great engineering work. It could be agent created, could be human created, could be a hybrid, doesn't matter. You pick the agents you want to use and benchmark. And then you get a quality versus cost and time breakdown on your code base." Selecting on *quality of the outcome* rather than on who wrote it is what makes the set easy to assemble — any repo with review discipline already has one. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 13:29-13:46)
- **The reason a public number does not substitute.** "If you're kind of going off the public benchmarks, SWE-bench or Terminal-Bench or other stuff, like those tasks may have absolutely nothing to do with your task. Like SWE-bench is all in Python, we're Ruby on Rails. It is not the case that the benchmarks are identical for them. There's trends that do compare, but the results can be very, very different." Note the shape of the claim: not that public benchmarks are wrong, but that the correlation is a trend rather than a transfer. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 13:46-14:05)
- **Two axes, not one, and they disagree.** Superconductor plots quality versus cost and quality versus time separately, and their results split across them: Anthropic agents "consistently getting better, but not really any faster" on time and "clearly just so much more expensive for us" on cost; Codex and Cursor "pretty fast and quite good"; open models "getting better and better over time, but they're kind of slow." A single quality score would have hidden the tradeoff that actually moved their default. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 14:10-14:51)
- **What it is for: moving a default, not settling an argument.** "When we saw these results, they kind of matched our vibe check. We wanted to kind of like have hard data, too. We switched our default to Codex at that time" — while continuing to run several models, because "there's different use cases for them. We like the variety." The benchmark selects a default; it does not collapse the portfolio. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 14:51-15:06)
- **The second payoff is about attention rather than accuracy.** Without a standing benchmark, every new release costs a decision: "I heard MiniMax is good. I heard GLM is good, and Kimi K2 is good, but haven't had the time to try it out, and everyone keeps telling me I need to cuz it's so much better and faster and cheaper. And you kind of have that anxiety for a little while, and then finally you take the two hours to try it, and it's like, 'Oh, actually didn't really work for us. So, I just wasted those two hours.'" A replayable PR set turns that recurring two-hour gamble into a run. This is the operational half of [model and harness agnosticism](use-stable-agent-harnesses-as-model-evolution-boundaries.md): portability makes switching cheap, and a private benchmark is what tells you whether to. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 15:27-15:53)
- **The argument against outsourced task routing.** "You've probably heard about people routing tasks to the right models and all that, but how does some third party know what to route for your code base?" If per-model strength is codebase-specific enough that public benchmarks do not transfer, then a router trained on general task shapes is guessing at exactly the thing the private benchmark measures. Singh treats this as the eventual use of the data rather than a shipped feature. Compare [routing between model tiers by quality dimension](route-between-model-tiers-by-quality-dimension-not-only-cost.md), where the per-dimension split comes from a vendor leaderboard rather than from the customer's repo. ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 16:51-17:12)
- **What this method does not give you, and the source says so.** "This is for our code base again. I'm not trying to make any general claims here." The transferable artifact is the construction, not the rankings — and the talk supplies no task count, scoring rubric, definition of "quality," axis units, harness versions, or account of how a replayed attempt is judged against the original PR. Anyone building this has to decide the scorer themselves, which is where the difficulty actually sits; see [measuring generated code quality beyond pass rate](measure-generated-code-quality-beyond-pass-rate.md) and [building coding benchmarks around construct validity](build-coding-benchmarks-around-construct-validity.md). ([Arjun Singh](../sources/20260809_OL7kfezynJM.md), 14:35-14:43)
- **The infrastructure bill this hides.** Replaying real PRs across many harnesses repeatedly is an infrastructure problem before it is an evaluation problem — per-task environments, a retry policy that separates model failures from infrastructural ones, and noise control all have to exist before a number is trustworthy ([run agentic coding evals as an infrastructure-reliability problem](run-agentic-coding-evals-as-an-infrastructure-problem.md)). Superconductor is a team already running everything in isolated cloud environments, which is why the replay is cheap for them and may not be for a team that has not made that move first.
