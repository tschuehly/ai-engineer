# Staff Forward-Deployed Engineers Who Run Deals and Build the Deal Tooling

Summary: The forward-deployed engineer role exists because AI collapsed two jobs into one — the technical person supporting revenue can now also build and maintain the internal systems that revenue runs on — and the corresponding move for non-technical GTM staff is trained heavy tool use, not vibe coding.

Use when:
- Deciding who owns internal AI tooling for a revenue, support, or operations function.
- Staffing a solutions or sales-engineering function at a company whose GTM stack is agent-heavy.
- A GTM team wants AI leverage and the debate is whether to teach everyone to build.

Details:
- The GTM org is three parts: account executives "which run the deals," SDRs that "help with demand generation," and "separately a FDE org. So forward deployed engineering organization." ([Wang](../sources/20260826_6pbQgnJ9Voc.md), 14:36-14:58)
- The FDE group owns the systems as well as the deals: it "does a lot of the maintenance and feature building of these AI systems," so members are "both running deals and supporting deals but then also making everything smoother by doing sales but then also building the sales system." The speaker's own description of this is "kind of a funky thing we have going on," not a settled pattern. (15:28-15:56)
- The origin argument is the durable part. The role previously did not exist — "once upon a time the FDE role didn't really exist. Palantir started calling some people FDEs, but that was really it. And what tech companies had was like solutions and sales engineers, and then account executives" — and what changed is capability, not org fashion: "because of AI, as a technical person that is supporting revenue generation, you can actually not only support the revenue generation, but then very easily build the tooling to smooth everything over and make your own life easier, make the lives of AEs easier… Before that was like two jobs, and now it's like one job." (17:16-18:07)
- **The non-FDE half is the more transferable finding.** Everyone outside FDE "has learned how to use AI really well," but "they're not generally vibe coding these interfaces that we have" — the company invests in training sessions so "people really understand how to use these tools" instead. Expert operation and building are treated as separate skills with separate investments, and only one is asked of the whole team. (14:59-15:28)
- The scale is small and the speaker does not project it: about eight or nine FDEs at a company of about 115 people, with his own answer to whether the everyone-does-everything model survives growth being "probably not. But at least right now that's what we have, and I think that's a really good working model to get pretty far." (18:07-18:31)
- **Limits.** One company's current arrangement, reported by a founder, with no productivity, coverage, or attrition data on either side and no comparison against a conventional solutions-engineering split. The model is explicitly presented as unscaled and possibly temporary. (14:36-18:31)
