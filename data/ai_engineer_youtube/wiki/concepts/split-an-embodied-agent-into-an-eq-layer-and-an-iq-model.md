# Split an Embodied Conversational Agent Into an EQ Layer and an IQ Model

Summary: Sidney Primas (LemonSlice) forecasts that face-to-face AI converges on one end-to-end model that takes the user's video and audio in and emits the avatar's video and audio out — handling listening, deciding what to say, and its own emotional state internally — explicitly *not* an intelligent model, fed by a separate IQ model that does the tool calling and reasoning. The split is a bet about which half is better learned end-to-end and which half stays a separate, swappable component.

Use when:
- Designing the architecture of an avatar, companion, or face-to-face conversational product.
- Deciding what belongs inside a native multimodal model versus in an orchestration layer around it.
- Evaluating a vendor that sells one layer of a conversational stack and expects you to bring the other.
- Planning a roadmap from a scripted expression layer toward a learned internal state.

Details:
- The forecast architecture: "there'll be a single model that is the EQ layer for AI… the model will literally take in directly the user video and the user audio… like you are in a FaceTime, and put out the avatar video and audio… Internally inside of the model it will do the audio understanding, generate what it's going to say, model its own internal state like own internal emotional state, and then based on all of that produce an output video and audio. And all of this will be trained in one model." Evidence offered is "early papers that are doing proofs of concepts around this" (19:08-20:12).
- The disclaimer is the interesting half: "what we're not saying is that this EQ model will be very intelligent. It'll have very high EQ and it'll be very good at interacting with people, but there'll be a separate model that will drive the IQ that will basically give this EQ model input to do all the magical things that AI can do today. Like do the tool calling, do the deep thinking, do all the intelligent stuff. But there will be an end-to-end layer up top" (20:13-20:41). Timeline: "within two or three years" (20:48-20:58).
- Note what this is architecturally: neither the cascaded pipeline nor the fully end-to-end model this wiki's voice-agent pages debate. The *presentation and interaction* layer is end-to-end (nothing is transcribed to text and back), while *reasoning* is a separate model. That is a different cut of the same stack — it collapses the boundaries that lose prosody and timing, and keeps the boundary that lets you swap models, add tools, and audit decisions. The voice-agent literature's usual objection to end-to-end models is weak instruction following and tool calling; this design concedes that objection rather than arguing with it.
- The company-strategy version of the same split, stated plainly: LemonSlice sells the visual layer as an API and expects customers to "bring their own LLM" and their own voices (08:14-08:32), and Primas declines the IQ side outright — "not for us. That's not what we plan to do… you could imagine people using our technology, giving it the right context, having the right harness around it, and then having the nice IQ brain around it… but we don't want to solve that problem" (24:10-24:34).
- Today's intermediate step, because the end-to-end model does not exist yet: an "emotional engine… having them be more aware of what's actually happening inside of the conversation, and then be able to react… the right emotions at the right time for the right duration, and same for the actions" (16:08-16:33). The mechanism is a predictor sitting beside the video model: "predicting based on the audio input from the avatar and the text input for what the avatar is going to say what the action they should be doing at this moment in time," targeted at one to two months out (18:24-18:48).
- The gap it exists to close is control, not capability — the single most transferable observation in the talk. Of the richer next-generation model: "our model can already do this today. It has the capabilities to do this. It's just not controllable enough to like make it real time with the conversation and not deterministic enough to to make it useful with the conversation" (18:03-18:24). A capability that fires unpredictably is not yet a feature; the engineering that turns one into the other is a control problem, and it is the reason a separate steering component exists at all.
- What the current defect feels like, which is the product motivation: "today when you interact with these avatars… it definitely still feels awkward. And I think a big part of that is they don't emotionally react to you. They're not listening to you. They're not emoting in the right [way]" (16:33-16:51). Compare the standing avatar critique from the platform side — "they're still all kind of weird… it's still kind of off" (5dCAmSDOAjI 08:56-09:12). Two vendors agree on the symptom; Primas is naming reactive emotion as the cause.
- Internal state has a stated trajectory with a real trade at each end. Today "the way we think about actions and emotions is basically words. If you can describe it in words, you can generate those actions and emotions" — describable, promptable, and debuggable. Long term he expects "an internal state in latent space that defines what the emotional state is or the goals are of the model," tracked over "some history of time," and "they won't be human interpretable necessarily" (21:31-22:22). The move buys expressiveness and continuity across a conversation and costs the ability to inspect or specify what the avatar is feeling.
- Caveats: the end-to-end EQ model is a forecast, not a system — no papers are named, no results are cited, and the two-to-three-year figure is one founder's estimate from a vendor whose product occupies exactly the layer being predicted.
