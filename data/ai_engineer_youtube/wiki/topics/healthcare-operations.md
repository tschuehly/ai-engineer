# Healthcare Operations

## Overview

Healthcare operations are a strong AI application area when the work is administrative, rules-driven, measurable, and financially material. Revenue cycle management shows the pattern: eligibility checks, registration, documentation, coding, prior authorization, denials, appeals, and payer-provider communication create friction that can delay or prevent payment even when clinical care has already happened.

The best AI opportunities in this domain are not only faster document generation. Longitudinal workflow data lets teams connect late failures such as denials back to upstream missing fields, policy mismatches, prior authorization errors, or documentation gaps. Generative AI can then help assemble evidence and draft appeals, but domain experts should still make final submission decisions when clinical judgment or payer-policy interpretation matters.

Hippocratic AI extends the domain from back-office friction to the clinical conversation itself, and shows what changes when the AI talks directly to a patient. Vivek Muppalla frames the opportunity as arithmetic rather than automation: healthcare has always been rationed by scarcity — "not enough clinicians, not enough time, not enough money, and hence the word triage" — so once clinically safe conversation gets cheap enough, "you don't have to have calls just for the sickest 5%, but you can call everyone." Their reported scale is 200M+ clinical interactions across 60+ health systems with zero significant safety incidents and 8.5/10 patient satisfaction, and the calls do real clinical work: confirming vitals back to the patient, resolving a spelled-out drug name, detecting that a medication the patient was told to stop was still taken (and deferring to the primary care doctor rather than advising), and escalating to a live nurse on shortness of breath. Three engineering consequences follow from the stakes. Recognition errors dominate apparent reasoning errors, so the ASR is [conditioned on conversation context and domain vocabulary](../concepts/condition-asr-on-conversation-context-and-domain-vocabulary.md) rather than bought off the shelf. A single model is treated as an unacceptable single point of failure, so the system [runs parallel specialist models behind a speak-up gate](../concepts/run-parallel-specialist-models-with-a-speak-up-gate.md) with background verifiers on tool calls. And accuracy is measured against consequence, so evals are [sized to the error rate the consequence demands](../concepts/size-eval-suites-to-the-error-rate-the-consequence-demands.md) and graded on the harm scale used for human clinicians. The same expert-in-the-loop principle that governs appeal submission reappears here in a different form: rather than reviewing each output, 7,000+ trained clinicians run continuous evaluation of the system, and the agent escalates to a human when the clinical situation warrants it.

Ufonia's Dora occupies the same patient-facing tier from the opposite direction — inbound-quality assurance rather than outbound scale — and supplies the release process that Hippocratic AI's numbers presuppose. Jared Joselowitz's product phones patients for post-op follow-ups and pre-op checks (~200,000 real clinical calls across 20 UK hospitals, contracted to reach a million patients in two years), and because it asks about symptoms and gives advice it "is what's called a medical device and therefore it has to of course be regulated." Regulation, he says, reduces to three questions — what does your software do, what could go wrong, how do you ensure it doesn't — and the second is where healthcare diverges from ordinary software: the hazard list is written with clinicians up front (missing a red flag such as sudden vision loss or severe pain, hallucinating an answer to a medical question, ignoring patient distress; "20, 30, 40" documented hazards). From there the standard de-risking loop is unusable, so the team [replaces ship-and-rollback with hazard-first simulation](../concepts/replace-ship-and-rollback-with-hazard-first-simulation.md) and treats simulation results as evidence only after they [validate the simulated patient and the judge against humans](../concepts/validate-the-simulated-user-and-the-judge.md) — a patient-and-public-involvement study for simulator realism, and a 240-case, 10-clinician, 10-specialty panel for the judge, where near-perfect sensitivity matters more than F1 because "you would rather overcall hazards that aren't there than undercall hazards that are there." The metric that drives improvement is likewise clinician-owned: prompts are [optimized against a cost matrix](../concepts/optimize-prompts-against-an-asymmetric-cost-matrix.md) in which a missed red flag is catastrophic and an over-called one costs the patient "a couple extra questions." Two framings travel outside medicine: autonomy is rationed by evidence ("as the system gets more evidence, you can give it more independence"), and the artifact that satisfies a regulator is the audit trail, not the model — "you don't ship the model, you ship the evidence." Both talks converge on clinicians staying in the loop as *evaluators* rather than per-output reviewers: 7,000+ running continuous evaluation at Hippocratic AI, and 10 specialties calibrating the judge plus supervision at every rollout stage at Ufonia.

Onlay returns the topic to the administrative tier that revenue-cycle AI opened, but with the execution layer built out. Vasant Kearney frames the objective the same way — drive down the cost of interacting with insurance, or improve the patient experience, because insurance shapes how the patient experiences the whole process — and then asks what it takes to let an agent actually *act* across eligibility, treatment authorization, documents, claim submission, and payment. His answer is that the domain already published the constraint: [ground agent actions in an existing domain transaction standard](../concepts/ground-agent-actions-in-an-existing-domain-transaction-standard.md). X12 belongs in the harness next to memory, tools, checks, permissions, handoffs, and evals, because LLMs "thrive … when they're confined" the way COBOL or TypeScript confines, because a ~50-step claims objective propagates errors and needs "something grounded that can be rejected to," and because the standard is public — "this is not my schema," so both an agent doing research and a new engineer can look up every transaction. The reframe that follows is the talk's sharpest: a phone call to a payer *is* an X12 transaction (identifying the patient is a 270), as is a portal session or an imaging attachment (275), so channel choice stops being an architectural fork and becomes a routing detail over one internal representation. But conformance is not truth. The payer's portal, phone system, X12 layer, and FHIR layer are often built by different teams or different contracted companies, so you must [treat every external system of record as non-authoritative](../concepts/treat-every-external-system-of-record-as-non-authoritative.md): they contradict each other, and they can also agree on the wrong answer — all three confirm coverage, you treat the patient, and the claim is denied because the patient was not covered at that time. Everything therefore normalizes into "your own internal semi-correct X12. Correct until downstream evidence proves it otherwise." This is the administrative mirror of the patient-facing talks above: where Ufonia and Hippocratic AI ration autonomy by *hazard evidence*, Onlay rations belief by *downstream evidence*, and both refuse to let a passing check stand in for a verified outcome. The deployment constraints follow the same enterprise logic — agent memory lives in a database rather than on local disk the way Claude Code and Codex do it, so [tiered database memory with an escape hatch](../concepts/give-enterprise-agents-tiered-database-memory-with-an-escape-hatch.md) replaces file-based memory — and the closing posture, "AI pilled and AI skeptical" at once, includes a cost rule that matters in a domain whose whole justification is cost reduction: do not spend an overpowered model on routine work done a thousand times a day.

Maven Clinic adds the fourth position in this cluster: a digital health platform (maternity, fertility, parenting, menopause) whose AI work is neither a regulated clinical device nor a claims execution layer, but member-facing product features plus an internal engineering transformation. Dan Feng's contribution to the domain is a risk classification a non-regulated health product can actually apply — [sort failures by whether the user can retry](../concepts/sort-failures-by-whether-the-user-can-retry.md). Appointment scheduling tolerates a one-in-a-thousand failure "because the users really can just click the button again"; a reimbursement claim tolerates none, because paying $50 on a $200 request or $200 on a $50 request "will cause a escalation right away" in either direction. The gate for the intolerable class is cross-model agreement on the same receipt with a human-agent handoff when the models disagree — which is the low-ceremony sibling of Hippocratic AI's parallel specialists and Ufonia's hazard traceability, applied to money rather than to clinical advice. His release process shows the same trait: [confidence earned from repeated runs and post-launch sampling](../concepts/earn-release-confidence-from-repeated-runs-and-post-launch-sampling.md), with hundreds of integration tests run many times each at a ~90% bar, automated rubric scoring on every conversation, and a dedicated human group spot-checking at roughly 20% coverage for new launches. Reading the four together, the domain's pattern is consistent across risk tiers: the tier sets the ceremony, not the principle. Ufonia rations autonomy by hazard evidence, Onlay rations belief by downstream evidence, Hippocratic AI rations confidence by eval volume, and Maven rations verification by whether the user can undo the mistake — but all four refuse a single global reliability bar and all four keep a standing human evaluation function rather than per-output review. Maven's other healthcare-relevant note is organizational: its AI journey started two years ago and produced Maven Intelligence, "an orchestration layer across all our product to enable AI for everybody in this company and for our clients," which is the same platform-before-features move Onlay's execution layer represents on the claims side.

Anterior appears twice in this wiki, a year apart, and the pair reads as one continuous argument about where a vertical AI company's quality actually comes from. Christopher Lovejoy's earlier talk described closing the gap from a ~95% pipeline baseline to ~99% with an adaptive domain-intelligence loop: clinicians review production outputs, label failure modes against an ontology, and their labelled misses become the focused eval sets engineers iterate on. Anuj Iravane's talk supplies the part that loop presupposes — the data — and reports that the obvious source is unavailable. The inputs are scanned fax bundles ("around 70% of medical communication still happens via fax") that run past 300 pages of handwriting, tables, checkboxes, and images, drawn from "a very long tail of rare … cases," and they are PHI Anterior's contracts forbid retaining, reusing, or deriving from, redacted or anonymized copies included: "nothing really survives in any sort of dataset that we want to persist." So they [generate the eval data by reversing the inference workflow](../concepts/generate-eval-data-by-reversing-the-inference-workflow.md), sampling a label and a reasoning trace from the explicit decision-tree representation of a policy — a CPAP medical-necessity policy is the worked example — and synthesizing the record backwards. This is the fifth risk tier in the 2026-08-19 cluster and the one where the binding constraint is *governance* rather than hazard, autonomy, or retryability: the other four could keep their data and had to decide what to do with a mistake; Anterior can observe production and then must forget it. The domain-expert principle running through all five reappears in its most literal form here — where Ufonia's clinicians calibrate a judge and Hippocratic AI's run continuous evaluation, Anterior's [own the pipeline as skills](../concepts/hand-domain-experts-the-pipeline-as-skills.md), because "no AI engineer would" know what a good medical record looks like. Reported results: roughly 90% of datasets synthetic, clinicians distinguishing synthetic from real "about 60% of the time" in blind review, and datasets "created just in time for these customer deployments" rather than waiting on customer data — which is the same evidence-before-exposure posture as Ufonia's simulation-first release, reached from a privacy constraint instead of a regulatory one.

Hinge Health completes the cluster with the sixth position: member-facing health AI in a consumer-scale product, where the risk is not a regulator's hazard list but an ambient baseline the whole industry already ships against. Rashi Agrawal opens with numbers that are about the market rather than her product — "40 million people actually use these models for triaging their health care issues," a man hospitalized three weeks with bromide at 200 times the safe limit after a chatbot told him to swap sodium bromide for salt, Mount Sinai's first independent safety test of a consumer health AI under-triaging life-threatening emergencies "50% of the times," and ECRI naming AI chatbot misuse the number one health technology hazard of 2026 — and draws the conclusion that sets her design brief: "this is not really a frontier problem. This is the production baseline that we're working with right now." Her thesis is that "most AI safety failures in health care are not model failures. They are architectural decisions that were made before even a single token was generated," and the three layers that follow are each a way of moving a safety property earlier. PHI is handled by [making the failure architecturally impossible](../concepts/make-regulated-data-failures-architecturally-impossible.md) — stripped at the pipeline boundary before the data lake, so "a developer opens a dashboard, there's nothing to redact. The PHI was never there," with no pipes between production and non-production and access scoped by role *and* geographic region. Must-not-fail behavior is handled by [running it in a code layer above the model](../concepts/run-must-not-fail-decisions-in-code-above-the-model.md): a deterministic layer runs first on every turn and owns 911/988 escalation ("the model should not even see this turn"), high-stakes intent routing, and identity verification, because "authentication is a security boundary. Prompts are not." And safety is handled as a continuous layer rather than a gate, with judges [scoring live traffic](../concepts/score-every-production-conversation-to-judge-agent-health.md) across dozens of dimensions, per-message member thumbs as "the truth signal," and 100% sampling on high-stakes cases.

The second half of her talk is the piece the rest of the cluster leaves implicit: what a human does when the architecture is not enough. Five stakeholders — clinical, legal, compliance, product, engineering — look at one open issue five days before launch and see five different risks, and the resolution is [an explicit set of severity rules](../concepts/decide-ship-or-hold-with-explicit-severity-rules.md) rather than a negotiation: worst plausible outcome sets severity ("a bug that lightly annoys 100% of users is way less severe than one that could cause serious harm in 0.1% of cases"), severity is independent of who can fix it, the default flips by bug class (hold on safety, ship on polish), and the launch bar is calibrated to what the org already tolerates in production rather than what it says it tolerates. Two of those rules qualify claims made elsewhere in this cluster. Rule one is the decision-procedure form of the harm-scale and hazard-list machinery: where Hippocratic AI sizes evals to consequence and Ufonia enumerates hazards with clinicians, Agrawal states the underlying move — frequency-weighted metrics structurally cannot express "worst case," so the triage question has to change. Rule five names the ceiling all six talks run into: "judges scale, pattern interpretation doesn't," and "the bottleneck is not the compute, the models, the capability. It's actually having enough people to read the signal." That is the same constraint behind Hippocratic AI's 7,000+ clinician evaluators, Ufonia's 10-specialty panel, Maven's spot-check group, and Anterior's clinician skill-authors — stated here as a budget line rather than a staffing anecdote. Underneath all of it sits one discipline the others do not name: [check whether the judge is right before changing the agent](../concepts/check-the-judge-before-changing-the-agent.md), because "in a non-deterministic system, the judge is also non-deterministic," and the five rules "all assume one thing, that your underlying signal is true."

A third Anterior talk closes the loop between the two above and moves the cluster's attention from the model to the stack it has to live in. Christopher Lovejoy — now describing the forward-deployed side of the job, "I embed within enterprise organizations and help them get value from using AI agents" — and VP of engineering Saul Howard replay the meeting that kills enterprise health AI: the POC hits its metrics, is fast and cheap, and the room is happy, but "everyone here is assuming that … the AI was … the challenging part," and the next day compliance asks for the audit trail, the compliance officer asks how PHI moves through the system, and the chief medical officer asks what the mechanism for clinician escalation actually is. Their claim is that these are architecture questions with known enterprise answers, and their three primitives each convert a healthcare constraint into a storage or platform decision. [An audit trail is a chain of evidence, not a developer log](../concepts/an-audit-trail-is-a-chain-of-evidence-not-a-developer-log.md): under SOC 2, HITRUST, and HIPAA it must record every action, every data access, and the authorization behind each, and hold up "if our agent's decisions came up in a court of law" — so they make it fall out of an append-only event log rather than reconstruct it, accepting a permanently more expensive read path in exchange. [Object storage beside that log](../concepts/store-agent-data-in-object-storage-beside-the-event-log.md) holds the records themselves, with the events carrying only references, which is what lets engineers retrace an agent's steps "without having access to the personal health information itself" while still seeing the *shape* of the data through its schema — the constraint Anterior's other talk states as a data problem, handled here as a data-plane boundary. And [treating humans and models as the same kind of agent](../concepts/treat-humans-and-models-as-the-same-kind-of-agent.md) answers the escalation question the cluster keeps raising: rather than enumerate escalation points, make any action an LLM can take one a clinician can take too, so the handoff can happen anywhere and "any step downstream doesn't care about whether it was a human or an LLM."

That last primitive is the sharpest cross-source result in the cluster. Six talks agree that a human must stay in the loop and each names a different arrangement — Hippocratic AI's 7,000+ evaluators, Ufonia's 10-specialty judge panel, Maven's disagreement off-ramp, Hinge Health's signal readers, Anterior's clinician skill-authors — but all of them place the human *outside* the run, reviewing or calibrating. Equivalency places the clinician *inside* it as an alternative executor, which is the version that fits the unpredictable, threshold-triggered escalations administrative clinical work actually produces. It also pays the cluster's recurring labelling bill: because both can perform the same task, "your difference is your eval." That is one of three ways [evals emerge from the primitives](../concepts/let-evals-emerge-from-your-architectural-primitives.md) rather than being attached to the side — replay from the ledger for exact counterfactuals, human-agent difference for scores, and object storage for running evals "inside your customer's environment without actually ever exposing that data," which is the same posture Anterior's synthetic-data talk reaches from the opposite direction: there the data cannot be kept, here it cannot be moved. The closing rule generalizes past healthcare to "finance, defense, government work, anywhere where process is so important and has to be followed": bolting eval, security, and auditability onto a high-accuracy POC yields "something very brittle, something very hard to … externalize and to generalize," so [take the constraints as the foundation and rebuild toward the POC's accuracy](../concepts/sequence-production-ai-by-pillars-and-choose-the-model-last.md) on top of them.

Abridge takes the cluster's eighth position and is the first entry whose binding constraint is unit economics rather than risk governance. Chaitanya Asawa, who leads engineering for clinical decision support and agentic experiences, starts from the same administrative-friction diagnosis as Ensemble Health but locates it earlier: healthcare's administrative costs "have only gone up over the past a few decades and productivity hasn't necessarily increased" ("Baumol's cost disease"), and "a lot of our problems in health care we solve with labor, but even that we cannot actually keep up." The wedge is the note every visit requires — a SOAP note with chief complaint, assessment and plan — which costs roughly two hours a day of "pajama time" after work and is high-stakes in both directions, since notes are "used as a basis of billing" and "provide context to the clinician of the patient's longitudinal medical record." That product alone reached "300 of the largest health systems in the United States, Kaiser, Mayo, Johns Hopkins, Sutter" in two to three years, and the expansion thesis is that "everything is around the conversation… billing, clinical trial matching, clinical decision support… we've just built all this administrative machinery around that." What follows is the cluster's most complete statement of the *scale* problem: three KPIs — quality, latency, cost — all "on hard mode," with quality contrasted against his previous job at Glean, where "I could be wrong and it would have been fine," and cost set by a run rate of "100 million medical conversations a year." His answers are the two this wiki now files under [decomposing the deliverable and post-training a model per section](../concepts/decompose-the-deliverable-and-post-train-a-model-per-section.md) — the clinical note's own sections supply the narrowness that makes small models viable, because "health care is actually many specific workflows" — and [gating always-on listening with cheap event detectors](../concepts/gate-always-on-listening-with-cheap-event-detectors.md), since a system that captures spoken orders through a whole visit cannot poll a large model "every few seconds."

His evaluation contribution completes a gap the other seven leave open. Where Ufonia enumerates hazards, Hippocratic AI sizes suites to consequence, and Hinge Health scores live traffic, all of them presume that a correct answer is recognizable once you see it. Asawa names the case where it is not: for contextual clinical decision support, "the generator and verifier gap is really small. If I had a really really good generator verifier, then that would just be my generator itself" — so [the gap itself sizes the eval effort](../concepts/size-your-eval-effort-to-the-generator-verifier-gap.md), and the reference has to be manufactured. Abridge's procedure is the cluster's most specific use of clinician time: [two physicians independently write rubrics of the elements a good response should contain, a third adjudicates them into one, a fourth QAs it](../concepts/build-judge-references-from-adjudicated-expert-rubrics.md), and an LLM judge semantic-matches responses against those elements, with separate judges for safety, adversarial boundaries, and tone. Read against the cluster's recurring staffing bill — Hippocratic AI's 7,000+ evaluators, Ufonia's 10-specialty panel, Hinge Health's "judges scale, pattern interpretation doesn't" — this is the same constraint answered by spending experts on the reference rather than on the reviewing, on the explicit grounds that "not all of us are clinicians. I'm not a clinician. So how can we, the rest of the company, still move fast is by encoding that clinician judgement into LM judges." The surrounding release discipline is the cluster's now-familiar shape stated compactly: internal benchmarks before any product is built, staged rollout from "an alpha group of clinicians that we trust and they understand the stakes" through beta and A/B testing, then continual monitoring, "because you cannot get away with just being like a prototype that you ship out there."

## Key Concepts

- [Size Your Eval Effort to the Generator–Verifier Gap](../concepts/size-your-eval-effort-to-the-generator-verifier-gap.md) - clinical decision support has no cheap check, so evaluation stops being a phase and becomes the product's hard part, covered by several partial judges rather than one score.
- [Build Judge References From Independently Written, Adjudicated Expert Rubrics](../concepts/build-judge-references-from-adjudicated-expert-rubrics.md) - four physicians produce one rubric of required response elements, and the LLM judge only has to semantic-match against it.
- [Decompose the Deliverable and Post-Train a Small Model per Section](../concepts/decompose-the-deliverable-and-post-train-a-model-per-section.md) - the clinical note's own sections are the decomposition boundary that makes small post-trained models cheaper than frontier intelligence at 100M conversations a year.
- [Gate Always-On Listening With Cheap Event Detectors](../concepts/gate-always-on-listening-with-cheap-event-detectors.md) - in-visit order capture triggers heavy order-matching models from cheap gates rather than polling the conversation.
- [An Audit Trail Is a Chain of Evidence, Not a Developer Log](../concepts/an-audit-trail-is-a-chain-of-evidence-not-a-developer-log.md) - what SOC 2, HITRUST, and HIPAA require of an agent's record, and the append-only event log that makes auditability a property of storage rather than a reconstruction.
- [Store Agent Data in Object Storage Beside the Event Log, Not Inside It](../concepts/store-agent-data-in-object-storage-beside-the-event-log.md) - separating what happened from the PHI it touched lets engineers debug an agent they are not cleared to read the data of, and gives zero trust a single enforcement point.
- [Treat Humans and Models as the Same Kind of Agent](../concepts/treat-humans-and-models-as-the-same-kind-of-agent.md) - clinician escalation becomes a property of every step instead of a branch someone had to anticipate, and downstream steps stay indifferent to who acted.
- [Let Evals Emerge From Your Architectural Primitives](../concepts/let-evals-emerge-from-your-architectural-primitives.md) - replay, human-agent difference, and in-customer-environment scoring arrive as byproducts of the compliance architecture rather than as a separate workstream.
- [Run Must-Not-Fail Decisions in a Code Layer Above the Model](../concepts/run-must-not-fail-decisions-in-code-above-the-model.md) - emergency escalation, high-stakes intent routing, and identity verification run in deterministic code before the model sees the turn, because the labs' own authority hierarchy admits a prompt is not a security boundary.
- [Make Regulated-Data Failures Architecturally Impossible](../concepts/make-regulated-data-failures-architecturally-impossible.md) - PHI stripped at ingestion, no production-to-dev pipes, and region-scoped access buy a system "incapable of certain failures" rather than one that depends on policy compliance.
- [Decide Ship-or-Hold With Explicit Severity Rules](../concepts/decide-ship-or-hold-with-explicit-severity-rules.md) - worst case sets severity, severity is not capacity, defaults flip by bug class, and the launch bar is the org's revealed rather than stated risk tolerance.
- [Check Whether the Judge Is Right Before Changing the Agent](../concepts/check-the-judge-before-changing-the-agent.md) - a dropped clinical-accuracy score may be an over-calling judge, and fixing the judge prompt "is not cheating."
- [Generate Eval Data by Reversing the Inference Workflow](../concepts/generate-eval-data-by-reversing-the-inference-workflow.md) - when PHI contracts forbid keeping the records evals need, sample the label and a policy-tree reasoning trace and synthesize the record backwards, with labels correct by construction.
- [Hand Domain Experts the Pipeline as Skills](../concepts/hand-domain-experts-the-pipeline-as-skills.md) - clinicians own the data pipeline's logic as skill files on a generic agent harness, extending the expert-in-the-loop pattern from reviewing outputs to authoring stages.
- [Sort Failures by Whether the User Can Retry](../concepts/sort-failures-by-whether-the-user-can-retry.md) - a non-regulated health product can classify risk by user-absorbable recovery: scheduling tolerates a retryable miss, reimbursement amounts get cross-model agreement and a human off-ramp.
- [Earn Release Confidence From Repeated Runs and Post-Launch Sampling](../concepts/earn-release-confidence-from-repeated-runs-and-post-launch-sampling.md) - repeated-run pass-rate gates before release, rubric scoring plus a dedicated human spot-check group after, with coverage raised for new features.
- [Ground Agent Actions in an Existing Domain Transaction Standard](../concepts/ground-agent-actions-in-an-existing-domain-transaction-standard.md) - X12 already encodes the provider-payer contract for every claim-lifecycle stage, so it confines the agent, gives a 50-step pipeline a rejection point, and makes a phone call and an EDI feed the same transaction.
- [Treat Every External System of Record as Non-Authoritative](../concepts/treat-every-external-system-of-record-as-non-authoritative.md) - payer surfaces built by different contractors disagree and can jointly be wrong, so the internal record is a working belief that downstream evidence can overturn.
- [Give Enterprise Agents Tiered Database Memory With an Escape Hatch](../concepts/give-enterprise-agents-tiered-database-memory-with-an-escape-hatch.md) - enterprise deployments cannot use coding agents' local-disk memory, and organization/user memory that predicts repetitive work also risks steering the user.
- [Replace Ship-and-Rollback With Hazard-First Simulation When Errors Are Irreversible](../concepts/replace-ship-and-rollback-with-hazard-first-simulation.md) - patient-facing AI cannot be A/B tested or rolled back, so hazards are enumerated with clinicians, run in simulation, and autonomy is earned stage by stage against evidence.
- [Validate the Simulated User and the Judge Before Trusting a Simulation](../concepts/validate-the-simulated-user-and-the-judge.md) - a simulated-patient eval loop is two models, and each needs human validation (realism study; multi-specialty clinician panel) before its verdicts count as a safety case.
- [Optimize Prompts Against an Asymmetric Cost Matrix, Not Flat Accuracy](../concepts/optimize-prompts-against-an-asymmetric-cost-matrix.md) - clinicians define the cost matrix rather than the prompt, so the optimizer maximizes sensitivity to red flags instead of a dataset average.
- [Condition ASR on Conversation Context and Domain Vocabulary](../concepts/condition-asr-on-conversation-context-and-domain-vocabulary.md) - clinical entity recognition (drug names, vitals, addresses) needs conversation and domain conditioning, because mishearing masquerades as reasoning failure.
- [Run Parallel Specialist Models Behind a Speak-Up Gate](../concepts/run-parallel-specialist-models-with-a-speak-up-gate.md) - patient-facing systems need model redundancy without a latency penalty, plus verifiers on the actions they take.
- [Size Eval Suites to the Error Rate the Consequence Demands](../concepts/size-eval-suites-to-the-error-rate-the-consequence-demands.md) - in care delivery a 1% error is a daily count of harmed patients, which sets both the accuracy bar and the test-suite size.
- [Revenue Cycle AI Targets Administrative Friction](../concepts/revenue-cycle-ai-targets-administrative-friction.md) - healthcare AI can create direct value by reducing payer-provider communication friction, denials, rework, and delayed payment.
- [Prevent Revenue Cycle Denials Upstream](../concepts/prevent-revenue-cycle-denials-upstream.md) - end-to-end workflow data can turn late denial patterns into earlier checks and corrections.
- [Expert-Reviewed GenAI Appeals Beat Off-the-Shelf Letters](../concepts/expert-reviewed-genai-appeals-beat-off-the-shelf-letters.md) - clinical appeal generation needs evidence assembly, local quality standards, and expert approval.

## Open Questions

- Which revenue cycle steps can be fully automated, and which should remain expert-reviewed because payer policy or clinical necessity is disputed?
- How should providers measure whether payer-side AI denial automation is increasing preventable administrative burden?
- Which clinical conversations can an AI agent safely conduct end to end, and which must escalate to a clinician by policy rather than by model judgment?
- What does a harm-graded rubric look like outside medicine, where no established human grading scale exists to borrow?
- How large does a hazard list have to be before it counts as complete, given that both talks enumerate hazards with clinicians but neither describes a method for finding the hazard nobody thought of?
- Where is the boundary between a health product feature that can be classified by retryability and one that needs a regulated hazard analysis? Maven's scheduling and reimbursement examples are administrative, but a member-facing health platform also carries clinical content that none of these talks classifies.
- If a vertical AI vendor cannot retain production records, how does it detect a failure mode nobody has yet written into the policy tree? Anterior's clinicians re-create observed production cases synthetically, but the synthetic set can only cover branches the domain model already encodes.
- When payer surfaces disagree, what decides which one an agent acts on before the claim adjudicates — a learned per-payer reliability model, a conservative default, or a human? Onlay reports that idiosyncrasies are learned but does not describe how they are encoded.
- If the reading team is the binding constraint on production safety monitoring, how should judge dimensions be retired? Hinge Health adds a judge for every new production failure and reports that "the rate never hits zero," but no source in this cluster describes when a judge stops earning its human attention.
- If a clinician can execute any action an agent can, what carries their authority through the record — Lovejoy and Howard require the audit trail to hold "all of the authorization by which the agent did something," but no source in this cluster describes how a human's licence, scope of practice, or sign-off is represented when the two are interchangeable executors of the same step.
- Human-agent difference gives eval scores only where a human actually performed the task, which is the escalated tail. How should a team combine that high-quality but biased sample with the full-traffic judge scoring the rest of this cluster relies on, without letting the escalated subset define the quality bar for everything?
- Abridge spends four clinicians per rubric to build a reference the whole company can hill-climb against, but nothing in the talk says how many cases get rubrics or how coverage is chosen. When the reference set is this expensive per item, what decides which clinical questions earn one — production frequency, harm potential, or observed judge disagreement?
- Every other source in this cluster keeps a standing human evaluation function over production output; Abridge's clinician time is spent up front on the rubrics instead, with online signals (note edits, thumbs, star ratings) carrying the production channel. Which of the two is the better use of scarce clinician hours at a given scale, and does the answer change when the deliverable is a note the clinician edits anyway versus an answer they merely read?
- Calibrating to revealed rather than stated risk tolerance sets the floor from what already ships — but every talk in this cluster argues the industry's shipped baseline is itself unsafe (Mount Sinai's 50% under-triage, ECRI's 2026 hazard ranking). What decides when an org's revealed tolerance is the wrong reference point rather than the pragmatic one?
