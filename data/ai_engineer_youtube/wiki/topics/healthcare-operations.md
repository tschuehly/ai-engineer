# Healthcare Operations

## Overview

Healthcare operations are a strong AI application area when the work is administrative, rules-driven, measurable, and financially material. Revenue cycle management shows the pattern: eligibility checks, registration, documentation, coding, prior authorization, denials, appeals, and payer-provider communication create friction that can delay or prevent payment even when clinical care has already happened.

The best AI opportunities in this domain are not only faster document generation. Longitudinal workflow data lets teams connect late failures such as denials back to upstream missing fields, policy mismatches, prior authorization errors, or documentation gaps. Generative AI can then help assemble evidence and draft appeals, but domain experts should still make final submission decisions when clinical judgment or payer-policy interpretation matters.

Hippocratic AI extends the domain from back-office friction to the clinical conversation itself, and shows what changes when the AI talks directly to a patient. Vivek Muppalla frames the opportunity as arithmetic rather than automation: healthcare has always been rationed by scarcity — "not enough clinicians, not enough time, not enough money, and hence the word triage" — so once clinically safe conversation gets cheap enough, "you don't have to have calls just for the sickest 5%, but you can call everyone." Their reported scale is 200M+ clinical interactions across 60+ health systems with zero significant safety incidents and 8.5/10 patient satisfaction, and the calls do real clinical work: confirming vitals back to the patient, resolving a spelled-out drug name, detecting that a medication the patient was told to stop was still taken (and deferring to the primary care doctor rather than advising), and escalating to a live nurse on shortness of breath. Three engineering consequences follow from the stakes. Recognition errors dominate apparent reasoning errors, so the ASR is [conditioned on conversation context and domain vocabulary](../concepts/condition-asr-on-conversation-context-and-domain-vocabulary.md) rather than bought off the shelf. A single model is treated as an unacceptable single point of failure, so the system [runs parallel specialist models behind a speak-up gate](../concepts/run-parallel-specialist-models-with-a-speak-up-gate.md) with background verifiers on tool calls. And accuracy is measured against consequence, so evals are [sized to the error rate the consequence demands](../concepts/size-eval-suites-to-the-error-rate-the-consequence-demands.md) and graded on the harm scale used for human clinicians. The same expert-in-the-loop principle that governs appeal submission reappears here in a different form: rather than reviewing each output, 7,000+ trained clinicians run continuous evaluation of the system, and the agent escalates to a human when the clinical situation warrants it.

Ufonia's Dora occupies the same patient-facing tier from the opposite direction — inbound-quality assurance rather than outbound scale — and supplies the release process that Hippocratic AI's numbers presuppose. Jared Joselowitz's product phones patients for post-op follow-ups and pre-op checks (~200,000 real clinical calls across 20 UK hospitals, contracted to reach a million patients in two years), and because it asks about symptoms and gives advice it "is what's called a medical device and therefore it has to of course be regulated." Regulation, he says, reduces to three questions — what does your software do, what could go wrong, how do you ensure it doesn't — and the second is where healthcare diverges from ordinary software: the hazard list is written with clinicians up front (missing a red flag such as sudden vision loss or severe pain, hallucinating an answer to a medical question, ignoring patient distress; "20, 30, 40" documented hazards). From there the standard de-risking loop is unusable, so the team [replaces ship-and-rollback with hazard-first simulation](../concepts/replace-ship-and-rollback-with-hazard-first-simulation.md) and treats simulation results as evidence only after they [validate the simulated patient and the judge against humans](../concepts/validate-the-simulated-user-and-the-judge.md) — a patient-and-public-involvement study for simulator realism, and a 240-case, 10-clinician, 10-specialty panel for the judge, where near-perfect sensitivity matters more than F1 because "you would rather overcall hazards that aren't there than undercall hazards that are there." The metric that drives improvement is likewise clinician-owned: prompts are [optimized against a cost matrix](../concepts/optimize-prompts-against-an-asymmetric-cost-matrix.md) in which a missed red flag is catastrophic and an over-called one costs the patient "a couple extra questions." Two framings travel outside medicine: autonomy is rationed by evidence ("as the system gets more evidence, you can give it more independence"), and the artifact that satisfies a regulator is the audit trail, not the model — "you don't ship the model, you ship the evidence." Both talks converge on clinicians staying in the loop as *evaluators* rather than per-output reviewers: 7,000+ running continuous evaluation at Hippocratic AI, and 10 specialties calibrating the judge plus supervision at every rollout stage at Ufonia.

Onlay returns the topic to the administrative tier that revenue-cycle AI opened, but with the execution layer built out. Vasant Kearney frames the objective the same way — drive down the cost of interacting with insurance, or improve the patient experience, because insurance shapes how the patient experiences the whole process — and then asks what it takes to let an agent actually *act* across eligibility, treatment authorization, documents, claim submission, and payment. His answer is that the domain already published the constraint: [ground agent actions in an existing domain transaction standard](../concepts/ground-agent-actions-in-an-existing-domain-transaction-standard.md). X12 belongs in the harness next to memory, tools, checks, permissions, handoffs, and evals, because LLMs "thrive … when they're confined" the way COBOL or TypeScript confines, because a ~50-step claims objective propagates errors and needs "something grounded that can be rejected to," and because the standard is public — "this is not my schema," so both an agent doing research and a new engineer can look up every transaction. The reframe that follows is the talk's sharpest: a phone call to a payer *is* an X12 transaction (identifying the patient is a 270), as is a portal session or an imaging attachment (275), so channel choice stops being an architectural fork and becomes a routing detail over one internal representation. But conformance is not truth. The payer's portal, phone system, X12 layer, and FHIR layer are often built by different teams or different contracted companies, so you must [treat every external system of record as non-authoritative](../concepts/treat-every-external-system-of-record-as-non-authoritative.md): they contradict each other, and they can also agree on the wrong answer — all three confirm coverage, you treat the patient, and the claim is denied because the patient was not covered at that time. Everything therefore normalizes into "your own internal semi-correct X12. Correct until downstream evidence proves it otherwise." This is the administrative mirror of the patient-facing talks above: where Ufonia and Hippocratic AI ration autonomy by *hazard evidence*, Onlay rations belief by *downstream evidence*, and both refuse to let a passing check stand in for a verified outcome. The deployment constraints follow the same enterprise logic — agent memory lives in a database rather than on local disk the way Claude Code and Codex do it, so [tiered database memory with an escape hatch](../concepts/give-enterprise-agents-tiered-database-memory-with-an-escape-hatch.md) replaces file-based memory — and the closing posture, "AI pilled and AI skeptical" at once, includes a cost rule that matters in a domain whose whole justification is cost reduction: do not spend an overpowered model on routine work done a thousand times a day.

## Key Concepts

- [Ground Agent Actions in an Existing Domain Transaction Standard](../concepts/ground-agent-actions-in-an-existing-domain-transaction-standard.md) - X12 already encodes the provider-payer contract for every claim-lifecycle stage, so it confines the agent, gives a 50-step pipeline a rejection point, and makes a phone call and an EDI feed the same transaction.
- [Treat Every External System of Record as Non-Authoritative](../concepts/treat-every-external-system-of-record-as-non-authoritative.md) - payer surfaces built by different contractors disagree and can jointly be wrong, so the internal record is a working belief that downstream evidence can overturn.
- [Give Enterprise Agents Tiered Database Memory With an Escape Hatch](../concepts/give-enterprise-agents-tiered-database-memory-with-an-escape-hatch.md) - enterprise deployments cannot use coding agents' local-disk memory, and organization/user memory that predicts repetitive work also risks steering the user.
- [Replace Ship-and-Rollback With Hazard-First Simulation When Errors Are Irreversible](../concepts/replace-ship-and-rollback-with-hazard-first-simulation.md) - patient-facing AI cannot be A/B tested or rolled back, so hazards are enumerated with clinicians, run in simulation, and autonomy is earned stage by stage against evidence.
- [Validate the Simulated User and the Judge Before Trusting a Simulation](../concepts/validate-the-simulated-user-and-the-judge.md) - a simulated-patient eval loop is two models, and each needs human validation (realism study; multi-specialty clinician panel) before its verdicts count as a safety case.
- [Optimize Prompts Against an Asymmetric Cost Matrix, Not Flat Accuracy](../concepts/optimize-prompts-against-an-asymmetric-cost-matrix.md) - clinicians define the cost matrix rather than the prompt, so the optimizer maximizes sensitivity to red flags instead of a dataset average.
- [Condition ASR on Conversation Context and Domain Vocabulary](../concepts/condition-asr-on-conversation-context-and-domain-vocabulary.md) - clinical entity recognition (drug names, vitals, addresses) needs conversation and domain conditioning, because mishearing masquerades as reasoning failure.
- [Run Parallel Specialist Models Behind a Speak-Up Gate](../concepts/run-parallel-specialist-models-with-a-speak-up-gate.md) - patient-facing systems need model redundancy without a latency penalty, plus verifiers on the actions they take.
- [Size Eval Suites to the Error Rate the Consequence Demands](../concepts/size-eval-suites-to-the-error-rate-the-consequence-demands.md) - in care delivery a 1% error is a daily count of harmed patients, which sets both the accuracy bar and the test-suite size.
- [Revenue Cycle AI Targets Administrative Friction](../concepts/revenue-cycle-ai-targets-administrative-friction.md) - healthcare AI can create direct value by reducing payer-provider communication friction, denials, rework, and delayed payment.
- [Prevent Revenue Cycle Denials Upstream](../concepts/prevent-revenue-cycle-denials-upstream.md) - end-to-end workflow data can turn late denial patterns into earlier checks and corrections.
- [Expert-Reviewed GenAI Appeals Beat Off-the-Shelf Letters](../concepts/expert-reviewed-genai-appeals-beat-off-the-shelf-letters.md) - clinical appeal generation needs evidence assembly, local quality standards, and expert approval.

## Open Questions

- Which revenue cycle steps can be fully automated, and which should remain expert-reviewed because payer policy or clinical necessity is disputed?
- How should providers measure whether payer-side AI denial automation is increasing preventable administrative burden?
- Which clinical conversations can an AI agent safely conduct end to end, and which must escalate to a clinician by policy rather than by model judgment?
- What does a harm-graded rubric look like outside medicine, where no established human grading scale exists to borrow?
- How large does a hazard list have to be before it counts as complete, given that both talks enumerate hazards with clinicians but neither describes a method for finding the hazard nobody thought of?
- When payer surfaces disagree, what decides which one an agent acts on before the claim adjudicates — a learned per-payer reliability model, a conservative default, or a human? Onlay reports that idiosyncrasies are learned but does not describe how they are encoded.
