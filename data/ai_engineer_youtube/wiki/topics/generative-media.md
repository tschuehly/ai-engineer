# Generative Media

## Overview

Generative media covers the model and product patterns behind image, video, and other audiovisual generation. The current sources frame large-scale media systems as more than a diffusion backbone: data curation, learned latent representations, denoising architecture, sampling procedure, step distillation, explicit controls, and interactive state all shape whether the model is practical and steerable. Compared with language models, media diffusion can exploit spatial or temporal topology, bidirectional attention, perceptual frequency weighting, and coarse-to-fine generation instead of strict token-by-token causality. Media evaluation has the same perceptual constraint: codecs, internet artifacts, FID-style scores, and object-count metrics may not match what humans notice or what a creative user values. Media products also need retrieval over their own outputs: once a studio or campaign generates many personalized variants, finding the right asset and preserving provenance can become as important as creating another image. World models add a different bar from passive generation: when users act inside generated environments, the system needs memory, consistency, controllability, and live prompting so the world remains coherent under interaction.

Serving cost is its own discipline once a media model is good enough to use. NVIDIA's view is that diffusion latency comes mainly from the default 20-50 denoising steps and that the diffusion serving stack is less mature than the autoregressive LLM/VLM stack, so optimization ideas are borrowed from LLMs and adapted. Three levers are presented as additive and stackable rather than mutually exclusive: quantization is the easiest lever but pays off less than in LLMs because diffusion is attention-heavy; caching skips recomputing latent regions that barely change between denoising steps; and step distillation trains a same-size student to match teacher quality in far fewer steps (50 down to 4, 8, or one), the most impactful lever and currently the only path to good-quality real-time generation. Teams are advised to start with quantization, add caching and multi-GPU/context parallelism if needed, and finish with distillation, which together can reach the 10x-200x speedup that produced near-real-time video on a single Blackwell B200.

Generative media also includes music: Google DeepMind's Lyria 3 now generates full songs with lyrics and ships as two variants — a clip model and a Pro full-length-song model. How media generation is invoked is itself a product pattern: rather than a single static prompt, a real-time conversational model can expose the media generator as a tool, gather the creative brief through dialogue, and then call it — demonstrated by a "jukebox" where Gemini Live calls Lyria on request. This composes a native sound-to-sound interaction model with a dedicated media-generation model through ordinary tool use, and generalizes to image and video endpoints behind an agent.

Media generation is not only diffusion pixels — it can be an agent-orchestrated pipeline that turns structured domain analysis into narrated video. TNG's chess channel produces daily puzzle-explainer videos fully unattended: pull source games nightly, have an agent analyze them (grounded by chess-engine tools), emit "a special format" as a machine-readable intermediate artifact, render that to video, and auto-upload. Two design choices generalize. First, decouple reasoning from rendering through the intermediate format, and let the agent own presentation decisions — which squares to highlight, which arrows to draw, whether a move is "brilliant" — while narration is generated with emotion baked in (ElevenLabs V3 audio tags like `[excited]` in the script rather than post-production). Second, manage quality by sampling rather than gating: at ~20-30 cents per video and a ~1-in-20 error rate (usually a skipped final tool call producing a missed checkmate), the operator shifts from watch-each-first to publish-then-take-down, treating errors as recoverable and as learning signal. The positioning lesson is an anti-slop one — because the input is real human games the product can personalize commentary of ordinary players' games (a long tail streamers won't cover), and it deliberately refuses view-count gimmicks (exploding kings) to optimize for domain quality.

Choosing a media model is its own discipline, and Pruna's Bertrand Charpentier reframes it as a quality–efficiency tradeoff rather than a leaderboard rank. Because image and video quality is driven by compute, leaderboard winners are usually the slowest and most expensive: the same 26,000-battle image evaluation cost 20 days of compute, $5,000, and ~400 marathons of energy on a slow model versus 7 hours and $265 on a fast compressed one. Since quality scores cluster (3–4 models within ~1,100–1,200 Elo) while efficiency can differ ~20x, evaluating on a Pareto front — ideally with a use-case metric such as text rendering — surfaces small specialized "performance models" over large foundation models. The same talk sharpens the perceptual-metric caution: a CLIP-score ranking of 8 models flipped across datasets with tiny between-model variation, so a metric is trustworthy only once you understand what it measures, match it to the use case, and run several. It also corroborates the diffusion-serving recipe from the efficiency-company side, adding per-module quantization and pruning to the quantization/caching/step-distillation stack and confirming Flux 2 / Flux work with Black Forest Labs to cut 20–50 denoiser steps to ~4–20x fewer.

Krea's Krea 2 talk is the wiki's first end-to-end account of building an image foundation model, and it turns the standing "curate data before tuning internals" claim into a procedure. Sangwu Lee's framing is that once the architecture is locked — a latent diffusion transformer — data is what remains: "you lock in your architecture and then… a lot of work just goes into like just feeding the model like what it wants." The pipeline is roughly 30-40 in-house filters, and four of its moves generalize. First, order everything by cost per sample, because the corpus is two to ten billion images: pHash/MD5 deduplication first, embedding methods (SSCD, SigLIP) only on the reduced set, and any judgment you want at corpus scale obtained by prompting a large VLM and distilling it into a SigLIP-sized classifier — the same trick Essential Web used on the text side with a ~500M-parameter student. Second, treat a captioner's *systematic* omissions as a data-selection problem: a painting photographed on a white wall is fine data, but because captions consistently omit the frame and the wall, the model learns to hang every painting it generates, so the class gets dropped or undersampled rather than re-captioned. Third, repurpose interpretability tooling as labeling infrastructure — an SAE trained on a vision model yields an unsupervised tagging system whose features (watermarks, signatures, border artifacts) become filter or oversampling criteria you never had to label. Fourth, check coverage as well as quality, by PageRank-ranking Wikipedia concepts and searching the corpus for the top percentile, a technique from the original CLIP paper that Lee reports without claiming a measured payoff.

Two of Krea's decisions are positioning rather than technique, and they explain the rest. The production models — "Chat GPT-2" and Nano Banana Pro in the captions — buy near-flawless output by collapsing their distribution: "the easiest and most reliable way to like render a person is render the most boring average person that exists and then like put it in a center frame," so "burning skull" returns competent, near-identical images every time. That is the right model when the user knows what they want (a poster, a birthday card) and the wrong one for a studio that "[doesn't] quite know what you want yet." Choosing the diversity end then constrains curation: generic aesthetic and image-quality scores cannot be used as filters, because low-resolution CRT aesthetics are someone's style. The second decision is refusing AI-generated training images entirely — distillation is "a shortcut" to a good model, but "synthetic data is like so sticky" that you inherit another lab's aesthetic, which for a company selling stylistic range means importing the competitor's mode collapse. The training pipeline itself, by contrast, is borrowed wholesale from LLM practice — 256→1K resolution curriculum, mid-training and SFT to mold the distribution, preference optimization on A/B pairs, GRPO-style RL against reward servers for text rendering and anatomy, plus a trained prompt expander that is "almost essential" because long prompts sit closer to the training distribution. Lee's stated reason for borrowing is reuse of LLM kernels and literature, and he notes the resulting stack (autoregressive prompt expander feeding a diffusion encoder) is inverting back toward DALL-E 2's shape.

The infrastructure half of Krea 2, presented separately by Gabriel Jorge Menezes, adds the operational price of that curation-and-post-training story: the model was pre-trained from scratch with no base checkpoint on thousands of InfiniBand-connected GPUs, and the run's binding constraint was uptime rather than method. Two open checkpoints came out of it — a raw pre-trained one for others to post-train, and a "turbo" post-trained one that returns an image in under a second — and the same model serves production on krea.ai. The generative-media-specific detail is the serving asymmetry it reveals: a diffusion transformer is small enough to serve on one node, unlike large LLMs that need multi-node inference, so Krea's serving tier runs happily on GPUs that are hot or intermittently faulty and can be evicted to external providers whenever a training run claims the cluster. Media-model economics therefore look different from LLM economics on both sides — the training side demands homogeneous healthy hardware and a filesystem that can absorb terabyte checkpoints, while the serving side is unusually forgiving about what it runs on. One cross-check on the research talk: the 256→1K resolution curriculum Lee describes as a method choice shows up in Menezes's dashboards as tensor core utilization climbing with resolution, which is independent confirmation that the curriculum changes arithmetic intensity and not just what the model learns.

Games are becoming a generative-media surface in their own right. Meta's AI-game team (Danielle An & David Hoe) reports that once everyone can vibe-code a platformer, the differentiators are the traditional ones — aesthetics, cohesion, playtesting — plus two AI-native moves. The first is asset cohesion: iterate to a single key-art image and feed it back as an anchor so the model holds one art style (and even the implied gameplay) across a session, shortening the distance between a kid's prompt output and a professionally made game that feels like one universe. The second is the *runtime LLM* — an LLM running during play rather than at build time — driving NPCs that make emergent, unscripted decisions (steal, block, kick) so every playthrough is unique, and acting as a "game master" that personalizes difficulty per player (e.g. carrying a coordination-challenged player through a co-op session). This genre is newly viable only because inference is now fast and cheap enough; the caveat is that the technology alone doesn't make the product better, and at platform scale it surfaces token-economy profitability and content-safety problems for runtime-generated images and content.

Reactor's Ahmed Ahres puts a different axis under all of the above: not what the model can generate, but whether the output can be acted on while it is being produced. His claim is that [real-time generation changes the medium rather than the latency](../concepts/real-time-generation-changes-the-medium-not-the-latency.md) — batch video is "a slot machine. You cannot change it," and "a generated video… is still a recording" — argued through two cases where a latency change created industries rather than speeding one up (maps to GPS, and film to the viewfinder, which is why "Instagram and TikTok would not exist"). That reframes the topic's serving chapter: NVIDIA's and Pruna's quantization/caching/step-distillation stack is what makes a single generation fast, while Ahres is after the affordance that appears past the threshold — output you can steer mid-generation, summarized as "instant feedback is the ultimate level of control," demonstrated by prompting a cat into a scene that is still generating. His narrowing of "world model" is deliberately unglamorous — not Gaussian splatting and not a longer clip, but video that is infinite, interactive, and fast enough to steer — and it produces a usable taxonomy: [pick a real-time world-model class by what the user steers](../concepts/pick-a-realtime-world-model-class-by-what-the-user-steers.md), whether that is the scene through prompts (Veo/Sora-shaped but live), a character inside a generated world (Genie 3-shaped, reaching into robotics training data and education), or a live avatar, which he says plainly "hasn't actually been cracked." What users ship on the first two classes is broader than games: vote-driven interactive livestreams, medical and cooking simulation, and real-time video-to-video VFX, all still bounded by model quality he does not oversell.

The engineering consequence is that none of the batch playbook carries over, and it fails on three axes at once: [serve real-time video as stateful streaming sessions, not batch jobs](../concepts/serve-realtime-video-as-stateful-streaming-sessions.md) — pixels stream rather than files return, every session is long-lived with model state that must be maintained, and a sub-100 ms bar puts GPUs near users instead of in one region, because without worldwide compute "the experiences are not real time anymore and it breaks completely the medium." Session memory is also where this model class currently fails in public, which strengthens the wiki's existing world-model page from the platform side: the Genie 3 symptom users notice is that "the character can look back and then will not remember what's going on." And the honest gap is measurement — [real-time world-model evaluation is still human judgment](../concepts/realtime-world-model-evaluation-is-still-human-judgment.md), since per-frame "fidelity is easy. It's just like pixels," while consistency across a steered session is unsolved "including, by the way, DeepMind." This topic's perceptual-metric chapter (FID-style scores, CLIP-score instability, personalized aesthetic classifiers) is entirely about single artifacts, so it does not reach the trajectory defects that decide whether an interactive world is usable.

Arturo Nunez, building a conventional (non-generated) game engine after almost ten years at Unity, supplies the outside check on that chapter — and he concedes the framing while pricing it. Generated world models are "another kind of branch of engines or tools… being generated on the fly," and "that's going to be a different medium even if we call them video games," which is Reactor's medium claim from someone with no platform to sell. What he adds is the bar the incumbent medium already meets: games "have to render 60 frames per second. And doing that at 4K resolutions in real time with a world model, I think it's still far away. And on top of that… simulating physics," which he calls "very difficult" while still describing the direction as exciting. The two arguments are separable and both hold — a new category can be genuinely new and still be years from the frame-rate, resolution, and simulation budget a 2026 game engine takes for granted. His own product is the other answer to the same steerability question, and it is entirely non-generative at runtime: the user describes intent in the vocabulary games are taught in, an assistant translates that into tags on assets, and deterministic systems do the rest.

Todd Fisher's talking guitar is the small, unfunded end of the same question, and it is useful precisely because the model layer is trivial: Piper or Apple text-to-speech, Whisper, and "any local model" — "it doesn't really matter what LLM" — behind a microphone-to-strings loop that let an audience member ask the guitar what reality is and hear it answer note by note. Everything he describes as hard is the non-model signal path. Slicing a synthesized sentence into playable words defeated the obvious heuristic, because [energy-gap segmentation assumes silence between words and running speech often has none](../concepts/segment-synthesized-speech-with-multiple-cues-and-manual-repair.md); a sonority-peak syllabifier keyed on vowel nuclei fails on different inputs, so the two combine, and the last few boundaries were cheaper to drag by hand in a small editor than to chase with a third heuristic. Singing meant YIN pitch detection on each fretted note, an ADSR-shaped synthesized tone, a vocoder, and a "clarity" lever mixing tone against voice. Two structural decisions generalize past the novelty. First, delivery: the whole thing is a [JUCE plugin dropped into Logic Pro like any other effect](../concepts/ship-ai-audio-features-as-plugins-inside-the-host-daw.md), so the host supplies routing, chaining, and the backing track, and the DAW is "effectively your IDE but for musicians and music producers." Second, and the sharpest contrast with the pages above: [separating what generated audio says from when it plays](../concepts/separate-what-generated-audio-says-from-when-it-plays.md) buys steerability without any of Reactor's infrastructure. The performer cannot change what the guitar is about to say, but owns its rhythm, emphasis, and melody, and because every note only triggers audio that already exists, generation latency never enters the live path at all. The same logic decides the singing stack: WORLD pitch-shifting of VocalSet samples is "a very heavy process… I can't really do that live," so the transform is [pre-baked offline and each fret indexes a finished sample](../concepts/pre-bake-transforms-too-heavy-for-the-realtime-path.md) — the cheapest of this topic's three answers to a real-time budget, paid for in coverage (five vowels, "not quite your opera singer") and available only because a fretboard is an enumerable control surface.

Keegan McCallum (uRun) supplies the number that the rest of the real-time chapter argues around, and it is a price rather than a metric. His claim is that the field has two axes and only one of them gets discussed: quality has an obvious lineage (Will Smith eating spaghetti in 2023, Sora, Sora 2, Seedance "so photorealistic" this year), while [the efficiency axis is where the product decisions now live](../concepts/track-the-efficiency-axis-in-generative-video-not-only-quality.md) — real-time output sits at "about the same quality as the frontier models were last year" and costs roughly a hundredth of a minutes-long generation, with the residual gap showing up specifically as motion. That converts the unit of account: $10 buys about three hours of continuously generated video and $50 about fifteen, against a batch loop that costs "about $10 a minute to try and get the shot that you want." It also explains the supply side this topic has been tracking one technique at a time — Helios, the model uRun serves, is a distill of an open 14B video model, and "at least 40 models with real-time capabilities and long horizon generation capabilities" shipped this year, so the distillation and serving levers on the pages above are now a field-wide movement rather than a lab result. Two consequences are worth separating from the vendor pitch. First, a workflow: because the cheap tier is representative but not final, [steer live and then render the chosen shot at full fidelity](../concepts/steer-in-real-time-then-render-the-chosen-shot-at-full-fidelity.md) — sub-second steering replaces the prompt-and-retry gamble, and the same live surface doubles as a way to watch an agent generate rather than waiting on its output. Second, an audience argument: [text-first AI interfaces exclude people who don't think in text](../concepts/text-first-interfaces-exclude-people-who-dont-think-in-text.md), and a visual medium is only affordable as an *interface* — not as content production — once a day of continuous interaction costs $50. His serving section is the same conclusion Reactor reaches, arrived at independently and stated more concretely (WebRTC with ICE and TURN, multi-model streaming pipelines, per-frame control synchronization), and it ends on the line that summarizes the chapter: "the models are here and the frontier is really in how we serve them."

Reelful's Ekaterina Deyneka is the first source here whose product deliberately does not generate the footage, and that inversion is what makes it useful to this topic. Her starting observation is behavioral rather than technical — nearly every hand in the room had recorded video at the conference and almost none had posted any of it, because "video editing is hard… it's tedious and it's largely still manual." The engineering claim is that an agentic video editor is not a new category of system: [it is the agentic app-builder architecture with the artifact swapped](../concepts/reuse-the-agentic-app-builder-shape-for-non-code-artifacts.md) — prompt UI (here media plus prompt), a sandbox spun up on a remote machine, "an agent with tools and skills," a codebase replaced by a video composition, an app preview replaced by a rendered video. What lets the substitution work is the composition format: Remotion, "an open-source framework to create videos as code, as React code," chosen because "agents are really good at writing code." That is the same argument Nori makes for HTML, arrived at independently in the medium where it should be hardest, and it buys a second thing the HTML page never claims — a mechanical failure mode, so a verification layer can check that "everything will be rendered" and send the agent back to fix compositions that will not, which is a coding agent's compile-or-retry loop applied to a video. Domain taste enters as skills rather than architecture: cut rules for selecting the best moments, font pairs, when b-roll helps — "this is where our taste and craft live."

The other half of her talk pushes back on this topic's generation-first framing. Reelful expects "your real life, your personal content," and Deyneka argues [editing real material constrains an agent more than generating new material does](../concepts/editing-real-material-is-harder-for-agents-than-generating.md): "if the agent has a blank canvas, it can do whatever they can. But in the editing case, the agent has to figure out which moments are the best… what to omit," from footage that "can be messy or incomplete," against an absolute polish bar — "ideally the viewers of this content don't get if it is like AI or human edited." A generation model that draws a bad frame redraws it; an editor with no good take of the moment cannot, which is why the pipeline leads with media understanding and speech transcription and why the first skill listed is a selection rule rather than a production rule. The last problem she names is delivery, which she calls "even maybe a bigger problem" than the pipeline: [hide the whole workflow behind templates and a manual editor](../concepts/package-agentic-pipelines-behind-templates-and-a-manual-editor.md) — mobile-first because consumers capture and post while "driving, walking or maybe lifting weights," directional templates (speak-to-camera, add b-rolls, voiceover) so the product works "even without any prompt," a creative plan surfaced for approval before the sandbox starts, and a conventional editor afterward so a near-miss costs a drag rather than a regeneration. Evidence is modest and honest about it: clips she assembled with the agent and posted herself, from an early product that shipped its second version at the conference.

## Key Concepts

- [Reuse the Agentic App-Builder Architecture for Non-Code Artifacts](../concepts/reuse-the-agentic-app-builder-shape-for-non-code-artifacts.md) - prompt, sandbox, agent with skills, artifact, render — the same stack builds a video composition once the artifact has a code-shaped format and a render check.
- [Editing Real Material Constrains an Agent More Than Generating From Scratch](../concepts/editing-real-material-is-harder-for-agents-than-generating.md) - selection and omission over messy, unchosen footage against an absolute polish bar, versus a blank canvas the agent can define its own success on.
- [Hide an Agentic Pipeline Behind Templates and a Manual Editor](../concepts/package-agentic-pipelines-behind-templates-and-a-manual-editor.md) - mobile surface, zero-prompt directional templates, an approval gate on intent, and a familiar editor for the last mile.
- [Inference Tolerates Degraded GPUs That Training Cannot](../concepts/inference-tolerates-degraded-gpus-that-training-cannot.md) - single-node diffusion serving keeps running on hardware a synchronous training run has to eject, which is why media serving can be relocated cheaply.
- [Track the Efficiency Axis in Generative Video, Not Only Quality](../concepts/track-the-efficiency-axis-in-generative-video-not-only-quality.md) - real-time output is last year's frontier quality at ~1/100th the cost, which changes the unit from dollars per clip to dollars per session-hour.
- [Steer in Real Time, Then Render the Chosen Shot at Full Fidelity](../concepts/steer-in-real-time-then-render-the-chosen-shot-at-full-fidelity.md) - explore live at sub-second latency, then commit the result through a slower high-fidelity model instead of paying per failed batch attempt.
- [Text-First AI Interfaces Exclude People Who Don't Think in Text](../concepts/text-first-interfaces-exclude-people-who-dont-think-in-text.md) - cheap continuous generation makes a visual medium affordable as an interface, not just as output.
- [Real-Time Generation Changes the Medium, Not Just the Latency](../concepts/real-time-generation-changes-the-medium-not-the-latency.md) - when output can be steered mid-generation the category changes, argued from GPS and the camera viewfinder rather than from benchmarks.
- [Pick a Real-Time World-Model Class by What the User Steers](../concepts/pick-a-realtime-world-model-class-by-what-the-user-steers.md) - prompted scenes, controllable characters, and live avatars are three different products with three different maturities.
- [Serve Real-Time Video as Stateful Streaming Sessions, Not Batch Jobs](../concepts/serve-realtime-video-as-stateful-streaming-sessions.md) - streaming pixels, per-session model memory, and sub-100 ms global routing are why real-time is separate infrastructure.
- [Real-Time World-Model Evaluation Is Still Human Judgment](../concepts/realtime-world-model-evaluation-is-still-human-judgment.md) - fidelity is measurable per frame; consistency across a steered session has no automated eval yet.
- [Separate What Generated Audio Says From When It Plays](../concepts/separate-what-generated-audio-says-from-when-it-plays.md) - slice the utterance and let note events fire the units, so the model owns content and a performer owns timing, pitch, and expression.
- [Pre-Bake Transforms Too Heavy for the Real-Time Path](../concepts/pre-bake-transforms-too-heavy-for-the-realtime-path.md) - when the control input is enumerable, run the expensive transform once per value offline and make the runtime a lookup.
- [Segment Synthesized Speech With Multiple Cues, Then Let a Human Fix the Rest](../concepts/segment-synthesized-speech-with-multiple-cues-and-manual-repair.md) - energy gaps plus sonority peaks get close, and a drag-to-edit UI is cheaper than a third heuristic.
- [Ship AI Audio Features as Plugins Inside the Host DAW](../concepts/ship-ai-audio-features-as-plugins-inside-the-host-daw.md) - build into the host that already owns the audio graph and the user's workflow instead of shipping a standalone app.
- [Anchor Generative Asset Cohesion on One Key-Art Image](../concepts/anchor-generative-asset-cohesion-on-one-key-art-image.md) - a single key-art image anchors art style and gameplay across a session so generated assets feel like one universe.
- [Drive Game NPCs and Difficulty With Runtime LLMs](../concepts/drive-game-npcs-and-difficulty-with-runtime-llms.md) - an LLM running during play drives emergent NPC behavior and personalizes difficulty, newly viable because inference is fast/cheap.
- [Design LLM-native interactive experiences around narrative state, not numeric mechanics](../concepts/design-llm-native-experiences-around-narrative-state-not-numbers.md) - an LLM-native game tracks mood, NPCs, and generated worlds as narrative state instead of HP and dice.
- [Stack Additive Diffusion Optimizations for Real-Time Generation](../concepts/stack-additive-diffusion-optimizations-for-real-time-generation.md) - quantization, caching, and step distillation are incremental, combinable levers ordered from easy to most impactful.
- [Quantize Diffusion Models for Memory and Throughput Despite Attention Heaviness](../concepts/quantize-diffusion-models-for-memory-and-throughput-despite-attention-heaviness.md) - the cheapest serving lever, with PTQ/QAT, static/dynamic, and pre-quantized-checkpoint choices.
- [Cache Unchanged Computation Between Diffusion Denoising Steps](../concepts/cache-unchanged-computation-between-diffusion-denoising-steps.md) - inter-step similarity, not per-token reuse, is the cacheable signal in diffusion.
- [Curate generative-media data before tuning model internals](../concepts/curate-generative-media-data-before-tuning-model-internals.md) - data quality can dominate model and optimizer tweaks for large-scale media generation.
- [Reliability and Stylistic Range Are Opposite Model Positions](../concepts/reliability-and-stylistic-range-are-opposite-model-positions.md) - production models buy consistency with mode collapse, so pick the end of the dial by whether the user knows what they want.
- [Refuse AI-Generated Training Images When Style Is the Product](../concepts/refuse-ai-generated-images-when-style-is-the-product.md) - synthetic aesthetic is sticky, so distillation imports another lab's look along with the free quality.
- [Filter Training Images Your Captioner Systematically Mis-Describes](../concepts/filter-images-your-captioner-systematically-mis-describes.md) - a consistent caption omission becomes an unconditional generation artifact.
- [Order Billion-Scale Data Filters by Cost Per Sample](../concepts/order-billion-scale-data-filters-by-cost-per-sample.md) - hashes before embeddings, and a big VLM's judgment distilled into a classifier cheap enough to sweep a billion images.
- [Use Sparse Autoencoder Features as an Unsupervised Data Tagger](../concepts/use-sparse-autoencoder-features-as-an-unsupervised-data-tagger.md) - SAE features act as off-the-shelf tags for watermarks, artifacts, and style properties you never labeled.
- [Run the LLM Post-Training Ladder on Diffusion Models](../concepts/run-the-llm-post-training-ladder-on-diffusion-models.md) - resolution curriculum, mid-training, SFT, preference optimization, GRPO-style RL, and a trained prompt expander.
- [Train image and video diffusion models in learned latent spaces](../concepts/train-image-and-video-diffusion-models-in-learned-latent-spaces.md) - autoencoder latents make high-resolution and video diffusion tractable while preserving useful topology.
- [Use guidance to trade diffusion sample diversity for conditional quality](../concepts/use-guidance-to-trade-diffusion-sample-diversity-for-conditional-quality.md) - guidance is a sampling-time lever for prompt adherence, quality, diversity, and artifact risk.
- [Distill diffusion models to reduce sampling steps](../concepts/distill-diffusion-models-to-reduce-sampling-steps.md) - diffusion distillation primarily reduces iterative denoising latency.
- [Expose explicit control signals for generative media models](../concepts/expose-explicit-control-signals-for-generative-media-models.md) - media products need controls beyond text, such as camera motion, masks, and depth.
- [Ground generated media with current search context](../concepts/ground-generated-media-with-current-search-context.md) - retrieval can make generated images depend on current facts and public context.
- [Evaluate generative media with perceptual metrics](../concepts/evaluate-generative-media-with-perceptual-metrics.md) - media scores should account for human-visible quality, not only easy embedding or object checks.
- [Account for compression artifacts in media model data and evals](../concepts/account-for-compression-artifacts-in-media-model-data-and-evals.md) - compressed internet media can shape both training data and metric behavior.
- [Personalize aesthetic evals with preference classifiers](../concepts/personalize-aesthetic-evals-with-preference-classifiers.md) - creative products may need user-specific taste models rather than one universal aesthetic score.
- [Design AI creative systems for generated-asset retrieval](../concepts/design-ai-creative-systems-for-generated-asset-retrieval.md) - generated-media products need indexing and search when output volume grows with personalization.
- [Interactive world models need memory, control, and live prompting](../concepts/interactive-world-models-need-memory-control-and-live-prompting.md) - generated environments need state and action consistency when users navigate inside them.

## Open Questions

- What does a "clean, well defined" composition check actually cover beyond render-validity — does a verification layer that only proves the artifact renders catch any of the taste failures (a bad cut, an unreadable caption over a bright frame) that decide whether the clip is postable?
- Which parts of editing judgment survive as written skills versus needing learned preference signal? Cut rules and font pairs are stated as skills, and no source here reports how well a prose rule transfers to footage its author never saw.
- How far does the single-node serving assumption hold as media models grow? Krea's degraded-hardware tolerance is a consequence of diffusion transformers fitting on one node, and no source here says at what model size media serving inherits the LLM multi-node constraints.
- How faithfully does a real-time preview predict the full-fidelity render of the same shot, and what has to be carried across the handoff — seed, conditioning frames, or a recorded control track — for the two-tier workflow to hold?
- Does the one-year quality lag between distilled real-time models and the frontier stay constant as both advance, or does the frontier's compute growth widen it?
- What is the actual gap between real-time world-model output and the 60 FPS / 4K / simulated-physics bar Nunez names, and is it a compute problem, an architecture problem, or a hybrid one where a conventional engine renders and a model supplies content?
- Which generative-media features are better served by decoupled playback of pre-generated units than by making generation fast enough to run live, and where does that substitution stop being convincing to users?
- What would an automated consistency eval for a steered generated session even measure — object permanence on return, physical plausibility, or drift from the initial conditioning image?
- Where should a real-time session's world memory live: inside the model's context window, in an external scene state the server re-conditions on, or both?
- Which data-curation checks best predict downstream image and video generation quality before expensive training runs?
- How much AI-generated data can a corpus tolerate before the borrowed aesthetic is detectable, and does the effect scale smoothly with mixing ratio or appear abruptly?
- How closely does a distilled SigLIP-sized filter agree with the large VLM it was distilled from, and which filter disagreements actually change the trained model?
- Does a PageRank-ranked concept coverage sweep measurably improve world knowledge, given that the source runs it without claiming a result?
- How should teams choose the compression ratio and topology of image or video latents for a target product and serving budget?
- Which controls should be trained into the base model, exposed through adapters, or handled by external editing workflows?
- Which generated-world failures should be evaluated as media quality problems, interaction-control problems, or long-horizon memory problems?
- How should perceptual media evals combine universal human-visible defects with user-specific aesthetic preferences?
- What retrieval and provenance metadata should generated-media tools attach before personalized asset volume becomes unmanageable?
- How should diffusion serving teams measure quality regression as they stack quantization, caching, and step distillation toward a real-time target?
