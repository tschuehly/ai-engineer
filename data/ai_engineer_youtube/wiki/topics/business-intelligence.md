# Business Intelligence

## Overview

GenBI applies agent and retrieval patterns to governed enterprise analytics. The safest starting point is not open-ended SQL over messy warehouses; it is a staged workflow that understands business context, retrieves certified reports or dashboards, and only then expands toward SQL generation or data pivoting when the system has enough metadata, expert validation, and access controls. This preserves trust because early answers point to assets the organization already knows how to govern.

Enterprise BI-agent programs should also be funded as incremental bets. Metadata enrichment, report discovery, data-owner discovery, and light pivoting can each be useful products before the full analytics copilot exists. In messy go-to-market systems, LLM extraction can also turn emails and CRM records into structured interaction graphs before teams attempt attribution or agentic analytics. That gives leadership visible business value, reusable benchmarks, and stop/go control while the team learns what accuracy, governance, and user expertise are required.

A second design pattern takes the opposite stance on autonomous SQL when the warehouse can be described. WorkOS's internal Studio lets any employee ask questions against Snowflake, Linear, and Notion in natural language; rather than routing to certified assets, it generates SQL directly and reaches a reported high hit rate using no RAG store at all, because LLMs read self-descriptive table schemas well when a hand-written context block encodes join quirks and entity representations. The durable output is not the answer but a "widget" the model writes once as declarative JavaScript that calls the data sources itself, so every later run is deterministic, cheap, and LLM-free and can be shared for self-serve use. Three things make the generation trustworthy: preflight sequencing with just-in-time tool-context injection, a layered prompt that distrusts the model's stale product knowledge in favor of primary sources, and execution-based query validation that catches valid SQL returning zero rows — most often from a forgotten active-status or non-deleted filter. The governance worry that a wrong query becomes accepted truth is answered by encoding those consistency filters into context plus the observation that residual errors tend to be large and obvious.

The same "trust the connected picture, not the isolated record" instinct carries into enterprise compliance and fraud analytics. Varsha Shah's research framework argues the most consequential fraud "exists between documents, not within them": a payroll record, vendor invoice, and tax filing can each pass independent validation while their *relationships* reveal the anomaly, so rule-based and document-level NLP systems structurally cannot catch cross-system patterns. Her architecture layers three components — a graph-based entity correlation engine that links employees, vendors, accounts, and filings across payroll/tax/procurement into one network ("what is connected?"); an adaptive probabilistic risk model that fuses anomaly strength, source reliability, and historic patterns into a confidence-weighted score to prioritize cases and *learns from audit outcomes* ("what is most likely genuine risk?"); and a cross-jurisdictional normalization layer harmonizing currencies, tax rules, and reporting periods so risk is judged consistently by regulatory context. The audit-feedback loop (confirmed frauds strengthen patterns, false positives refine scoring) reframes compliance from reactive validation to predictive governance — asking "what is likely to go wrong next" rather than "what went wrong." Reported on ~3M records across 4 jurisdictions: ~91% precision / 87% recall / F1 0.89, a 76% cut in false positives, and ~40% less manual audit effort.

Upside's GTM-analytics patterns extend the same trust discipline into subjective and definition-heavy questions. The recurring failure is not a crash but hallucination's "older sibling," a trust problem: asked to report revenue, an agent returns a wrong number "that looks exactly like being right." Two design patterns answer it. First, a **librarian** every agent consults before it queries — it holds documentation, a library of company knowledge items, and the schema of prior failed queries, and injects just-in-time definitions (the fiscal calendar is Feb–Apr, "pipeline" means stage two or later) so the agent stops silently guessing what a business term means and returns cited answers. This is the semantic-layer analogue of onboarding a new hire, and it complements WorkOS-style execution validation that catches valid-but-wrong SQL after the fact. Second, for questions with no empirically correct answer (multi-touch attribution), a **jury-and-judge** workflow: independent analysts each produce an evidence-cited opinion and a consensus judge weighs their reasoning quality rather than treating any as fact, escalating and expanding the jury when consensus is weak — better than one agent perseverating on a subjective call.

For regulated analytics the design question is where the boundary goes rather than how much freedom the agent gets. Krieger's version for financial services is a verified — "not immutable, but at least verified" — data substrate underneath, with the model free to "dive in and create just-in-time analyses or dashboards or workflows" on top, because "having all of that be totally free form is a recipe for confusion and is not what most companies in the financial services space want." The art is "finding that right cut line where you have verifiability and audit logging and data provenance here, but not in a way that constrains the kinds of applications that you can build on top." The corollary is uncomfortable for incumbents: the systems that already deliver verifiability are "almost by design not super flexible in terms of agentic workloads on top," so the agent layer usually cannot be bolted onto them.

The longest-running deployment in this material shifts the question from how to make talk-to-your-data trustworthy to what happens after it is. Snowflake's internal go-to-market assistant has answered over a million questions — roughly 40,000 a week — for about 6,000 sales users since launching, over 15 semantic views, 85 tables and 3,000 columns, and its operating lessons are mostly not about SQL. Trust is bought by scope rather than by certification: because a free-form box is judged on each user's first five answers and a bounced user is far harder to recover than a new one, the rule is to answer 50 questions at 95% rather than 100 at 70%, with coverage restored later — 60% of that system's data arrived after launch. The eval set that makes the tradeoff arguable is written from the documented business process before the data is connected, so unanswerable questions become a demand-ranked data backlog. Rollout is staged with a different exit question per phase, and the failure that surprises engineers comes after general availability, when only a fifth of the organization has tried the thing at all. Then the harder problem: talking to your data stops feeling remarkable within months and becomes the baseline users judge you against, so the roadmap has to climb — automation over integrations, then team-built tooling — while the question log itself, classified by LLM into a topic taxonomy, becomes the instrument that tells you what to build and which enablement content is missing.

A fourth shape appears when the buyer is a back-office operations team rather than a data platform group. Cloudflare's revenue-operations version starts from a diagnosis about people: analysts rebuild the same analysis in spreadsheets every week, dashboards "meet the needs of most teams, but not all of them," and the population that will never open a dashboard cannot be reached by any improvement to it. The response inverts the usual emphasis. Rather than making the agent better at querying, it makes the data harder to misread — filters, aggregations, and trend detection are engineered up front into tables organized by time, organizational slice, and metric, which the speaker says covers most requests and leaves raw data to the self-serve surface where a human is driving. The narrative is then written by three agents with separated objectives (draft, veracity check, tone rewrite that weights risks and opportunities equally) and pushed rather than published. Two things carry over to any recurring generated analysis: the business's real question set belongs inside the skill file, not only in the eval set, and where no ground-truth label exists, the honest acceptance procedure is a human reading every run for months — cheap at weekly cadence, unavailable at forty thousand questions a week, and worth writing down as a defect taxonomy before the reading stops.

## Key Concepts

- [Pre-Shape Analytics Data by Time, Slice, and Metric Before the Agent Reads It](../concepts/pre-shape-analytics-data-by-time-slice-and-metric-before-the-agent-reads-it.md) - move the filters, aggregations, and trend detection into the transform so the model's analysis stops wobbling between runs.
- [Split a Generated Narrative Into Drafter, Fact-Checker, and Tone Agents](../concepts/split-generated-narrative-into-drafter-checker-and-tone-agents.md) - a third stage whose objective is framing balance, which no veracity check measures.
- [Read Every Run for Months Before Trusting an Unevaluatable Narrative](../concepts/read-every-run-for-months-before-trusting-an-unevaluatable-narrative.md) - exhaustive manual reading as a defensible acceptance procedure when there is no ground truth.
- [Push the Narrative Because Dashboard Adoption Is Always Uneven](../concepts/push-the-narrative-because-dashboard-adoption-is-always-uneven.md) - some people will never open a dashboard, so the story has to be delivered and the dashboard demoted to drill-down.
- [Put the Business Question Set Inside the Skill File, Not Just the Schema](../concepts/put-the-business-question-set-inside-the-skill-file-not-just-the-schema.md) - the questions the business actually asks, shipped as context rather than only as a test.
- [Layer Ask, Push, and Self-Serve Because Teams Interface Differently](../concepts/layer-ask-push-and-self-serve-because-teams-interface-differently.md) - three delivery modes over one substrate, segmented by user preference rather than by capability.
- [Choose Quality Over Coverage Because the First Five Answers Decide Adoption](../concepts/choose-quality-over-coverage-because-the-first-five-answers-decide-adoption.md) - a free-form analytics surface is judged per user on a handful of early answers, so breadth bought with accuracy destroys adoption.
- [Write the Question Set From the Business Process Before the Data Is Connected](../concepts/write-the-question-set-from-the-business-process-before-the-data-is-connected.md) - derive the eval set from the job, not from what the warehouse currently reaches.
- [Stage the Internal Agent Roadmap From Answers to Automation to Team-Built Tooling](../concepts/stage-the-internal-agent-roadmap-from-answers-to-automation-to-team-built-tooling.md) - the four rungs that keep a data assistant ahead of its own baseline.
- [Classify the Assistant Question Log to Find Feature and Content Gaps](../concepts/classify-the-assistant-question-log-to-find-feature-and-content-gaps.md) - the question log is a demand map and a free source of negative labels.
- [Land the Data in One Governed Store So Agents Inherit Access Control](../concepts/land-the-data-in-one-governed-store-so-agents-inherit-access-control.md) - consolidation makes authorization a property agents inherit rather than implement.
- [Draw the Cut Line Between Verified Data and Free-Form Agent Analysis](../concepts/draw-the-cut-line-between-verified-data-and-free-form-agent-analysis.md) - just-in-time dashboards and analyses generated over a verified substrate, with provenance and audit logging below the line.
- [Preflight agents through a business-definitions librarian](../concepts/preflight-agents-through-a-business-definitions-librarian.md) - consult a definitions service before querying so the agent uses real fiscal/metric semantics instead of guessing.
- [Run a jury of analysts and a consensus judge for no-ground-truth questions](../concepts/run-a-jury-of-analysts-and-a-consensus-judge-for-no-ground-truth-questions.md) - answer subjective analytics questions (attribution) with independent analysts weighed by a consensus judge.
- [Fund enterprise AI through incremental productizable bets](../concepts/fund-enterprise-ai-through-incremental-productizable-bets.md) - keeps uncertain analytics-agent research tied to short, measurable business deliverables.
- [Start GenBI with certified assets before autonomous SQL](../concepts/start-genbi-with-certified-assets-before-autonomous-sql.md) - treats certified reports and dashboards as the first trusted answer surface.
- [Evaluate BI agents with real metadata and expert feedback](../concepts/evaluate-bi-agents-with-real-metadata-and-expert-feedback.md) - validates metadata, schema context, and answer quality against realistic enterprise data and expert review.
- [Extract enterprise interaction data into structured graphs](../concepts/extract-enterprise-interaction-data-into-structured-graphs.md) - converts messy outreach and CRM records into graph-backed attribution and analytics context.
- [Compile Natural-Language Analytics Into Reusable Deterministic Widgets](../concepts/compile-natural-language-analytics-into-reusable-deterministic-widgets.md) - the model writes a query tool once as declarative code so later runs are deterministic and shareable.
- [Validate Generated SQL by Execution Before Trusting It](../concepts/validate-generated-sql-by-execution-before-trusting-it.md) - run a generated query and confirm it returns data before freezing it into a dashboard.
- [Correlate entities across documents to surface cross-document risk](../concepts/correlate-entities-across-documents-to-surface-cross-document-risk.md) - fraud/compliance risk lives in relationships across payroll/tax/procurement records, invisible to per-document validation.
- [Learn risk scoring from audit feedback for predictive compliance](../concepts/learn-risk-scoring-from-audit-feedback-for-predictive-compliance.md) - a probabilistic risk model that prioritizes cases and improves from confirmed frauds and false positives, shifting compliance from reactive to predictive.

## Open Questions

- What happens to a burn-in-validated pipeline after the burn-in ends? Joyce's team read every run of the weekly summary for two to three months and then trusted it, with no reported exit criterion, replacement monitoring, defect taxonomy, or policy for re-running the burn-in after a model or prompt change. ([Joyce](../sources/20260826_Qw_tC68KKes.md), 11:41-11:55)
- What is the current accuracy of a mature talk-to-your-data deployment, and measured how? Izmit reports a 50% first run against a 150-question set written from the sales process and a target shape of 95% on a restricted set, then no accuracy figure at all for the system that now answers 40,000 questions a week — and no definition of what "accuracy" means over free-form business questions. ([Izmit](../sources/20260826_DrTdD-ttjCY.md), 04:08-05:05)
- Does heavy assistant usage change any business outcome? A million questions, ~40,000 a week, and >70% weekly retention are reported; book coverage, win rates, deal-cycle length, and incremental revenue — the reasons the system exists — carry no figures and no before/after comparison anywhere in the source. ([Izmit](../sources/20260826_DrTdD-ttjCY.md), 02:33-03:09)
- Which BI questions can be answered by routing to certified assets, and which require generated SQL, joined data, or human analyst review? (Northwestern Mutual starts from certified assets; WorkOS generates SQL directly with context blocks and execution validation — when does each approach win?)
- Which metadata fields most improve LLM performance on enterprise analytics tasks?
- Which sales and marketing interaction fields should be extracted before graph analytics or attribution agents can be trusted?
